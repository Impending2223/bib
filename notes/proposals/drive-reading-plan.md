# The Drive and what the series has read of it: a reading plan

Written Nov. 26, 2026, recut Nov. 27, 2026, and mended Nov. 27, 2026 after its independent review (`notes/proposals/drive-reading-plan-review.md`). The recut follows the owner's words of Nov. 26 (ruling 63, `notes/owner-rulings.md`): the batches are by subject, a government document goes with its subject, apportionment leaves civil rights, and a batch is read by readers one after another. The mend follows the review: it corrects the ratings from the repository, splits the rows that held more than one work, counts each copy once, puts the works read in part into the batches of their subjects, and fixes the order, the sequence and the numbers. It starts no reading. It answers the owner's words of Nov. 24 and Nov. 26: a full inventory of the works on the Drive and a suggested breakdown of the unread items into reading batches. Nothing in `lists/`, `sources/` or on the Drive was changed. The full list is `notes/proposals/drive-reading-plan.tsv`, one row a work.

R0 governs this plan and I disregarded nothing in it. The plan sets no scope. Every batch below is a list of sources to read for every dated event they report.

## A side effect to know before any reading

To count pages I asked the Drive for the end of each PDF, in about 1,800 PDFs, with many small requests each, and kept no copy. That ran the Drive's download limit out: a test of 12 files picked at random, minutes after I stopped, gave Google's "Quota exceeded" page and not the PDF. I stopped the counting at once and made no further requests. I cannot lift the limit and do not know when it lifts; Google's usual period is about a day.

Until then a reader who fetches a file by the curl line in `notes/guide/reading-a-copy.md` gets an HTML page, and `file` will say "HTML document". The guide reads that as "the file is not shared by link". Here it means the limit, and the reader should wait and not report the file as unshared. Whoever starts the first batch should try one file before briefing readers.

## How much of it the series has read

A work is rated from the repository: its card, the calendar entries that cite it before any "To check:", the claim-map rows, its book-check file, `./bib notes-coverage`, and the briefs and the hand-off that name it. "Read through" means the book-check or the calendar shows every page for the calendar's span was read. "Read in part" means some pages, chapters or years. "Cited only from other works" means the calendar names it behind "To check:" or its card says it was cited by another book, and no one has opened it. "Not read" means no trace of any of these. Pages are the PDF page counts taken from the files; where a file would not be counted, or is an EPUB, the count is estimated from the file's size (the median size per page of the counted files of that size class, with no file put above 1,500 pages) and the TSV says so in the column `pages_basis`.

## The unread, in batches

A batch is the unread works of one subject, read together, with the remainders of that subject's works already read in part. "Readers" is the number of reader-runs: pages to read divided by 70 for a dense book, by 150 for a work read by date (a chronology, a set of documents or hearings, a report, a diary); each work rounds up. A periodical issue or an oral history does not take a reader of its own: issues and transcripts are packed in date order, up to about 150 pages to a reader. That brings the oral histories from 845 readers to 232 and the 36 CQ issues from 36 to 14.

"Pages in the files" counts each file once. "Pages to read" is what a reader still has to read. For a work read in part it is the file's pages less the distinct pages the calendar cites, which overstates the unread part. For the 51 old remainders (old batch R) the book-check names the unread part, and the whole book is still counted. "Queue" is the number of the batch's rows that a queue item (9 to 22) or the older queue already reaches. "Proposed Nov. 25 only" counts rows that only the runs proposed in the inventory of Nov. 25 name, which wait on this plan. "Neither" counts rows no queue item and no earlier proposal reaches; it includes works read in part and the oral histories.

Every batch is read by readers one after another (ruling 63): reader 1 works from the tree as it stands, the audit of reader 1's patch is merged, and reader 2 starts from that tree. Two batches may run at the same time only where their entries do not overlap (another subject, other years); the suggested order below says which. The reader numbers in the next section are positions in that sequence. The same numbers are in the TSV, with the column `order` the position of the work in its batch.

## Each batch: what it is, and its order of reading

The order in every batch follows one rule. The record and the chronologies come before the books that rest on them (stage 1 and stage 2). Histories and studies come next, earliest publication first, so a later book can be read against an earlier one. Lives, memoirs and diaries come last, because they are the most partial. Series that run by year (the Posture statements, the Current Documents, the Senate Foreign Relations volumes, the CQ issues, the Newsweek issues) go in date order. A reader who finds that a book cited the day before is better read earlier should say so in the audit and the order be changed; the order is a default.
