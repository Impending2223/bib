## 1. The short answer

- The display the owner described on Nov. 24 at 4:00 p.m. is what `footnotes.py` already does (STYLE 1; `build()`): consecutive units with the same sources are one run, one mark at the run's end, one footnote compiled from the map's rows. The design note's own example, `cal.1960-06-23.soviet-union`, has five units and, today, three footnotes: ¹ Wu Lengxi; ² Wu Lengxi, Yang Jisheng; ³ Wu Lengxi and the rows not yet placed. Neither proposal file says this. The comparison's table has today's system "cut anyway"; it is cut in the arithmetic, never in the data or on the page.
- So what is wrong today is narrow, and it is three things. (a) A part is named by a letter: a position in a cut that is worked out again from punctuation each time, and nothing carries the letter through an edit. (b) A source that stands under every mark of an entry is printed under every mark, or (STYLE 8) the footnotes are dropped. (c) 19,941 rows are `?`: read and pinned, their part never placed.
- The current proposal (stand-off notes that quote the words they bear on; rulings 35–35d) cures (a) with a much larger rebuild than (a) needs, leaves (b) and (c) where they are, and says nothing on visibility, variants, additions to an entry, patches, checks or migration. It is over-built in what it stores and unspecified in nearly everything else.
- A much smaller change to today's system meets the owner's requirements: keep `c`, `n`, `src` and the footnote code; store a cut in `c` only where a pass needs one (the owner's own "not-human-readable 'separator'", Nov. 24, 3:25 p.m., which neither design took up as written); let rows, rulings and variants name the parts between cuts. Section 8.

## 6. Problems not yet raised

1. **What happens to `n`.** Today `n` is the citation record and `src` says which of its items bears on which units; `check` holds the two together. The comparison's example drops both for `notes:`. If `n` stays, Design 3 is today's `src` with quotes for letters. If `n` goes, 58,000 notes with their parentheticals, "To check:", "Made public:", "Names:" and "Check:" need a new home and a converter. Not said.
2. **"The whole entry" drifts.** A whole-entry note covers whatever `c` holds now. A sentence added later by another source falls under every earlier note. The owner's scheme does not allow it: after an addition the old sources stay on claim_0 and the new matter is claim_1. Design 2 handled it (`all` expanded when the patch is checked); Design 3 lost it. It is already happening: 1,023 stale maps are `all` rows made against a text that has since changed.
3. **A pass that speaks to part, in a patch.** Not specified. To settle: the quote is over the stored markup (`*Title*`, `[text](url)`, straight quotes, CJK the line drops), not the page a reader sees; a quote may end inside a link, italics, a quotation or parentheses, where the splitter never cut; the mark must fall after the closing punctuation, which the quote may not include (the owner's Nov. 21 "catches on the full stop in 'no. 10'"); a phrase found twice needs a prefix; two readers quoting nearly the same part ("Moscow will send Zorin" and "will send Zorin.") make two cuts and a sliver passage with its own footnote. Letters cannot do that last; free quotes can.
4. **`c` edited, the quote no longer found.** The file says "`check` lists a note whose words no longer appear, for a reader to re-attach." What the page shows meanwhile is not said: the whole entry (overclaims), nothing (unsourced), or the block (today's stale rule). The notes pass (Q6) rewrites `c` at scale, so this is the common case. The web-annotation tools meet it with fuzzy matching, which is not "mechanically". Here the patch that edits `c` is in hand at the merge, with the old text: the anchors should be carried through the edit by the tool, and a reader asked only when the supported words themselves changed. A stored copy of the words is what makes the mismatch possible.
5. **Adding to an entry.** Section 3, Q6. Whichever design: an addition needs an op that does not rewrite `c` whole, and it must leave the earlier sources off the added words.
6. **R22 has no unit, and may not need one.** Whether an edit is an addition or a change is a property of the edit: are the old words all still there, in order? `patch-check` can test that against the base, part by part, with no atoms: only insertions, an addition, free; anything else needs the settling record in the note or a declared rewording. That is the three sentences of R22 as a check.
7. **Variants.** 1,325 rows name a unit by letter. They are data only (no page prints them; "Check: … gives …" in `n` is what a reader sees). Under 35d: do they cut passages; are they printed in the footnote (the Bluebook's "*But see*" is the house's kind of answer to the owner's Nov. 24 "alternative, irreconcilable accounts"); if printed, the compile rule is "the same notes, supporting and disputing", not "supporting". Not said.
8. **Visibility.** (i) Today every unit is a claim with its own computed tags, its address taken from the names in its own words (`vis.py` STYLE 5). If passages replace units, a five-sentence entry with one source becomes one claim, and the computed tags change wherever an entry's sentences name different people or places; if units stay for visibility, the splitter stays and two cuts share one line. Not chosen. (ii) The 257 `@` rulings are tags, flat strings; a quote cannot live in a tag. (iii) A cut made only for a ruling or a variant must not make a footnote mark. (iv) `vispage.js` hides a claim's span and the mark in it; it never touches the footnote list, so a hidden claim's footnote still shows, numbered, with no mark (today too). (v) With "the mark's place names the part", hiding the passage before a mark makes that mark read as covering more than it does. (vi) The EPUB leaves the panel out (`calbook.py`, STYLE 2) and so shows every claim in full; the site greys by default. "Identical online, in print, and in the ebook" holds for the footnotes, not for this.
9. ***Id*. and repetition.** By the owner's rule (Bluebook 4.1; STYLE 4) *id*. reaches back across footnotes only when the footnote before has one authority. The comparison's example works because note 1 is FRUS alone. In the calendar a shared source usually sits among several: section 4's 71,748 printed for 35,985 distinct. The treatments in the code: "block" (hides the matching); "first" ("Sources cited *supra* note 1"), which as coded keeps note 1's own extra cites in note 1, so the later notes' "*supra* note 1" claims them too; none (full repeats). None is chosen under 35d. First cites (STYLE 5) are unaffected.
10. **Marks with overlapping parts.** One mark per change of the set, so n overlapping notes can make 2n − 1 passages, and a source on a middle clause makes two cuts and two footnotes alike on either side of it (1,880 entries have such pairs now). A source on parts that are not consecutive (1,594 entries) becomes several notes of one cite, since 35d drops discontiguous targets. Two rules with no stored row need a unit to name: the day's label "in the first footnote", and a link in `c` standing for "the unit holding it".
11. **The `?` tail.** 19,941 rows whose part was never placed are printed in the entry's last footnote. Under "the mark's place names the part" that is a misstatement: in `cal.1960-06-23.soviet-union` Westad, *Brothers in Arms* and the Peng Zhen nianpu sit under note 3 ("The same text had gone ... cables the points") though they bear on the handing-over, note 2's. No design has a word on them. They are the largest body of indeterminate cites in the calendar.
12. **Placing the marks.** Marks and spans are placed by rendering each unit alone and searching for it in the rendered line (`footnotes.place`, `vispage.c_html`); where a piece is not found, the entry silently falls back to a block. An arbitrary quoted stretch that cuts through a link or a title will miss more often. Whatever the store, the build should carry the cut through the renderer as a private mark, not search for it afterwards; and count the misses.
13. **The converter.** Item 35's test, "every footnote and mark where it is today", is the right one, and it means the page does not change for any existing entry: the whole gain is in later edits. Then the conversion should touch only what names a part: about 10,000 entries (the 8,396, and those with a ruling or a variant by letter). 48,000 need nothing.
14. **Scale.** Besides the copied text (Q16): every quote re-anchored at every `check`; orphans accumulating as stale maps do now; readers that are language models normalizing a quote (a diacritic, a dash, a doubled apostrophe) and failing `patch-check`, which is caught, or matching the wrong occurrence, which is not.
15. **Nothing to point at.** A passage with no id cannot be named by `./bib stemma --unit`, a book-check row, an auditor's report, a variant, or R22's "a unit already in an entry".
16. **The claims of today's tools and briefs that would change** (docstrings are the house's specification): `claims.py` STYLE 1 ("A reader wanting a finer unit writes a semicolon ... The letters are the owner's ABCD"), 2, 4, 5, 7; `footnotes.py` STYLE 1, 2, 3 (the `?` tail), 6, 8; `vis.py` STYLE 1 ("A claim is a unit of `c`"), 5, 8 (`@` letters; the frame "its letter the unit's"); `vispage.py` STYLE 2–3 (`data-u`); `witnesses.py` (the variant's unit, 5a; the stemma); `addops.py` (`units`, `unit`); the reader's brief R22, R33, R33a, R34, R35, R37, R41, R42; CLAUDE.md, "What each source stands for" and the patch ops; `notes/guide/brief.md`, `changing.md`; `notes/proposals/visibility-spec.md`; the handoff's 4aw.1.

## 7. An established model; over-built or not

- **W3C Web Annotation** is an interchange format for notes made by third parties on documents they do not control: JSON-LD, bodies, targets, motivations, selectors. The proposal takes one idea from it, the quote selector, and none of its tooling. The quote selector's bargain (keep a copy of the words, because the document may change behind your back, and accept orphans) is the wrong one here, where the text and its notes sit in one record and change through one tool. The name answers "is there a preexisting model" without bringing what a preexisting model is wanted for.
- **The footnote call.** The owner's words are "footnoted passages". The established model of a footnoted passage is a call in the text at the point where the passage ends (TeX, Markdown and Pandoc's `[^1]`, every word processor). `./bib claims show` already prints its footnotes that way. A separator in `c`, unseen on the page, is a footnote call without its number: the owner's own suggestion of 3:25 p.m.
- **The critical edition.** The owner's analogy of Oct. 30 ("manuscript/editio trees ... textual criticism") names the closest fit: a base text with anchors, an apparatus of sources and an apparatus of variants (lemma, reading, witness) attached to the anchors; in TEI, `<anchor>` and the "double end-point attached" apparatus. R41's variant rows (`unit`, `witness`, `reads`, `at`) already have that shape; the witness registry is the stemma. Only the anchor is missing.
- **Statements with references** (Wikidata's statements, references and ranks) are the established model for atomic facts (Q8). They would make the calendar a database and its line a rendering of rows: not annals, not the brief. Not recommended; named so that the question has its answer.
- **Over-built?** Yes. Of the three faults in section 1, Design 3 addresses (a) alone, by replacing the map's rows, the rulings, the variants and the Checks with a new kind of object, when (a) is a fault of addressing. And it is under-built where the work is: it specifies none of section 6.

## 8. The smaller change

Stated as a sketch; it wants one page with a real entry before any code, not another round of designs.

1. `c` stays one string. `n` stays the citation record. `src` keeps its rows. `footnotes.py` keeps its compile (runs of parts with the same sources, a mark at the run's end) and its ten STYLE rules.
2. A **cut** is stored in `c`, at the end of a part, only where a source, a ruling or a variant bears on less than the whole entry. It is never shown. No cut: one part, the whole: the owner's claim_0. Its written form is a detail (braces occur in no entry's `c` today; `{2}` would do). `store.py` takes the cuts out as it loads an entry and puts them back as it writes, so the places that read `c` as prose (110 in 30 modules, by the design note's count) see what they see now.
3. The stretches between cuts are the **parts**, each with a permanent id on its cut. A part that is cut again gives way to two new ids, the old one kept as an alias of both (the owner's claim_0 into claim_00 and claim_01; the lineage ids themselves would serve).
4. Rows, `@` rulings and `variants` keep today's forms with part ids where the letters stand. `all` is written only while an entry has no cut; the first cut rewrites it to the ids, so an addition is never covered by an earlier source.
5. **Patches.** A part is named in a patch by its words, once: `cite: {id, item, on: 'Moscow will send Zorin.'}`. `patch-check` finds the words in `c` at the patch's head, requires the part to end where a clause or sentence ends (where the house's marks sit), and prints the parts it will use or the cut it will make; the merge writes the cut. The quote is never stored, so it cannot go stale. Letters are accepted for the transition. An `edit` of `c` must keep every cut of its base; a part whose words changed is reported with its rows: R22, part by part, by diff (section 6, 6). Words only added become a part of their own with the patch's cite.
