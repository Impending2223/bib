# The JFK and RFK oral histories, sorted into the reading plan's batches

This note sorts the 840 transcripts of batch O1 by subject. The table is in `notes/proposals/oral-histories-sort.tsv`. It has one row for each transcript, with a chief batch, any other batches the scope names, and a few words on why. An audit checked the sort and changed it; the last section says what changed and why. The counts below are the counts after the audit.

The sort uses the finding aids' one-sentence scopes and who the interviewee was. No transcript was opened. Where a scope is general, the interviewee's job decided the batch. Where a scope is about the Kennedys as people and names no subject of any batch, the transcript stays in O1.

Four of the 840 files are not to be read as interviews of their own. Three are saved web pages of the libraries' collection pages: the John F. Kennedy collection page (52 pages) and the Robert F. Kennedy collection page, saved once in each library's folder (13 pages each). They are marked `page`. The fourth is a second copy of Kenneth O'Donnell's second RFK interview (83 pages). It is marked `copy`. None of the four is in a batch. That leaves 836 interviews and 29,619 pages.

The 46 rows with no code in the input were matched by the interviewee's name and the interview's number. 43 were matched to a code and a scope. Three were the web pages. The audit corrected three of those matches and one other row; see the last section.

U8 is large because most of the JFK interviews are about the 1960 campaign and most of the RFK interviews are about the 1964 Senate race and the 1968 campaign. By the sorting brief, both go to U8.

## How U8 should take its oral histories

The 268 interviews should be a stage of their own within U8, read after U8's books. They should not go to another batch, because their subject is U8's subject. The stage should be read in three parts, one for each campaign. Each part should be packed in the order of the events it treats, not the order of the interviews.

- 1960 and before: about 144 interviews and 4,755 pages, nearly all from the JFK library. This part is the 1956 convention, the 1960 primaries, the convention and the campaign.
- 1964 to 1967: about 19 interviews and 783 pages. This part is RFK's Senate race in New York and the New York party fights that followed.
- 1968: about 105 interviews and 4,321 pages, nearly all from the RFK library. This part should be read after U8's books on the 1968 Democratic contest and after U2.

The figures for each part are close, not exact. They come from the years each scope names, and some scopes name two campaigns. The three parts treat different years, so their entries do not overlap, and they may run at the same time.

## Stays in O1

22 interviews, 763 pages, stay in O1. Their scopes are about the Kennedys as people and name no subject of a batch. Examples are the interviews on JFK's health, the White House renovation, the Carlyle Hotel, the Mount Kennedy climb, the White House tape system, and Art Buchwald's friendship with RFK. No O1 row lists another batch.

## More than one subject

635 of the 836 interviews have a scope that names the subject of a second batch or more. Their `also` column lists those batches. O1 and F1 are never listed as an `also`. The batches named most often as a second subject are U9 (145), U1 (143), U8 (84), U4 (59), U3 (54), H1 (48), J1 (37), P1 (37) and W4 (36). U9 is high because many 1960 and 1964 campaign scopes name a State or a city.

Some `also` lists are short. A scope that the sorter read in full gave all its subjects. A few long scopes name many places, and the sorter listed the main ones.

## Rows least sure of

- Kenneth O'Donnell, file `RFKOH-KPO-02-TR (1) (1).pdf` (83 pages). It is marked `copy`, because the other file of that code has the same 83 pages. A look at its first page would confirm it.
- Jacqueline Kennedy (24 pages). The file is named `JFK3`, so it is coded JBK-03. The finding aid has seven interviews with her, and only one file is in O1. A look at the first page would confirm which interview it is.
- Seven rows have no scope in the finding aid, and the interviewee's job decided them: Kohler (A1, Ambassador to the Soviet Union), Donahue 4 (U1), Gilpatric 4 (D1), Vanocur JFK 2 and 3 and Donald Wilson JFK 2 (P1), and Donald Wilson RFK 2 (U8, as his RFK 1 and 3 are about the 1968 campaign's media). Rafer Johnson RFK 1 and 2 (U8) also have no scope. They are the weakest, because he is known only as a friend of RFK and a worker in the 1968 campaign.
- Some rows are close calls between two batches. The Justice Department interviews on organized crime, wiretaps and Hoover are in U5. Those on its staff, its divisions and appointments are in U1. The assassination of JFK is in U5 where the scope names its record (the autopsy, the Warren Commission); a memory of the day stays with the rest of the scope.

## The audit

The audit checked every row's chief batch and `also` against its scope sentence and the plan's batches. It changed the chief batch of 89 rows, the `also` of 160 rows, and the code of 7 rows. Each changed row says so in its `basis`, after "audit:". The changes are these.

- F1 is the State Department's six volumes of documents, read by date. It holds no subject of its own. The 27 interviews sent there now go to the batch of the region the scope names, or to U1 (the administration) where the scope names none: 18 to U1, 3 to W4, 2 to A1, 2 to V3, 1 to W3, 1 to W5. F1 was also taken out of every `also` and replaced by U1.
- P1 is the press and the periodicals, read by date. Interviews stay in P1 where the scope is about the press itself: coverage, press relations, the press office. Ten P1 rows whose scopes are chiefly about something else were moved: six to U8 (the 1960 campaign and the debates), one to U1 (Joseph Alsop 2, on staff and appointments) and three to W4 (Joseph Alsop 1, John Scali's channel to the Soviet embassy in the missile crisis, and Donald Wilson's JFK 1 once its code was corrected). Wilson's JFK 2, which has no scope, went into P1 by his job at USIA (see the codes below).
- O1, by the sorting brief, is only for scopes that name no subject of a batch. 21 of the 43 rows left in O1 named one, and were moved: nine to U8, three to U1, two to W4, and one each to D1, H1, P1, U4, U5, U6 and V3. The one to P1 is Donald Wilson's JFK 2, whose code was corrected. The 22 that stay lost any `also`, because their scopes name no batch's subject.
- Vietnam: V2 is the war on the ground and in Saigon, and V3 is Washington's decisions. Eight interviews about Washington's policy on Vietnam and Laos (Rusk 1, Gilpatric 1, Harriman 2 and 3, Ball 3, Cooper 1, Forrestal 1, Robert Johnson) were moved from V2 to V3. Hilsman and William Sullivan 1 moved to V3 from F1, Rusk 2 from V1, and Lord Harlech's RFK interview from O1. In nine `also` lists V2 became V3 for the same reason.
- China: the plan puts China's foreign relations and Taiwan in A6, not A3. The four A3 interviews (Chiang Kai-shek, Chiang Ching-kuo, Thomson 2 and 3) went to A6, and A3 became A6 in every `also`.
- Labor: U8 holds the unions, and H1 holds labor policy and the Labor Department. George Meany, Albert Zack (AFL-CIO) and Jack Conway 1 and 2 (UAW) moved from H1 to U8.
- J1 is the Supreme Court and the Constitution. Four interviews about Justice Department divisions (Tax, Civil, Lands, Antitrust) moved from J1 to U1. J1 stays in their `also` where the scope names judges or court cases.
- Single rows: Lowenstein 1 and 2 moved from U8 to W5 (RFK's Day of Affirmation address in Cape Town). Ben-Gurion moved from W6 to A2 (his scope is Russia and China). Halaby 1 moved from D1 to U1 (the FAA). Smathers 4 moved from U8 to U1 (LBJ as Vice President). Belford Lawson moved from U4 to U8, like Marjorie Lawson. Donahue 3 moved from U1 to U8: its scope in the input was cut short, and the finding aid's full scope is about the 1952 and 1960 campaigns.
- Small `also` fixes: P1 was taken out of nine rows about speechwriting, which is not the press. W5 was taken out of two rows that name no African subject. A few batches the scope names were added.

The audit checked the 43 rows matched by hand, and the four the sorter was unsure of, against the finding aids' titles and against the file names in the input. The finding aids give no page counts, so the pages could only be compared between files.

- Blair, Donald Wilson (JFK) and Russell: each has two files, and in each pair the two codes were the wrong way round. The file named #1 had been given the code of interview 2, and the file named #2 the code of interview 1. The file names settle it, and the codes were swapped back. Blair's 23-page file is WMB-01 (Stevenson, 1956 and 1960, U8) and his 12-page file is WMB-02 (the Danish reaction to JFK's death, W3). Wilson's 24-page file is DMW-01 (his work on JFK's staff, the Bay of Pigs and the missile crisis, W4) and his 43-page file is DMW-02 (no scope, P1). Russell's 30-page file is FHR-01 (Tunisia) and his 33-page file is FHR-02 (Ghana); both stay in W5.
- O'Donnell: two files carry the code RFKOH-KPO-02 in their names, and both have 83 pages. The finding aid has two O'Donnell RFK interviews, and the other file is interview 1 (128 pages). So the second KPO-02 file is a copy. It is marked `copy` and taken out of the counts. The finding aid cannot prove this; only a look at the two files can.
- Jacqueline Kennedy: the input coded her one file JBK-01, but the file is named `JFK3`. It is now JBK-03 (the 1954 McCarthy hearings, the 1960 campaign, election night and JFK's health), and it moved from O1 to U8. The finding aid's titles alone do not settle this.
- The other 40 hand matches agree with the finding aids' names, numbers and dates, and were left as they were.
