# FRUS: what the calendar cites and what it does not

Nov. 26, 2026, about 10:15 p.m. PT. For the owner, on the question of 10:07 p.m.: "Have we ever done an inventory of uncited FRUS documents? It might make sense to conduct a volume-by-volume survey to see what we're missing, particularly in threads we've already read related literatures on (e.g., VN, the PRC, Cuba)."

## What we have done before

No such inventory has been made. But every FRUS document of the span is already in the series: the daybook lists each one under its day (from the volumes' own files, the microfiche supplements included), and the series holds the text of each (`sources/frus-text/`). So the inventory needs no reading. The table below is counted from those two sources and the calendar's links tonight.

## What it shows

Of 26,968 FRUS documents from January 1960 to January 1969, the calendar's entries cite 3,518, about one in eight. The volumes the calendar leans on most are the Vietnam volumes of 1964 to 1968 (a third to a half cited) and the Cuba volume of 1961 to 1962 (42 percent). The volumes on China, Laos, Cuba after 1962, and the microfiche supplements are hardly touched:

- **Vietnam:** 3,887 documents in eleven volumes (1961–63, I–IV; 1964–68, I–VII), 1,248 cited, 2,570 not.
- **China:** 1958–60, XIX, 3 of 49 cited; 1961–63, XXII (Northeast Asia: China, Korea and Japan), 64 of 383; 1964–68, XXX, 19 of 347.
- **Cuba:** 1958–60, VI, 56 of 213; 1961–63, X, 185 of 443; XI (the missile crisis), 75 of 390; the microfiche supplement to X–XII, 0 of 746; 1964–68, XXXII, 44 of 441.
- **Laos:** 1958–60, XVI, 24 of 226; 1961–63, XXIV, 53 of 491; 1964–68, XXVIII, 25 of 401.

An uncited document is not always a missing entry. Many record an event the calendar already has from a book; then the book is cited and the record is not, against R28 (the primary record first). Others record events the calendar lacks.

## A survey, volume by volume

1. **Sorting, by script (no reading).** For each uncited document, the script looks for calendar entries of the same day or the day before or after that name the same people (FRUS's lists of persons are already joined to the name entries) or fall in the volume's thread. It sorts the documents into three lists: those that match an entry (the entry lacks its record); those that match nothing (a likely missing entry); and the routine ones (transmittals, which R18 passes over). The result is a note for you with the counts for every volume, before any reading.
2. **Reading, by volume, Sonnet then Opus, in sequence.** For each document that matches an entry, the reader reads it and cites it first in the entry, correcting the entry against it where they differ (R28, R33b). For each that matches nothing, the reader reads it and enters the events it records (R5, R7), in the day's order (R25c). FRUS documents are short, about two pages each, so a reader takes about 75 documents. Vietnam's 2,570 would take about 35 readers; China's, Cuba's and Laos's together about 40 more; the whole series about 310.

## Where in the queue (the default taken, ruling 71)

- The sorting script now, beside the place pages: it reads nothing and costs little.
- The reading for Vietnam, China, Cuba and Laos, whose literature the calendar has read most, right after item 13a, the Soviet record. The other sides' records (China, the DRV, the Soviet Union) come first, then the American record against them.
- Every other FRUS volume with its subject's batch in the reading plan (the Middle East volumes with W6, Berlin with W3, and so on), each read before that batch's books.
