## The two namesakes

**David Lawrence.** The namesake is `kjc.who.lawrence-david-l`, "Lawrence, David L.", Governor of Pennsylvania to 1963, in K–J Cong. III.F. His tag, `name:lawrence-david-l`, is on the entries of the 1960 and 1964 conventions, Pennsylvania's politics and the Credentials Committee. The patch leaves them as they are. The columnist's entry would be `kjc.who.lawrence-david`, tag `name:lawrence-david`. It is put on the two entries that quote his column, which carry no Lawrence tag now.

How the two are kept apart:

- Part III may drop a middle initial (CLAUDE.md, Names: "Part III may drop a middle initial but not a suffix"). The bare "Lawrence, David" would therefore join "Lawrence, David L." as one person. A row in `sources/people-apart.yaml` stops that:

  ```yaml
  - names: ['Lawrence, David', 'Lawrence, David L.']
    reason: David Lawrence, columnist, founder and editor of U.S. News & World Report; David L. Lawrence, Mayor of
      Pittsburgh, Governor of Pennsylvania, 1959-63. Part III may drop a middle initial, so the bare name would join
      the fuller.
  ```

  The row goes in with the patch's merge, not before. `check` fails on a name here that no source writes, and no source writes "Lawrence, David" until the entry exists. The patch format cannot carry it, because the file is not a list. The reason's facts are recalled: check them before it is written.
- In a Names line, "Lawrence" fits both men. The entry's tag settles which one ([names-ambiguous] is quiet where the tags settle it).
- The merge will flag `kjc.who.lawrence-david` as a possible duplicate of `kjc.who.lawrence-david-l` (patch-check warns of it). Settle it with `./bib resolve kjc.who.lawrence-david --take keep`.

**Howard K. Smith.** The namesake is `kjc.who.smith-howard-w`, "Smith, Howard W.", Representative from Virginia and Rules chairman to 1967 (K–J Cong. III.D; also in II.I.2 and the Congress rosters). The commentator already has his own entry, `kjc.who.smith-howard-k`, in III.K, with two tagged entries. Their initials differ. The name rules join two names only where their initials agree, so the rules already keep them apart, and no `people-apart` row is needed. The calendar's bare "Howard Smith" (Jan. 18 and 26, 1964, and the others) is the chairman everywhere it was checked, and is tagged so. The patch only moves the commentator's entry to the press section (III.J) and adds ABC to his role.
