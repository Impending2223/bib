# The claims design: an independent review

For the owner, Nov. 24–25, 2026, at the owner's word ("Could you send this to an auditor? I want a fresh set of eyes on this. I'm not sure I've caught nearly everything, and we're far from where we started with."). The subject: how a calendar entry's `c` is tied to its sources, so that footnotes, variants, visibility, R22 and patches work part by part. A paragraph a line.

Read: the owner's messages of Nov. 22–24 (notes/owner-brief.md) and the earlier ones they rest on (Oct. 23, Oct. 28, Nov. 13, Nov. 18, Nov. 21); tools/bib/claims.py, footnotes.py, vis.py, vispage.py, addops.py; the reader's brief, R22, R32–R35, R41; notes/proposals/claims-model.md, claims-model-options.md; notes/owner-rulings.md 31–35d. ## 1. The finding that changes the picture

Today's build already does what 35d describes. footnotes.py, STYLE 1: "A run of units with the same sources closes with a mark ... The note's sources follow the line as footnotes, numbered", each footnote compiled from the map's rows in the house form, with *id*. and first cites (STYLE 4, 5), and the same HTML feeds the Names, the Threads and the EPUB (STYLE 10; calbook.py STYLE 1). Identical adjacent footnotes cannot arise today: runs with the same sources are merged before marks are placed (build(), the run key).

What 35d changes is therefore not the display but two things under it: where a passage may end (today only at a unit's end, i.e. a full stop or semicolon; under 35d at the end of any quoted words) and how a part is addressed (today by letters that shift; under 35d by quoting the words). The options file presents 35d as a new display; it should say that the display is today's, so the change is to storage, and so the owner's Nov. 24 4:00 p.m. concern ("I'm troubled by you rerolling the entire citation design scheme") is met by changing nothing on the page that the task does not touch.

The numbers, today: 58,454 calendar entries; 40,874 with a reader's map, 17,188 with the mechanical map only; 1,048 reader maps stale (the hash no longer matches `c`; those entries are shown as one block). Of the 39,826 current maps, 30,892 compile to one passage and 8,934 to two or more. On the page, 7,510 entries carry footnotes (20,649 footnotes, about 340 characters each); 50,944 are one block. 856 footnoted entries print the same footnote twice, never adjacently (the pattern [X][X, Z][X]). In about 1,165 entries three or more sources stand under every footnote, and STYLE 8's REPEAT = "block" turns them back into one block, so their part-by-part attribution is not on the page at all.

## 5. Problems not yet raised

- 5.1. **Additions and whole-entry notes.** R32 makes a source cited "stand for the whole of `c`". A whole-entry annotation has no words, so when a reader adds a sentence (R33a, the owner's chief wish, O6) every existing whole-entry source is made to vouch for it. Today the hash makes the whole map stale. The withdrawn design note had seen this ("`all` is expanded at patch-check ... so it never covers a claim added later"); the stand-off design lost it. The fix is the owner's own claim_0/claim_1. Every note records the text it was attached to: a whole-entry note stores the hash of `c` it was made against, as `src.c` does now. When an addition merges, every whole-entry note made against the old text is narrowed to the old words. The new words then form claim_1 under the new source alone. An insertion in the middle makes the old notes' target two stretches. Either discontiguous targets come back (35d dropped them) or the merge writes the old note twice, once per stretch. Today's data already has 3,708 discontiguous rows ("A, C-E"), so the converter meets the same question.
- 5.2. **R22 by diff, not by eye.** With every note storing its base text, the merge can classify an `edit` mechanically. Words inserted where no note's words were are an addition (the looser rule). Words changed or deleted under a note are a correction (strict R22: the settling record said in the note, else a Check or a variant). Changes to unsourced connective words are a rewording. That is O7 made checkable. The proposal leaves it at "check lists a note whose words no longer appear", which catches part notes only.
- 5.3. **A note's quote that no longer matches.** Edits are of two kinds. Through a patch, the merge has base and new text and can carry every anchor mechanically: offsets shifted by the diff, the quote rewritten where only its neighbours changed. A direct hand edit (CLAUDE.md allows it) must be caught by `check`: an orphan error. Hypothesis-style fuzzy re-anchoring is not wanted here, since an anchor moved silently onto changed words is an R22 change made unseen. The rule: carry exact anchors through diffs; anything else is an error for a reader.
- 5.4. **The patch form for a part, and its checks.** An additive op names its part by words: `cite: {id, item, on: 'Moscow will send Zorin'}`, and likewise `variant`, `src` and a visibility ruling. patch-check should require: the words occur in `c` at the patch's head exactly once, or with a few words of prefix to make them unique (25 entries have a repeated unit today); they start and end at word boundaries; they do not straddle a quotation, parenthesis, link or emphasis boundary unbalanced; the item is one `n` prints (today's problems() rule). The merge applies the op to `c` as it is then, so the same test runs again at merge. A quote that no longer anchors becomes a conflict on the entry (a new kind, `{kind: anchor, op, from}`), never dropped and never guessed. Whether the part "stands as a statement" is judgment, for the audit to sample, not for check. Today's additive ops carry letters with no base (addops.py `_units_ok` tests only that the letters are within range). So a `cite` written against one `c` and merged after another patch changed it lands on the wrong sentence, unseen. That is a live defect today, and the strongest single reason to move from letters to words.
- 5.5. **Visibility under passages.** vis.py computes a claim's address from the names its own text holds (STYLE 5), and its public dates from the claim's own witnesses (STYLE 1). If the visibility unit becomes the footnote passage, adding a source changes visibility. A Beschloss note on "Moscow will send Zorin" splits that clause from "Rusk to Dobrynin", and the clause, no longer naming Rusk, loses the US by address. Visibility must be computed on a segmentation that does not move with the sources: the sentence or clause, which is today's unit, kept for visibility alone, or the atomic segments cut by every annotation, with the address read from the whole sentence. Two segmentations are needed in either case: support boundaries for marks, and the visibility rulings' own boundaries (`@` today, 254 entries, 20 `vis:frame`). A visibility ruling must not cut a passage, or it produces exactly the identical adjacent footnotes O15 forbids. And STYLE 6 ("a hidden claim takes its mark with it") then has a gap: where the last segment of a passage is hidden and the earlier ones are not, the visible words lose their footnote mark. The gap exists today within a run, but more segments make it common. The mark should go to the last visible segment of its passage (a page-script change, listed as a departure).
- 5.6. **Variants.** Variants are not printed today (only `./bib show` and the stemma); the reader sees the "Check: ... gives ..." sentence. A variant annotation must not cut passages (it is not support). It needs a check that a witness does not both support and dispute the same words: cal.1960-06-23.soviet-union cites the Peng Zhen *nianpu* as a `?` row and as a variant on unit A. If the owner ever wants variants on the page, the law reviews' own device is a signal in the passage's footnote ("*But see* ..."), compiled from the variant row. No new layout is needed for it, but it is not asked for now.
- 5.7. **The repetition the owner objects to.** The compiled footnote repeats the shared core under every mark. *Id*. shortens it only where the previous footnote has a single authority (STYLE 4, Bluebook 4.1), and most cores have several. Today's way out, STYLE 8's "block", hides the parts entirely (about 1,165 entries), and that contradicts O15. Its alternative, "first" ("Sources cited *supra* note 1"), is ambiguous wherever note 1 also holds a source the others lack: 3,875 multi-passage maps split only at the first unit, a date-place head such as "Montgomery." or "Bucharest, morning.", and in 2,660 of them that head carries an extra source. Three things can be decided without new layout. Drop "block" for entries with genuine parts. Print a footnote identical to an earlier one of the entry as "*See supra* note 1." (the law reviews' cross-reference; the 856 entries with repeated footnotes). Drop from the footnotes a parenthetical that only names the part, now that the mark names it (5.10). The rest of the repetition is what compiling each footnote from its supporting citations means; the owner should see a few heavy entries (the 519th NSC, Oct. 2, 1963; the Presidium, Jan. 8, 1960) compiled both ways before it is called cured.
- 5.8. **Placing marks.** Today place() renders each unit alone and searches for it in the rendered line. It fails where a unit renders differently cut, and the entry falls back to one block. Arbitrary quoted parts make that much worse: words ending inside `*Title*`, inside a link's text, before a closing quotation mark, or across characters cjk.py drops from the line (footnotes STYLE 5). Two rules are needed. A mark snaps forward over closing punctuation, quotation marks and brackets (the law reviews put the call after the punctuation, "…;¹"). A part may not end inside a link or emphasis. Placement should not search rendered text. Insert a private-use sentinel character into `c` at each boundary, render once, then replace the sentinels with the marks and the spans. That is the owner's "not-human-readable separator" (O11) used where it belongs, at build time, never stored. It removes the fallback.
- 5.9. **The `?` rows, unrowed items and labels.** 19,941 rows (in 8,066 entries) are `?`: the source's parenthetical narrows it and no reader has said to what. Today they close the last footnote, so the page shows them as bearing out the last passage, which is often false. The 35 migration ("rows on all units become whole-entry annotations") says nothing of them. Made whole-entry, they would claim the whole, contrary to O1's "Z (C)". They should stay unplaced. They can be printed apart from the numbered footnotes (an unnumbered last sentence, as "To check:" is), or kept in the last footnote with their parentheticals and listed for a mapping pass. Either is a listed departure, and the mapping pass is the real cure. The NYT patches waiting in inbox/ add more of them (`units: '?'` with long content parentheticals). The label rule (STYLE 7; O5, "only in the first footnote") and the 17,188 mechanical maps need the same explicit treatment in the converter.
- 5.10. **Parentheticals of two kinds.** O5 keeps parentheticals that say what the cited matter says ("the morning of the 23rd", 285; a headline; a figure). O14 and 35d drop those that only name the part. The data does not mark which is which. The converter cannot tell them apart, and readers will keep writing both. A rule is needed, with a check: a parenthetical on a source that has a part target names content only, not which part. The existing ones are left as they are until a reader passes. Otherwise the 7,510 footnoted entries change wholesale.
- 5.11. **The converter.** Its inputs, with today's counts: 1,048 stale maps. Their letters were made against an older `c`, and git history likely holds the text whose hash `src.c` records, so a mechanical rebase (old units matched to new by text) can save most of them before conversion; none should be converted by its letters as they stand. 3,708 discontiguous rows (5.1). 4,417 rows that list every letter of their entry: equal to `all` today, but they should become targets on the words, not whole-entry notes, because they were written against those words. 19,941 `?` rows (5.9). 254 `@` and 20 `vis:frame` tags (5.5). 1,326 variant rows on letters. Letters in the eight pending patches. About 31,000 lettered rows to turn into words, averaging 157 characters each if the whole units are quoted (about 5 MB on lists/cal's 56 MB). A start-and-end anchor ("Moscow will … Zorin", the W3C range form) is a fifth of that. The provable first step is: the same passages, the same sources per passage, every entry. Display changes come after, by list.
- 5.12. **Today's maps already over-split, a little.** 1,269 entries split only because a work's page row and its note row (`via w.… n.17`) cover different units. The footnote prints them as one citation ("88 & n.17 (citing …)"), but the passage still splits. Counting a work's page and its notes at one page as one source for compiling passages takes multi-passage maps from 8,934 to about 7,700 and passages from 56,156 to 53,658; leaving the registry's own `via x.` rows aside takes them to about 7,340. The `via` rows are the stemma's (R37: "the tree is drawn for each unit"). They should keep their precision there and not cut the page.
- 5.13. **Scale.** No cost problem. footnotes.build over the whole calendar took about 35 s here; string anchoring is cheaper than today's render-and-search. The cost is people's: every reader brief, every patch op and about a dozen modules (claims.py, footnotes.py, vis.py, vispage.py and its `data-u`, witnesses.py and the stemma's `--unit`, addops.py, patchcheck.py, merge.py's conflicts, check.py, cli.py's `claims show/seed`, lives.py and threads.py through apply(), the goldens). Whatever the model, the tools' claims to change are these: units as the address (claims STYLE 1), letters in rows, `@` tags and variants (claims STYLE 2, vis STYLE 8, R41), the hash as staleness (claims STYLE 4), the last-footnote tail (footnotes STYLE 3), the "block" fallback (STYLE 8), visibility per unit (vis STYLE 1, 5), spans by unit letter (vispage STYLE 2, 3).
- 5.14. **Names.** "Notes" in the options file means annotations. The house already uses "the note" for `n` and "footnotes" for the display. A field called `notes:` beside `n:` will be misread by every agent. Call the annotations `on:` rows of `src` (below), or `anchors`.
- 5.15. **Where the citation text lives.** Design 3's `cites: frus-12` does not say whether `n` stays the visible, hand-written record whose items the annotations name, as `src` rows do now (claims STYLE 3), or whether the citations move into the annotations and `n`'s source sentences are compiled from them. The first is the smaller change and keeps every reader's habit. The second is what "compiled mechanically from the supporting citations" ultimately implies. It should be decided and written down. Leaving it open is how two sources of truth arise.
- 5.16. **Small ones.** Footnote anchors (`fn-<id>-<n>`) renumber when a part is added, so a link to a footnote is not permanent; that is acceptable, but it should be said. A work's first full cite on a page (works.py FIRST CITE) can fall in an entry the visibility panel hides, and the next shown entry then has only the short form. That happens today; more passages do not change it.

## 6. An established model, and the size of the change

- The proposal borrows one idea from the W3C Web Annotation model: a target named by quoting its words, with prefix and suffix where needed. The idea is sound and is the right one for additive ops that carry no base (5.4). The rest of that model (JSON-LD bodies, motivations as a vocabulary, interoperability) buys this project nothing, and calling it "the W3C standard" overstates what is adopted. It is closer to how editors such as ProseMirror or a Quill delta keep marks over ranges and render them as runs. That model fits the compile step exactly: boundaries from every range, runs between them, a mark where the support set changes.
- The other established model the owner's words point to is the citation processor (Pandoc's citeproc with CSL): citations written at the point they support, compiled to footnotes with *ibid*. and first-full-then-short. footnotes.py already is a small citeproc for the house's Bluebook. Writing the citations inline in `c` would make readers edit `c` for every cite (a base, a conflict) and would put markup in the prose. It is not recommended.
- Is 35/35d over-built for the need? As written in 35 (every source, variant, visibility ruling and Check an annotation with a motivation; `src` replaced), yes.
