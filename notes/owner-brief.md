## Citation: what each source stands for; hard and soft

2026-10-23, during the pause (queued; taken up Oct. 24, 00:02 UTC), session "1968 series".

> During reading, sourcing, and citing runs, be precise about what the cited source states.
>
> Unless each cited source is authority for the whole of the entry it appears beneath, including its dates, numbers, names, quotations, and statements of fact, the entry and citation block together must identify, either by inline citation in the entry, citation parenthetical in the citation block (as presently done, on occasion), or otherwise, the source or authority for each claim in the entry, and what claims in the entry each source or authority stands for.
>
> (Implication by omission permitted if disciplined and determinate. If an entry reads "ABCD" and the citation block reads "X; Y; Z (C)", I assume that X stands for ABD but not C, Y stands for ABD but not C, and that Z stands for C, and for C in collocation with ABD, but not necessarily for ABD in full. )
>
> ((So, e.g., Z's C might be "Charles bought the red hat at Dorothy's request," while ABD might be collocated facts about "Charles buying a red hat" that Z does not speak to (date, price, store, make), so long as we have some minimum level of confidence in the identity of the "Charles," "buying," and "the red hat" between ABD and C and between XY and Z.))
>
> (((I'm sure that's all clear as mud, but I lack the language, the perspicacity, and the grace to specify the rule accurately. Please do ask clarifying questions if it reads as though I'm asking for something foolish, strange, redundant, etc., and not just a perfected or perfectible norm of citation, attribution, sourcing and authority.)))
>

In our source blocks, separate the hard sources, including the primary authorities and our pincited books, from the "soft" sources (i.e., sources as-yet unread). >
> (For our purposes, of course, "from which no material is upstream" really means "from which no *identifiable and recoverable* material is upstream."  If the upstream material is not both identifiable and recoverable, the trace/map ends with the downstream matter we have in hand, which, *for our purposes*, is material "from which no material is upstream.")
>
> For the ultimate I'm really groping for something like... the kinds of manuscript/editio trees you see in textual criticism, or source criticism? But with a recognition that here, in history, unlike philology, the ultimate "ultimate," the root "root," is the event, not the document?
>
> That is, we'll often have multiple items "from which no material is upstream."
>
> First, because a fact or event is something that may leave multiple immediate traces, each of which is matter "from which no material is upstream" except the unrecoverable fact or event itself.
>
> Second, because the immediate traces may be unidentified or unidentifiable, unecovered or unrecoverable, the actual body of identifiable and recoverable matter may include multiple items "from which no material is upstream" for any (express, implied, inferred, or notional) immediate trace.
