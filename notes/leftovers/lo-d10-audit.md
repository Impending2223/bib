## The points the brief named

- **Wu Lengxi, May 2–5, 1963.** The records rule out Wu's days, but none of them gives the days of his Hangzhou meetings. Deng was at Pyongyang May 2–4 and in Peking until the afternoon of May 10. That day he left for Hangzhou by train with Kang Sheng's party "because the weather was bad" (Yang, II, 277; Deng, III, 1757). Mao was at Hangzhou on May 2, at Shanghai May 3–5 and back at Hangzhou from May 6. Zhou Enlai and Deng attended part of Mao's meetings of May 7–11, and Mao talked with Zhou the evening of May 10 and on May 11 (the Mao nianpu). Peng Zhen was at Hangzhou May 2–12 (IV, 261). The four entries stay as Wu's account, with their Check. The Checks on May 2 and May 5 now give the records' nearest days. Wu's weather matches the train journey of May 10, but nothing settles that his trip was that one.
- **Wu Lengxi, June 25, 1963.** This one is settled. `cal.1963-06-25.china-leadership-2` is redated to June 23, with `kind: correct`. Three records give June 23. Deng's nianpu (III, 1760) has Deng and Peng Zhen lead the members of the drafting group to Chengdu that day. Peng Zhen's nianpu (IV, 279) says the same. Yang's diary (II, 289) has Peng say on June 22 that he goes the next day, and Yao Zhen telephone on June 23 that he is there. Wu's June 25 is now a variant on the date. The three old variants that read June 23 are taken off, because they now agree with the line.
- **What else the June 25 redate changes.** The link from the Emei drive (`-7`, June 25) to the flight is removed, because a link across two days is none. The drive gets a Check saying that Wu puts it on the day of the flight. `cal.1963-06-23.china-leadership-3` (Yao Zhen's call that night) now `follows` the flight; it repeats the arrival in part.
- **The return of June 26 and 27.** The reader's ops stand. Yang's diary of June 27 has the party "already back" but gives no day.
- **`cal.1963-05-28.china-leadership-2`, redated to May 29.** The redate stands: Yang (II, 282–283), Deng (III, 1759) and the Mao nianpu all have Kim's flight and Mao's meeting at Wuchang on May 29. It duplicates in part `cal.1963-05-29.china-leadership-4` (Kim's flight, seen off at 8:30 a.m.) and `-8` (Deng and the drafters fly to Wuhan). Its lodgings and Mao's villa are Wu's alone. It is kept, with the reader's Check naming the overlap. A later pass may cut the flights from its line.
- **Jan. 17, the morning meeting.** Yang's diary (II, 244) has "several comrades of the Secretariat" from 10:30 a.m. to 2 p.m. discussing the congress and drafting a new passage of the speech. Deng's nianpu (III, 1742–1743) has the Politburo in the morning under Liu, deciding to revise the delegation's address. After that meeting, Deng, Peng Zhen, Kang Sheng, Yang and Liu Ningyi revised it (`-5`). The 10:30 meeting may therefore be either the Politburo meeting (`-3`) or the revision after it (`-5`). The Check on `-3` now names both possibilities. The split to the 4:30 p.m. meeting at Liu's (`cal.1963-01-17.china-polemic`) and Yang's evening at the theatre (`-9`) stand.
- **Goldwater, Apr. 26 or 27.** It was one speech. White (137–138) puts Goldwater's address to the Republican Women's Conference on Friday, Apr. 26, and quotes Loftus's report in the *Times* "the following day." Novak (171 n.9) has the war whoops when Goldwater appeared at "that April 27 meeting." No record I read settles the day, and the *Times* of Apr. 27 was not read. The two Checks now say "one speech," and Novak's reading is a variant on the speech's clause in `cal.1963-04-25.goldwater`.
- **The splits: Birmingham, May 11–13.** The bombs, the 9 p.m. statement (the Papers' note; Branch has 8:48 p.m., kept as a variant) and the telegram are each one act. Their works are where they belong. The statement now `follows` the Sunday's recorded meeting (Branch, 800).
- **The splits: June 19.** Mann, 371, gives Celler's introduction of "Kennedy's entire program" on June 20, but not the bill's number. That introduction is another day's act and is already in the tree as `cal.1963-06-20.civil-rights-bill-2`. So "Celler's bill, H.R. 7152, June 20" is cut from the message entry, and Mann, 371, is cited on the June 20 entry. In the Record, S. 1731 and S. 1732 are at 11,076–11,081, early in the sitting; S. 1750 is at 11,161–11,162, after the message was read (11,156–11,161); all three are listed together at 11,083–11,084. S. 1750's range now runs from the Republican conference's close at 4:02 p.m., where Dirksen says the third bill is still to come. The new bills' tags now name only Mansfield, Magnuson and Dirksen as their lines do.
- **The splits: the Young Republicans.** White (169–170) has Goldwater speak on Thursday night, June 27, with the *Times* of June 28 reporting it. Perlstein (219–221) and Kabaservice (64–65) have the session of twenty-two hours, Lukens declared the winner near dawn, and the adjournment at 5:15 a.m. White has the vote late on Friday instead, and that Check moves from the old note to the June 29 entry. The via row for Perlstein, 219 n.1, follows the speech to the new entry.
- **Schlesinger, 537, read.** It has the demand after half the prisoners were loaded, and Cushing and Clay raising the money. This bears out the Dec. 23 and Dec. 24 entries as they stand.

## Ops changed

- **Pointers removed from the old notes of the splits.** Every old note of a split pointed forward to its new entry ("Split under R25b: ... is [[new]]"). `notes/guide/changing.md`, "Splitting an entry", forbids this. Removed from Dec. 24 (`cuba-2`), Jan. 17 `-3`, Apr. 12 `-3`, Apr. 19 `-3`, Apr. 25 (Birmingham and `-5`), Apr. 26 `-2`, May 3 `-2`, May 6, May 7 (both), May 11, May 20, May 31 `-3`, June 19 and June 25. The new entries of Dec. 23 and 24 and of May 21 no longer point forward either. May 3 `-2` needed a new sentence to name its record, so it now says that the second call is an act of its own, quoting Branch, 762.
- **Jan. 8.** The reader's edit is rewritten. Zhou's call to Yang Shangkun from Hangzhou is already its own entry, `cal.1963-01-08.china-leadership-8`, dated Jan. 8 from Yang's diary. Mao's three points therefore go into that entry by `add-words`, cited to Deng's nianpu, III, 1741–1742 (the record of the call that Deng read on the 9th), with their names and polity tags. The Mao nianpu's "the 9th" becomes a variant on it. The old entry keeps only Mao's evening talk with Zhou, and its note says the call is an entry of its own.
- **Apr. 25, Birmingham.** Branch gives the mass meeting no part of the day. The new entry's evening range and "The night's" are taken out, and its `follows` now rests on Shuttlesworth's report of the fountains and toilets.
- **Apr. 25, Rangoon.** Neither nianpu dates the farewell banquet to a part of the day. "Evening" comes out of the line, and the range becomes the dinner's kind range (`range_basis: kind`). The Apr. 19 banquet keeps its evening: Chen Yi, 963, has 晚上.
- **Apr. 19.** The double cite of Chen Yi, 962, is now one.
- **May 1.** Chen Yi (964) has the call at the palace at 5 p.m., so under R25b it becomes its own entry, `cal.1963-05-01.china-liu` (claimed in the ledger). Both nianpu have the Queen, 王后, where the old line had "the King." Chen Yi puts the Sangkum exhibition at Siem Reap on May 2, and that is now a variant on the new entry. The old entry's three variants, which had become stale, are removed.
- **May 6, Huế.** The page (305–306, quoting Thích Trí Quang) has Ngô Đình Cẩn's request as "not to fly the Buddhist flag" on the day. The line had "keep it quiet," so it is corrected (`kind: correct`, the page named), and Trí Quang's article goes behind "To check:".
- **Names lines.** Only persons who have a name entry are named: Smyer, Vann, Pitts, Nolan, Williams and Nadasdy are dropped from Names lines.
- **May 21.** The referendum is the growers' act, so the President's tag and his name come off the new entry.
- **Pages of the Papers' notes.** The note on the Feed Grain signing is on 415, not 414. The note with the 4 o'clock of May 22 is on 424.
- **Order links added (R25c).** On May 31, Kim's arrival at Pyongyang `follows` his leaving Wuhan. On May 3, Marshall's second call `follows` the hoses. On May 12, the statement `follows` the recorded meeting.
