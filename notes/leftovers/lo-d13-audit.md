## The points the brief named

- **May 6 and O'Brien.** The tree has O'Brien's words where the record puts them, in the call of May 8 at 6:11 p.m. (`cal.1964-05-08.civil-rights-bill-2`, Presidential Recordings, VI, 527–28). So they are now nowhere in May 6: the reader's gloss quoting them, the note's "O'Brien's words, in the gloss", and the "To check" for the recording are all taken out. The gloss keeps only Purdum's judgment that the vote was "the lowest moment" of the cause since the bill reached the floor (278). Stewart's diary stays in the line, with the manuscript under "To check".
- **The days moved.** The Cleveland press conference is June 9 (White, 150–51; Perlstein, 358), and now takes the morning range of the record ("Come daylight") instead of the kind range. Reedy's night is Aug. 26 (Branch, 473), with a Check that the summons came "about midnight Tuesday". Humphrey's nomination is 12:24 a.m. Aug. 27, but the page is Young, 139, not 138: the folio at the foot of the page reads 139, as the tree had it. Both the old entry and the new one now cite 139. May 20 to May 19 holds (Purdum, 292; Risen, 219–20; Whalen, 185–86).
- **The 1966 entries.** Two of the reader's three were already in the tree: the Assembly members' letter of Apr. 15 (`cal.1966-04-15.elections-1966`) and Pearson's column (`cal.1966-05.elections-1966`). Both adds are struck and their ledger claims dropped. The tree's Pearson entry had Smith's later recollection in its line ("Christopher, on the defensive, loses his ground"); an edit moves it to a Commentary gloss (Smith, interview of Feb. 12, 2007; Kabaservice, 172 n.54). The May polls entry stays; its forward pointer now points back to the Apr. 15 letter.
- **Perlstein, Schlesinger, Kotz, the Diary, Cosmas.** All read; each bears the ops that rest on it. Perlstein, 359 changes the June 11 day (below).
- **Aug. 4, 1964.** The note's parentheticals now speak only of the attack. The words of the later acts are already in their own entries (the NSC, the strikes, Herrick, the leaders, the address), where the split had put them. What was missing there is added: the diary's "Lyndon had ordered air action" on the NSC entry; Moyar on Saltonstall on the leaders' meeting; a Check from Bernstein on the 4:47 p.m. meeting; the lunch's sources to "To check" on the lunch; Kaplan's note on McNamara's call to Sharp. The wrong sentence "The time is Johnson's address" and the address's Checks are taken out. Moyar's verdict that no attack occurred moves to a gloss.
- **The splits.** No old note points forward now. The "Split under R17 ..." sentences are taken out of seven old notes, and five forward links are taken out or turned back (to June 12 from June 11, June 10 from June 9, July 30 from July 29, and the two 1966 pointers to June 1 and June 7).

## Changed

- **June 9, Byrd.** The tree's `civil-rights-bill-4` already had Byrd's 7:38 p.m. start, his words and Humphrey's question in its line. So the reader's new `-6` duplicated it. An edit now takes Byrd out of `-4` (which keeps the day's votes), and `-6` holds the whole speech: the start, the words, Humphrey's question, the end at 9:51 a.m. The end is Whalen, 197, not 196.
- **June 11, Scranton.** The reader's decision entry (`-4`) repeated Eisenhower's call, which `-2` already holds. It now holds only the decision, about noon, and Mrs. Scranton at lunch. Its link to `-2` is struck, because White says the order of the call and the decision is "uncertain". A Check gives White's "by that Thursday morning" and Perlstein's evening. The evening entry (`-5`) is now the buffet at the mansion: Scott's plea, Scranton's "I'm going to run" (Perlstein, 359), "We must do it because it is right", said by Scranton and not his advisers (Kabaservice, 107), and the staff set to work (White, 156).
