## What I found that changes the readers' work

- Row 111 (Mansfield, 113 Cong. Rec. 11194): both readers had the line wrong. Mansfield reads Westmoreland's paragraph ("When a field commander does not have to look over his shoulder to see whether he is being supported ...") and then says in his own words: "no field commander has ever had to look over his shoulder to find out if he is 'supported,'" because the military carry out policy. The line is Mansfield's. It is already cited on 11194; no Check.
- Row 105 (Guam): FRUS V, doc. 115, n. 2, from the attached list, names Rusk, McNamara, Wheeler, Westmoreland, Lodge, Bunker, Harriman, Sharp and Komer among the Americans. Both readers missed it. Already cited.
- Row 171 (the FBI's monograph on King): the second reader wrote that the monograph is of a different date. It is not: Book III, 174 has "The revised monograph, dated March 12, 1968, was disseminated to the White House, the Attorney General, and the heads of various government intelligence agencies." The quotation differs: the memorandum reads "should serve again to remind top-level officials in Government of the wholly disreputable character of King," not "to again remind." I cited the page (choice 1) and added a Check on the wording and the day.
- Rows 149–151 (Wheeler's BNK 1876 of Dec. 25, 1965, at Drea, 450 n.90c): the registry's link is wrong. Drea's note 90 reads "ed note, FRUS 1964-68, 19:1062-63," and the right record is FRUS XIX, doc. 541, the editorial note on the vote ("unanimously adopted the British draft resolution as Resolution 242"). The BNK 1876 witness in `sources/witnesses/1965.yaml` is a catch-all: its copies take in editorial notes of half a dozen volumes, "XIX, docs. 541–42" among them. I cited doc. 541 on the part it bears (row 149); rows 150–151 stay at choice 3, since doc. 541 does not print those words (the resolution, already cited, does). The edge `450 n.90c` in `sources/witnesses/by-work/drea-mcnamara-clifford-and-the-2011.yaml` should point to a witness for doc. 541; I did not edit `sources/`.
- Row 108 (the luncheon at Bonn): the Diary shows Johnson leaving the Bundestag's memorial service at 11:04 a.m. for the luncheon at the Villa Hammerschmidt (image 2) and leaving the Villa at 1:11 p.m. for the Requiem Mass at Cologne Cathedral, 2 p.m. (image 4). The second reader's Check stands; I corrected it to name image 4, where the Mass is. The Diary was already cited in the entry, images 2 and 3, so both of the second reader's `primary` ops were dropped.
- Row 143: FRUS V, doc. 393 gives the trade of the bombing for an end to attacks across the DMZ to Acheson, "and one or two others agreed," and has Ball as the one speaker who did not hold the bombing important. A Check, as the second reader proposed.
- Row 154: "There is no substitute for Phantoms" is General Hod's (FRUS XX, doc. 40, Jan. 8); Eshkol asks for "50 Phantoms as rapidly as possible" (doc. 39, Jan. 7). A Check.
- Rows 80–81 (H.R. 12047): the Record of Oct. 13 (26592–93) has one call of the House (Roll No. 359) and a later point of no quorum before the vote, not seven quorum calls, and no party count. Voteview's roll call counts the 64 nays as 54 Democrats and 10 Republicans, not 56 and 8. One Check for both.
- Row 48 (E.O. 11289): the order sets "not more than twenty-five members" and leaves the chairman to the President. The twenty members and Burke Marshall as chairman are in the White House's list of July 2, which the Public Papers print in the note to the statement (1966 Pub. Papers 698; twenty names counted). No record contradicts the line, but the order alone does not bear it. A Check.
- Row 128: the Record (20706) has Symington speak first after Fulbright, saying Russell "is in full agreement with the resolution"; Russell, then Ervin, after him. A Check on "Russell first."
- Row 78: Komer's memorandum reads "We're doing better than we think," not "much better." A Check.
- Row 61: Lodge's telegram (para. 25) has American forces "carrying the main burden of search and destroy operations," not "nearly all." A Check.
- Row 19: Valenti's notes have Bundy for examining "a continuous pause"; nothing of announcing only the thirty-hour cease-fire. A Check beside the existing one from Preston.
- Rows 1–2: the part of William Bundy's manuscript that FRUS prints (III, doc. 44) has Harriman to Europe under the cover of a sightseeing visit to Moscow, and nothing of a Geneva conference or of decisions put off to the month's end. A Check.
- Row 67: the subcommittee's report has the Real Estate Board withdraw its opposition "to the philosophy of open occupancy legislation at the state level," if the law applies to owners as well as brokers. A Check, giving the terms the line asks for.
- `cal.1968-04-05.king` (row 174's entry): the Diary (image 6) has Johnson leave the National Cathedral, after the memorial service, at 12:54 p.m. on Apr. 5, against Bernstein's Apr. 6 in the entry's Check. A Check.
- Row 130: the vote of 85–3 is on 23503, not 23500 as the entry's note says. A Check.
- Row 158: the dead sailor is in FRUS XXIX, pt. 1, doc. 226 (Jan. 25), not in docs. 217 or 225; I cited doc. 226 on that part.
- Row 122: the question of where McNamara might meet Soviet representatives is in FRUS XIV, doc. 229 (McNamara "willing to meet with Soviet representatives at any time in Geneva or elsewhere"; Kosygin asked to suggest how), which I cited on that part. Row 120's part is in doc. 230 (the debriefing: "ABM's and ICBM's"), and the first reader's op stays. The row's record, JSOP 69-76, is not held: rows 119–122 stay at choice 3.
- Row 98: the German cabinet's meeting of Mar. 15 is in FRUS XIII, doc. 240, not doc. 241. The first reader's op put doc. 240 on the "rotational plan," which it does not hold; I moved it to the first part, with a parenthetical.
