## The question the audit turned on: what the triangles show

Most of the reader's links rest on the order in which the three chronologies print the items of one day. The reader's note says they "mark a second and later item of a day with a triangle, in time order." The pages do not bear that out. The triangle (△) marks another item of the same day. It does not say the item came later. On these pages the printed order often departs from the clock:

- Jan. 15. The dashiji (386) prints Zhou's reception of Ouzegane first, with no hour, before the 5 p.m. talks. The Chen Yi nianpu (1009) puts the same reception in the evening.
- Jan. 15. The Chen Yi nianpu prints Nkrumah's presents, then talks it calls "evening", then the banquet. The dashiji puts those talks at 5 p.m.
- Jan. 20. The Chen Yi nianpu (1010) prints the embassy dinner before the farewell reception. The dashiji (388) says the dinner was given "after the reception" 招待会后.
- Jan. 28. The dashiji (390–391) prints Abboud's reception between the morning talks and the afternoon visit to Omdurman. The Chen Yi nianpu (1012) puts it in the evening.
- Jan. 10. The two works print the evening items in different orders: the dashiji (385) has the banquet at the Majestic before the visit to the Neo-Destour headquarters; the Chen Yi nianpu (1008) has the headquarters first.
- Jan. 3 and Jan. 29. The Chen Yi nianpu prints an item with no hour (a cable sent, a letter received) first in the day.

So an item's place in the printed order says nothing about when it happened. Where two items carry time words in different parts of the day, the words order them. Where they share a part of the day, or one has no time word, only the print orders them, and the print is shown not to follow the clock. I applied R25c's sentence on a collection's printed order this way: a link stands only where the entries' words, the source's own words ("之后", afterward; "招待会后", after the reception; "on the way to"), or the route show the order. This agrees with the audit of August 1966, which struck a link resting on a triangle in Zhou's chronicle for the same reason.
