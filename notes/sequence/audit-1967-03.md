## Links checked: 13. Kept: 12. Struck: 1. Turned: 0. Repointed: 0. Added: 1.

### Kept

- Mar. 1, Chavez boarding the plane (robert-kennedy) after the Court turns down Hoffa's appeal (crime-and-hijacking). Thomas, 337: Chavez had sworn to kill Kennedy if Hoffa went to prison; the Court denied the last appeal on Mar. 1, and "That same day" the armed men boarded. The boarding is the act on that oath, so it comes after the order. A basis sentence is added to the note. This rests on Thomas's telling; no other record was read.
- Mar. 2, Kennedy's speech at 3:40 p.m. after his note to Katharine Graham. The note's words show it: he is "about to" give the speech (Thomas, 334).
- Mar. 14, the three stops of Lady Bird Johnson's tour, Canada Township, then Cullowhee, then the class at Lee Edwards High School in Asheville. The diary gives her own route with hours: leaving Canada Township by 1:30, "Then we were off down the mountain" to Cullowhee (497); back in Asheville "a little past 5," an hour's rest, then the class (498). These are her own movements on one road, with hours in order and nothing against them. Basis sentences added to both notes.
- Mar. 15, Nashville. The address follows the Hermitage (its own words, "after the Hermitage"); the protesters follow the address (their entry's words, "after the President's address," and the diary, 501, as the car left the Capitol); Columbia follows the protesters ("We drove on to Columbia," 502); the evening reception at the Governor's Mansion follows Columbia ("We next drove" to Polk's home, then "Back at the Mansion," 502). The diary is the weakest kind of basis, but here it is the writer's own route through one day, with "a little before 12" for the address and the evening for the reception, and nothing against it. Basis sentences added. The reader's worry about the address's hour is unfounded: the entry's 18:05 UTC is 12:05 p.m. Central, and the diary has them filing into the chamber "a little before 12."
- Mar. 18, the Texas students after the opening of the Governors' conference. This is the link the reader called weakest. The Papers' note puts Johnson's remarks at 9:05 a.m. in the Fish Room; they are a welcome that ends by wishing the Governors a useful day, so they opened the conference. The diary has Johnson coming to the students "breaking away from the Governors' briefing," and the Governors "all day long" in briefings with the Cabinet (504, 505). A briefing he broke away from had begun, and it began after his welcome. Kept, with the basis in the note.
- Mar. 18, the wives' tree-planting after the students. The diary's "Meanwhile" is about the wives gathering downstairs while Lady Bird Johnson was with the students; she then went down and "We boarded the buses" for Gravelly Point, reaching the State Department "at one" (503–05). Her own movements are in order. Kept, with the basis in the note.
- Mar. 23, the decision on military control of Daqing (china-leadership-2) after Zhou's draft sent to Mao (china-leadership-3). The words show it, and the Zhou chronology (III, 137) has the draft sent to Mao and his answer "照办." The two entries both hold Mao's approval; see "What is left."
- Mar. 30, the *Hongqi* issue (china-leadership-3) after Mao's 9 p.m. note (china-leadership-5). Mao's note on the commentator's article is "issue it as it is"; the issue prints that article, so it was issued after the note. The date is a separate question: the Zhou chronology dates the issue Mar. 30 (III, 139), the Liu Shaoqi chronology Apr. 1 (the variant already on the entry). A Check is added on the day; if the issue moves to Apr. 1, the link goes.

### Struck

- Mar. 16, the House's passage of the investment credit (economy) after the war's supplemental (vietnam-supplemental). The roll-call numbers alone are no order (R25c, on serial numbers). The Record does give an order: the House's vote on the appropriation is at 6889 and its debate on H.R. 6950 begins at 6891. But the supplemental entry holds two acts, and its first is the President's signing of the authorization, whose hour I did not find (the signing statement, p. 356, gives none). A link would put the investment credit after the signing too, which nothing shows. And the passage already follows its own debate (economy-investment-credit), the entry it comes directly after. Struck. A Check on the supplemental entry asks for the split (R17); once split, the debate can follow the vote.

Basis sentence added.

## Pairs the reader left, read again

- Mar. 9, the special message on the investment credit and the news conference. The Papers' note puts the conference at 3:33 p.m. in the East Room (310), and the President opens it: "I am sending a message to the Congress this afternoon" (303). That does not say whether the message had gone up yet. The Papers' item order (the message first) is no order by itself (R25c). No link. The conference entry now carries its hour (20:33 UTC) and the Papers as its primary record, replacing its "To check: the Public Papers."
- Mar. 10, Mao's note on Zhou's report and Zhou's revision of the Jiangsu telegram. The Zhou chronology (III, 131–32) gives under Mar. 10 only the Jiangsu telegram and the Kunming note, with no order and no mention of Zhou's report, though Mao's entry cites that page for it. No link; a Check is added on Mao's entry.
- Mar. 21, Mao hearing Zhou in the evening and Zhou's evening meeting with the railway, communications and posts ministries: the chronology (III, 136) gives both as items of the day, the second marked "晚," with nothing between them. No link.
- Mar. 22 and 23, the Peking workers' congress. The Zhou chronology (III, 137) has Zhou talk with Kuai Dafu Mar. 22 "after attending" the congress; Ye Jianying's chronology puts Ye at the congress Mar. 23. The congress may have sat more than one day. No link across days; left.
- Mar. 24, Qinghai. The Zhou chronology (III, 138) gives the talk with the marshals as an item of the day, with no hour. Yang (I, 400) has Zhou announce the Center's decision only at the end of the meeting that ran from the evening to 2:30 a.m., and the decision, dated Mar. 24 (Zhongfa [67] 110), rests on the finding of that same meeting that "8.18" had no guns. So whether the decision (china-leadership-4) was taken before the meeting began is not shown. No link. The same decision is read out again at the meeting of Mar. 29, 9 p.m. (Yang, I, 400); the two entries are two meetings, not a double.
