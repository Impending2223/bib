# Audit of the sequence pass, October 1960 (R25c, R33b)

The patch is `inbox/2026-11-25-sequence-1960-10.yaml`. The reader's note is `notes/sequence/1960-10.md`. I read all 31 days of the month from the day sheet (`day_order.py 1960 --from 1960-10 --until 1960-10 --full`), each day's entries together. I held each of the reader's links against the two entries and against the volume the notes cite, *Freedom of Communications*, Part 1, read from the owner's copy. Under R0 I disregarded nothing: the batch brief set no limit. The patch now has 148 ops and 157 links, and `patch-check` shows 0 errors and 0 warnings.

## The question the audit turned on

About 140 of the reader's links rest on one thing: the order in which the volume prints a day's remarks. The volume does not say that it prints them in the order they were given. Its counsel's letter of transmittal (p. VI) says only that the texts were gathered "day by day" as the candidate's staff sent them. So I read the texts themselves for anything that shows the order: a stop he says is next, a place he says he has just left, a list of the day's stops, the part of the day ("this morning", "this noon", "tonight"), and the volume's own notes on stops it has no text for.

On most days the texts agree with the printed order wherever they speak. On three days they do not:

- Oct. 3. At the Marion airport Kennedy says it is "about the 14th speech I have made today" and that "we are going to Springfield" for television at 9 o'clock, and thanks the crowd for "saying goodby to us." At Springfield he calls it "the 15th speech we made today" (pp. 465, 475). So Springfield comes straight after the Marion airport. The volume prints Harrisburg and Venice between them. Venice is in Price's district by East St. Louis, and Price is on its platform, as he is at the morning's East St. Louis stops. Both stops came earlier in the day, at places the texts do not show.
- Oct. 12. The volume prints the four speeches first and the remarks after: the speech "prepared for a dinner" (it opens "I want to talk with you tonight") comes before the Constitutional Rights conference and the Harlem rally. The conference speech says "As Hubert said this morning." The campaign's release of Sept. 24 (p. 358) puts the conference's report to Kennedy at a luncheon and the Harlem rally at 4 p.m. So on this day the printed order is not the order of the day.
- Oct. 22. At Grand View Kennedy lists the day: "came to St. Louis, then to Joplin, then Wichita, now Truman Corners, back to Kansas and we end up in Green Bay." At the Kansas City, Kans., high school he lists "St. Louis, Joplin, Wichita, Kansas City, now Johnson County, and we are going to Green Bay, Wis., tonight," and names "President Truman, who we were with tonight," who was on the platform at the Kansas City, Mo., Auditorium (pp. 700–01, 703–04). So the Kansas stop was the last of the day, after both Grand View and the Auditorium. The volume prints it before both.

So the printed order alone is not proof. I kept a link that rests on it only where the texts agree with it at every point they speak to and nothing in them, or in the route, goes against it. I took this as a default, recorded in `notes/owner-rulings.md`, 55, so the owner can overrule it by rule.
