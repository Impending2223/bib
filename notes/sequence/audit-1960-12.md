# Audit of the sequence pass, December 1960 (R25c, R33b)

The patch is `inbox/2026-11-25-sequence-1960-12.yaml`. The reader's note is `notes/sequence/1960-12.md`. I read all 31 days of the month from the day sheet (`day_order.py 1960 --from 1960-12 --until 1960-12 --full`), each day's entries together, and held each of the reader's links against the two entries and their notes. Under R0 I disregarded nothing: the batch brief set no limit. The patch now has 20 ops and 20 links, and `patch-check` shows 0 errors.

## The reader's links

- Checked: 19. Kept: 19. Struck: none. Turned round: none. Pointed at another entry: none.
- Dec. 1, Lovett with Kennedy (transition-and-staff-cabinet) after Schlesinger's noon meeting (transition-and-staff-2). The reader called this the weakest kind of basis, a diary's order. It is stronger than that. Schlesinger's journal (94–95) says: "In due course Bob Lovett came in for lunch. I finished my drink and soon took my leave." *A Thousand Days* (131) says the same: "In due course Lovett arrived for lunch, and I took my leave. Actually, after this meeting with Lovett ..." Clifford (338) puts the meeting on Dec. 1, in Washington, with "a rather shifty end-run by Caroline Kennedy"; the journal has Caroline dashing in and out of the Georgetown house. So the lunch is Clifford's meeting, and it began as Schlesinger's visit ended. Kept, on these words.

- Dec. 27, Kennedy's agreement to live television (press-and-broadcasting) after Salinger's five hours with the network heads (-2). I read Salinger, *With Kennedy*, 56, on the page image. After the agreement "on December 27, 1960" and Kennedy's words, Salinger writes: "There was now no question that TV was willing ... I had met for five hours that day with the top news executives of the networks. All were enthusiastic." Then: "My next chore was to tell the newspapermen. JFK was puffing on a cigar when I stood up to leave." The meeting is told in the past perfect from the scene in Kennedy's room, and Kennedy had earlier said to hold off "until we know they'll buy it" (54). Kept, on one account's words.
