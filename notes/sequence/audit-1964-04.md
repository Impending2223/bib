## How I read Yang Shangkun's diary

Several links, the reader's and the tree's, rest on the order in which Yang writes the items of one day. The diary is not a collection of items: it is one man's account of his own day, written in parts (上午, 下午, 晚间) and closing with the hour he went to bed. On every page I read, each clock time and each part of the day stands in the diary's order (Apr. 3: 3:30 to 6, then 6:30, then the evening; Apr. 17: afternoon, evening, 9 to 10, then work to 2). So by the test of owner-rulings 55 (the items' own words agree with the order wherever they speak, and nothing goes against it) the diary's order is a basis. I kept the links that rest on it and added a few, and I flag them below as the weakest kind.
