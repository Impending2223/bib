# Audit of the sequence pass, August 1964 (R25c, R33b)

The patch audited is `inbox/2026-11-25-sequence-1964-08.yaml`, written by a Sonnet reader, with its note `notes/sequence/1964-08.md`. I read all 31 days of the month in the order the site now gives them (`day_order.py 1964 --from 1964-08 --until 1964-08 --full`), every entry of every day. For each link in the patch I read the two entries in full with `./bib show`. Where the lines did not settle the order, I read the sources. The reader opened no outside source; I did, and two of the questions the reader left for want of a record are now settled (the acceptances of Aug. 27), one is not (the Chinese replies of Aug. 5). The corrected patch is in place: 24 `follows` ops (25 links), one `check` op and 7 `edit` ops. `./bib patch-check` gives 0 errors and 7 warnings, one for each edit, because each edit sets only `n`; that is expected, since addops.py says the basis of a link goes into the note by an edit. Each edit adds one sentence beginning "After [[id|...]]" that says where the order comes from. I disregarded nothing under R0.

## Links checked: 25 (in 23 ops). Kept: 20. Struck: 4. Turned round: 1.

### Struck

- Aug. 3, Sharp's objection (`vietnam-tonkin-4`) after the proposal to end the patrol and Moorer's refusal (`vietnam-tonkin-3`). Marolda, 425, puts Sharp's objection "at the same time." The message times in the notes put Moorer's message (032259Z) 54 minutes before Sharp's (032353Z), but the entry also holds the Chiefs' agreement, and its note gives no time for that; Sharp's note cites a JCS message of 032351Z beside his own. Nothing shows that the whole entry came first.
- Aug. 4, the second attack (`vietnam`) after the leaders' breakfast (`vietnam-congress`). The entry's own time is 7:40 a.m., the *Maddox*'s first warning that an attack seemed near (FRUS I, doc. 272, in its note). The breakfast was at 9:12 a.m. (Bernstein, 336, in its note). The link would put the entry after a breakfast that its own time comes before. The report of the attack under way did come later (the 9:43 call still fears an attack "tonight"; the 11:06 call brings word of it), but that is a question for the entry's time, not for a link.
- Aug. 6, Johnson's call with DeLoach (`civil-rights-the-executive-2`) after the confessions and arrests in the Penn case (`civil-rights-the-executive`). The call's line says the arrests were made, but the Miller Center's summary of the call has DeLoach recommend that federal, not local, authorities make the arrest, and Branch, 437, has the arrests "Thursday night." So the call came after the confessions and before the arrests, and the arrest entry holds both. No link either way. A `check` op puts this in the call's note. The arrest entry should be split under R25b: the confessions in the day, the arrests that night after the call.
- Aug. 27, Humphrey's acceptance (`elections-1964-3`) after Salinger's seconding of his nomination (`elections-1964-atlantic-city-2`). Young, 138, has Humphrey nominated by acclamation at 12:24 a.m. on Aug. 27, and the film for Kennedy shown "after the nominations but before the candidates' speeches." So the seconding came before the film, and the acceptance comes directly after Robert Kennedy's speech, which the op keeps (Young, 140). The day of Salinger's seconding stays in doubt (its note: "Check: the day"); it may belong to the night of Aug. 26.

### Turned round

- Aug. 6, the campaign meeting in Jenkins's office (`elections-1964-johnson`) and the Oval Office meeting (`democratic-party`). Valenti, 203: after the roles were settled in Jenkins's office, "Walter suggested we see the president," who dismissed them within the hour with "get a sensible plan, and then come back to talk to me." The Oval Office meeting now follows the meeting in Jenkins's office. Its own note already gives this order.

### Kept, with the basis put in the note

- Aug. 5, the resolutions introduced (`vietnam-7`) after the meeting in Mansfield's office (`vietnam-congress`). Mann, 357: they agreed that Fulbright "would introduce the resolution in the Senate later that day." Woods, in the meeting's note, puts the meeting on the morning of Aug. 6; Mann, whom the entry follows, is the one source read that dates it.
- Aug. 6, the Senate debate (`vietnam-congress`) after the joint hearing (`vietnam`). The hearing sat from 9 a.m. (McNamara, in the hearing's note). The Senate met at noon (110 Cong. Rec. 18,339), and Fulbright reported the resolution from Foreign Relations (18,341) before the debate (18,399).
- Aug. 10, the signing (`vietnam`) after the 12:35 p.m. meeting (`vietnam-commitments`). The basis is Bundy's call of 9:50 a.m., which has "the signing at four" (`vietnam-5`). The Public Papers and APP give no hour.
- Aug. 25, the sit-in night (`mississippi-3`) after the Freedom Democrats' vote against the compromise (`mississippi-4`). Kotz, 219, has that vote "earlier Tuesday evening"; Branch, 470–72, has Rauh return the credentials after the delegates voted to reject.
