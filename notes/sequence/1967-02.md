# Sequence pass, February 1967 (R25c)

Read all 28 days of February 1967 as the site orders them (`day_order.py 1967 --from 1967-02 --until 1967-02 --full`), each day's entries together, asking of each: does it come after another entry of the same day? Patch: `inbox/2026-11-25-sequence-1967-02.yaml`, 10 links (below), `patch-check` 0 errors. I disregarded nothing under R0. Sources opened: Yang Jisheng, *Tiandi fanfu*, vol. 1, p. 397 (Feb. 19 Xining) and the 2020 *Zhou Enlai nianpu*, vol. 3, pp. 118–119 (Feb. 1), each a scan or text copy fetched into a scratch directory and deleted. Every other basis is the entries' own words or the notes already in the entries.

## The days

- Feb. 1: Freeman's call on Johnson "after the Cabinet meeting" follows the Cabinet (vietnam-support after vietnam). No other link. Zhou's talks with the army reception staff, the Railways rebels and the industry-and-transport rebels print in that order in the 2020 chronology (118–119), but a printed order is no order of the day. Mao's note to Zhou on tall hats and Zhou's own remarks on the same point: the chronology prints no note of Mao's under Feb. 1, so nothing says Zhou spoke after it. No link.
- Feb. 2, 3, 4, 5: no new link. The existing link on Feb. 4 (Kennedy at the airport after Rome) is right. On Feb. 5 the General Order of the temporary committee is already `part_of` the rally entry. The government's statement and Chen Yi's and Zhou's cable to the embassy staff have no stated order.
- Feb. 6: Mao's meeting on seizing power follows Zhou's letter proposing it (china-leadership after china-leadership-3). The letter proposes that Mao see the men "that afternoon", and the meeting is that afternoon's. The `see:` on the letter is not a link.
- Feb. 7: no link. Mao's note on Xinjiang and Zhou's talks with the Xinjiang leaders have no stated order (the chronologies put Zhou's talks on Feb. 7 and 8). Mao's approval of the telegram to the embassies and his signing of Chen Yi's cable to them may be one act (see below).
- Feb. 8: no link. The *Stoddard* was fired on "the same day" as the cease-fire began (MACV history, 494): the source says same day, not after the 7 a.m. start. Mao's reading of Zhou's letter that evening and Zhou's approval of the proposal to bring provincial leaders to Peking have no stated order.
- Feb. 9: no link. The liaison meeting that adopts the Peking security notice and Zhou's order to put the airports under military control have no stated order (the chronology's "the item after" is its print order).
- Feb. 10: Zhou's liaison meeting follows the meeting at Mao's ("after the meeting at Mao's"; china-leadership-2 after china-leadership). The existing link (Chen Boda near suicide after the meeting) is right.
- Feb. 11, 12, 13: no new link. The existing links on Feb. 13 (the bombing resumed after the White House meeting; Kennedy's statement after the resumption) are right.
- Feb. 14: no link. The banquet and the ballet for the Mauritanians carry the same evening range and the entries do not order them.
- Feb. 15: no link. Mao's reception of the Mauritanians (6:30 p.m.) and Chen Yi's farewell banquet for them have no stated order.
- Feb. 16: the report to Mao by Zhang, Yao and Wang Li follows the writing of the record (china-leadership-3 after china-leadership-6). The two entries share the drafting. Basis outside the two lines: Yang, vol. 1, 390 and MacFarquhar, 194–195, in the entries' notes: the record is written first, and the three go to Mao about 10 p.m. The existing link (the record after the meeting) is right. Jiang Qing's report to Lin that night is placed by its range; it and the report to Mao have no stated order, and Teiwes (78) puts the report to Lin on the 17th.
- Feb. 17: Lin Biao's receiving Tan Zhenlin's letter follows the letter (china-leadership-6 after china-leadership-2). Wheeler's cable to Sharp and Westmoreland comes before the meeting with Johnson (vietnam after vietnam-air-war): the cable says Wheeler and McNamara are "to see the President" on the broadening of the target system, and the later cable (JCS 1337-67, Feb. 18) reports that meeting; Sharp's answer carries the time group 170820Z, earlier than the meeting. The existing links (the meeting after Rostow's memorandum; Sharp's and Westmoreland's answers after Wheeler's cable) are right. Kintner's report to Johnson on Kennedy's speech and the *Times*'s story of that morning: no stated order.
- Feb. 18: no link. Mao's calling Ye Qun that night and Zhou's liaison meeting at Huairen Hall have no stated order.
- Feb. 19: no new link. The existing link (Kang Sheng's remark after the Politburo meeting) is right. At Xining, Yang (vol. 1, 397) tells the death of Zhang Guilan, "the conflict sharpened," and "that day" Zhao Yongfu's meeting proposing to declare "8.18" counter-revolutionary: a narrative's order with no hour, so no link on that alone.
- Feb. 20, 21: no link. Zhou's going to Mao's on Feb. 21 and Mao's approval of the telegram to Shanghai: nothing says they are one occasion.
- Feb. 22: no new link. The existing link (Clark's call after the coroner's finding) is right.
- Feb. 23: the telephone call from the General Staff to Lanzhou follows the shooting at Xining (china-leadership-5 after china-leadership-4): the call asks "what has happened at Xining" and is answered that the riot is put down. The Military Commission's "You fought well" follows the shooting too (cal.1967-02.china-leadership-8 after china-leadership-4; "immediately after the shooting"; Yang, 399, has the call about 8 p.m.). The Military Commission's instruction on spring plowing and Zhou's talk with the regional commanders on the same subject: no stated order. The Joint Chiefs' messages (Rolling Thunder 54, the wider interdiction, the artillery into Laos, Wheeler's cable) have no order among them.
- Feb. 24, 25: no link. The statements of Kang Sheng, Jiang Qing and Chen Boda on Feb. 25 are each from the "life meetings" entry's series; no order among them.
- Feb. 26: Kennedy's meeting at Schlesinger's follows the Gallup poll it names ("that morning's Gallup poll"; robert-kennedy after opinion). The weakest link of the month: the entry names the poll as that morning's, not the hour of the meeting. The dinner party where Kennedy speaks to Mrs. Graham ("the night") and the meeting have no stated order. The existing link (the Guiyang counter-seizure and the "Feb. 26 Corps") is right.
- Feb. 27: no link. Mankiewicz's memorandum speaks of Johnson's "new tactics, announced four days before the speech", which could be the news conference, but the count is no day. Harriman's call at 6 p.m. is placed by its hour.
- Feb. 28: Zhou's announcement of military control in Guangdong follows Mao's approval of it (china-leadership-2 after china-leadership). Mao's note is timed one o'clock on Feb. 28 (Mao's manuscripts, XVIII, 491–492, in the other entry's note), and the chronology puts Zhou's talks on Feb. 28. The weaker of the pair of Chinese links: neither entry gives an hour for Zhou's talk. The Chicago primaries and aldermanic elections, and the Senate debate and the House Democrats' statement on the supplemental, have no stated order.
