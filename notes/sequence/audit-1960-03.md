On these twelve pages the hours and parts of the day stand in the diary's order on every day: morning, afternoon, evening, then the night's work to a stated hour. Two places are not quite plain. On Mar. 24 the telephone call from Mao's office is written first, before "上午", though it came after 9 a.m. On Mar. 26 Deng's return, which the Deng nianpu puts at noon, is written after the afternoon's football. Both are reports of something done or heard, put at the head or in the middle of the day's entry, not the diarist's own acts out of order. I took the diary's order as a basis only for Yang's own acts within one part of the day, as the third default of owner-rulings 55 allows. Those links are the weakest kind, and each says so in its note ("the diary's order alone").

### Struck

- Mar. 18, Zhou's return and the Politburo meeting (`-2`) after the football match (`-3`). The two acts of `-2` do not fall on the same side of the match. The diary (I, 489) has the match in the afternoon and the Politburo from 7 to 11 p.m., so the meeting does come after the match. But Zhou was at Hangzhou that morning with Mao and Chen Yi on Nepal (`cal.1960-03-18.china-leadership`, the Mao nianpu), and he watched the match in Peking (Waijiao huodong, 271; the Peng Zhen nianpu, IV, 9). So his return came before the match. The day sorts an entry by its first act, which here is the return. A link would put the return after the match. The entry should be split under R25b: the Politburo of 7 to 11 p.m. as an entry of its own, with its hour and a link to the match. Listed below.
