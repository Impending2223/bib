# Audit of the sequence pass, April 1968 (R25c, R33b)

The patch audited is `inbox/2026-11-25-sequence-1968-04.yaml`, written by a Sonnet reader, with its note `notes/sequence/1968-04.md`. The reader wrote 31 `follows` ops holding 34 links, all on Apr. 1–5 and 8–10. The reader opened no source and read the entries from the month's file by a script of its own. I read all 30 days of the month on the sheet from `python3 -I tools/times/day_order.py 1968 --from 1968-04 --until 1968-04 --full`, every day with the same care, those with no link included. I read every entry in a link, and every entry of a pair the lines did not settle, in full with `./bib show`. Where the lines did not settle the order, I read the sources below. The corrected patch is in place: 35 `follows` ops holding 39 links, 9 `edit` ops and 2 `check` ops. Each edit adds one sentence to a note, beginning "Order:", that gives the source of a link the lines alone do not show. `./bib patch-check` gives 0 errors and 9 warnings, one for each edit, because an edit that sets only `n` is warned of; that is expected, as in the audits of March and May 1968. ## Struck

- Apr. 4, Fauntroy's call and Johnson's night calls (`civil-disorders-king-2`) after the start of the Washington riots (`civil-disorders`). The reader took Fauntroy's words as a report of violence already begun. Califano, 274, says Fauntroy called "shortly before 10 p.m." and "anticipated 'unprecedented violence'": a forecast, not a report. Nothing in the two lines or the page puts the call after the first rioting. The entry keeps the link already in the tree to King's death (`king`); Califano, 273–74, bears it out: the call came after the word of the death at 8:20 p.m. and Johnson's statement.

## Repointed

- Apr. 4, Indianapolis. The reader hung three entries on the rally. The sources give their order. Witcover, 142: from the rally Kennedy "went straight back to the Marott Hotel" and telephoned Coretta King. Schlesinger, 876: the militants Lewis had gathered "waited for him now," and "Kennedy finally arrived"; then "After the meeting," Kennedy said to Greenfield that King's death was not the worst thing, and spoke of "Harvey Lee Oswald" (876–77). So the call to Coretta King (`robert-kennedy-campaign-2`) stays after the rally; the meeting with the militants (`-3`) now follows the call; and the night at the hotel (`robert-kennedy-campaign`) now follows the meeting. Each repointed entry has its basis in its note.
