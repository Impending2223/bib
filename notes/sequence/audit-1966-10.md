## The reader's links: 8 checked, 8 kept, none struck or turned

- Oct. 9, china-leadership-6 (Ye at the second group) after china-leadership (the conference opens). **Kept.** The earlier reader took the day correctly: the Ye nianpu (II, 946) gives "Oct. 9–28" for the conference and then a separate item, "Oct. 9", for Ye's words at the second group. - Oct. 17, vietnam (Johnson leaves for Asia) after program (Johnson calls Califano to his bedroom). **Kept; the day is now settled.** Califano (150) says Johnson gave him the presents "just before he departed in a helicopter from the south lawn of the White House". - Oct. 20, the House's five conference reports: poverty after cities-and-housing, school-aid after poverty, economy-investment-credit after school-aid, program after economy-investment-credit. **All four kept, now on the Record's pages.** The reader chained them by roll-call number alone, which is no order (owner-rulings 55, second default). - Oct. 25, china-leadership (Mao's speech to the conference) after china-leadership-5 (Lin Biao's address). The Zhou nianpu (III, 81): "at the meeting, Lin Biao and Mao Zedong spoke one after the other" (林彪、毛泽东先后讲话). That is the chronicle stating the order in words, not only the order in which it prints items. Nothing in the Mao nianpu, which says only "at this meeting Lin Biao gave a long speech", goes against it. The reader read the same sentence as a compiler's order. I read 先后 as a statement of sequence. If the owner rules that such a word in a chronicle is no basis, this link goes.

## Days the reader left, and what I found

- Oct. 10, Lodge's briefing (vietnam-2) and the talk at Lodge's house (vietnam-3). Gibbons (IV, 450–52) gives the briefing without a day, and the private talk "on October 10". Nothing says which came first. No link. (The briefing's day is the entry's, not Gibbons's: the text of Lodge's statement is in Saigon 8262 of Oct. 12.)
- Oct. 15, Katzenbach's memorandum (vietnam-2) and Carver's (vietnam-3), against the NSC meeting of 2:40–3:30 p.m. Katzenbach's begins "You requested a position paper", which gives no hour. Helms sent Carver's on Oct. 16. Neither memorandum is tied to the meeting. No link. The NSC meeting itself has no entry; the vietnam entry is Johnson's private session with McNamara and Wheeler. FRUS doc. 272, n.2 (the President's Daily Diary) says that session began at 2:10 p.m., and the edit sets that hour in place of the kind range.
- Oct. 24, Ye's words at the second group (china-leadership-12) and Mao's evening talk at the pool (china-leadership). Ye repeats what "the Chairman says" ("anti-Party and anti-socialist"), and Mao said it that evening. But the Ye nianpu (II, 947) prints the group item without an hour and then "△ evening", when Ye reported to Mao on the missile test. A chronicle's printed order is no order, and Ye may have had Mao's view before the evening. No link.
- Oct. 28, Zhou at Mao's (china-leadership) and Zhou's closing speech (china-leadership-2). The Zhou nianpu (III, 82) has Zhou going to Mao's "to ask for instructions on conveying the conference's spirit and on the specific policy measures", and then the closing speech, which explains "some specific policy rules". The Mao nianpu has Mao calling the meeting, with no hour. Asking for instructions suggests a time before the speech, but conveying the spirit is work for after the close. That is likelihood, not order. No link.
- Oct. 3, Zhou's reception of the Pakistani delegation and Chen Yi's speech at its thanks banquet. Both are in the evening. The Chen Yi nianpu (1167) gives them as two items in the same evening, with no order. No link.
- Oct. 1, 2, 5, 7, 11–14, 16, 18, 19, 21, 22, 26, 27, 29–31: I read each day. No link: the hours or ranges already place the entries, or nothing in the lines or their sources orders them.
