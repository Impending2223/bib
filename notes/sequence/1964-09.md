I opened no outside source: the basis of each link is the entries' own words and notes, and a line below says so where the note, not the line, shows the order. The month is mostly clock-placed (the Peking diplomatic days carry an hour or a band each), and its same-day pairs rarely say which came first.

## The links

- Sept. 10: the Senate's vote on the Rules Committee's inquiry into the D.C. Stadium (baker, roll calls 514 and 519) follows the cloture vote on the Dirksen–Mansfield rider (court, roll call 512). Basis: the numbers of the voteview links the two entries already carry; the Senate numbers its roll calls as it takes them. Same rule as the 1964-05 and 1968-07 passes. Both entries hold the Senate's whole sitting as their range, so nothing else placed them.
- Sept. 21: Westmoreland's message on resuming the Desoto patrols (vietnam-tonkin-2) follows Sharp's (vietnam-tonkin). Basis: the notes' own date-time groups, Sharp's "CP 210025Z" and Westmoreland's "COMUSMACV 211422Z" (both cited by Marolda, 460 nn.57–58, and both still behind "To check"). The notes give the order; the entries' lines do not. Neither entry has a `time` field, and the groups are Zulu, so Sharp's, at 00:25 on the 21st, falls on the afternoon of the 20th in Honolulu; both stay dated the 21st.

## What I left unlinked, and why

- Sept. 4: Zhou's note on Lü Zhengcao's railway report and Deng's reading of the same report (china-leadership, china-leadership-4). Zhou's line repeats Deng's remark on the command post at Xichang, but neither note says who read first.
- Sept. 8: Bundy's memorandum to Johnson "with the courses of action agreed with Taylor" and the Chiefs' talking paper "discussed with McNamara and Taylor" (vietnam-3, vietnam-2). Both rest on the same Sept. 8 courses of action; nothing says which was written first.
- Sept. 9: Johnson's ruling on American jets "outside NSAM 314" (vietnam-macv) and the 11 a.m. meeting (vietnam). The ruling is probably the meeting's, and the note says "See" the meeting, but Cosmas (136) is not read here and the line does not say the ruling came at the meeting. An auditor with Cosmas could link it.
- Sept. 13: Mao's afternoon talk with Miyamoto at Hangzhou (china-leadership) and Chen Yi at the airport to see him off (china-leadership-3). Peng Zhen's note says Miyamoto flew to Hangzhou with him in the morning and left for home that evening, so the see-off could be the Peking departure before the talk or a later one. The Chen Yi nianpu (p. 1049) would say; I did not fetch it.
- Sept. 20: Boumaza hands Mao Ben Bella's letter (china-leadership) and Chen Yi sees off the delegation that morning (china-leadership-3). The same doubt: the delegation may have flown to meet Mao and left from there.
- Sept. 25: Johnson at El Paso (latin-america), Eufaula, and Oklahoma City. The Public Papers print the three in that order (1117, 1122, 1128), and Oklahoma City is already linked after Eufaula. Nothing in the lines puts El Paso first, and a print order alone is no basis (R25c), so no link. The Daily Diary does not cover the day.
- Sept. 27 and 28: Sihanouk's day (Zhou's airport welcome at noon, Mao's evening meeting, Zhou's talks with Liu, Liu's banquet speech) and on the 28th Mao with Sihanouk against Zhou's luncheon. The airport entry and the evening entries are placed by hour or band. The evening ones (Mao's meeting, the talks, the banquet) share a band, and the notes give Zhou's "6 p.m." for Mao's meeting under the date Sept. 28 and the banquet only as "evening"; no order is stated. On the 28th Mao's meeting with Sihanouk has no hour.
- Sept. 29: Khrushchev's talk with Sukarno and his toast at the lunch for Sukarno (soviet-union-leadership, -2). Documents 22 and 23 of the stenograms follow each other, but a print order is no order, and the talk's place is not given.
- Sept. 22: the Presidium stenogram on the 1966–70 lines and Khrushchev's note on aircraft engines (soviet-union-leadership, -2). The second note's own "Check" asks for the day against the stenogram.
- Sept. 24: Forrestal to McNaughton "sent on to Bundy" and Bundy to Johnson (vietnam-2, vietnam). Nothing says Bundy's memorandum came after the one he was sent.
- Sept. 2: the Housing Act (78 Stat. 769) and the Interest Equalization Tax Act (78 Stat. 809), both signed that day. The Statutes' page order and the Public Law numbers are serial numbers, which R25c says are no order alone.
- Sept. 1, 8: the primaries (Michigan's three entries; New Hampshire, Vermont, Wisconsin, Arizona on the 8th; Massachusetts and Washington on the 15th) are all election-night entries with no order among them.
- Sept. 3, 7, 12, 18, 19, 22: pairs of entries sharing an event or a day (the Johnson and Goldwater campaign entries, the circulars of the Vietnam Workers' Party Secretariat, the Chinese banquets, the Desoto entries of the 19th) were each read for an order and show none.

- Several days cite the same Chinese chronology (Chen Yi, Zhou) for entries that carry a band (workday midday, evening). The bands separate most of them; the Peking receptions of one evening (Sept. 2, 7, 15, 19, 29) share the 19:00–22:00 band and no entry says which came first.
- Sept. 27: the entry on the Sihanouk banquet carries a "Check" naming Sept. 27 as the first day of the visit against the book's Sept. 26. The Sept. 26 entries (Guangzhou) and the Sept. 27 entries read consistently with that.
