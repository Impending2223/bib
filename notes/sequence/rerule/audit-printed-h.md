## The rule for narrative books

Ruling 56, the fifth reading: a narrative's page order counts only where the book tells that day through in order, and the basis then names the book as a narrative. I applied it to every link resting on Yang or Witcover. Six such links stand, and their basis now reads "the printed order alone, in a narrative that tells the day through in order". One is struck (Mar. 23, 1968, below).

- July 14, 1967 (two links), Yang, I, 495: the day's paragraph is a dated chronicle of the Chongqing fighting and tells the day in order: the reception, the 9 a.m. order, the accord and its signing, the meeting.
- Aug. 6, 1967, Yang, II, 1096: the paragraph runs Aug. 5 (Jakarta), then the note of Aug. 6, then "the afternoon of Aug. 6".
- Aug. 13, 1967, Yang, I, 496–497: the dated chronicle gives the Guojiatuo attack, then "同日" the Fierce Tiger Regiment's losses and the shooting of Tang Tianlun. This is the weakest of the six: "同日" says "the same day", not "then".
- Aug. 26, 1967, Yang, II, 669: the passage tells the day through: the thirteen held and Zhu Jingwen killed, then the call, then "放下電話就" the meeting, then the 27th. The call reports a list already drawn up of people already held, which fits.
- Mar. 21, 1968, Witcover, 109: the one-day trip is told by its stops, "Atlanta, ... Tuscaloosa and ... Vanderbilt", and again as "Georgia, Alabama and Tennessee". This is a list, not a scene-by-scene telling. I kept it because the book gives the day's stops twice in the same order and the route runs the same way. The owner may strike it by rule with the other narrative links.
- May 11, 1968, Witcover, 190: the book tells the day through: Aberdeen, then Huron, then "Then Kennedy returned to Omaha".

## Cases left without a link, and why

- Feb. 19, 1968: Mao's reception of the garrison units (`china-leadership`) and Chen Yi at the China–Africa cocktail party (`-2`). Chen Yi nianpu, 1200 prints the reception first. On Aug. 5, 1968 the same volume (1206) prints Mao's 5 p.m. reception of Arshad Husain before Zhou's 2:30 p.m. one. So it puts Mao's reception first even when it came later, and its order between Mao's reception and Chen Yi's own engagement is not taken as a time order. The default is recorded here. If the owner holds that only a day shown out of order loses the presumption, this link goes in by rule: `cal.1968-02-19.china-leadership-2` after `cal.1968-02-19.china-leadership`, Chen Yi nianpu, 1200.
- July 24, 1967, Zhou hears Yang Chengwu (`-3`): the day itself is in doubt (the entry's Check; the audit of 1967-07). No link on a day in doubt.
