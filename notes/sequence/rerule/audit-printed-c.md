## Left without a link, and why

- Items under a heading of two or more dates, or a span ("Sept. 16–Oct. 12"): the volumes do not sort these by the hour among the single days (Deng prints June 15–16, 1962 before June 15). No link on any of them.
- Items printed inside another day's paragraph ("on the 9th, again to Chen Yun's"): not among the day's items. July 9, 12 and 23, 1962; Jan. 8, Apr. 12, May 9, June 3, July 12 and 21, 1963 (Zhou); June 19, 1963 (Chen Yi's poem, printed in June 11's item); July 31, 1962 (Deng); several Peng Zhen and *Wengao* days.
- Days where an hour goes against the print: Oct. 27, 1962 (Zhou: the 9:30 a.m. reception printed after a State Council plenary); July 11, 1962 (Zhou: the Center's notice printed after a report to Mao that ran to midnight); June 27, 1962 (*Wengao*: a 1 a.m. note printed second); Apr. 13, 1963 (Liu: Chen Yi's chronology puts the Air Force Day before the 7 p.m. banquet the Liu chronology prints first).
- Jan. 31, 1963 (Zhou): the script talks were on the trains to and from Suzhou, so part of that entry came before the Suzhou visit.
- Oct. 28, 1962 (foreign activities): one item names both meetings, "separately"; that is a list, not an order.
- Items that are another's act abroad (the Tokyo broadcast of Oct. 1, 1962; the cables sent to Chen Yi on Sept. 27, 28 and 30, 1963; the Pyongyang gift of July 1, 1963): the chronology records them on the day, and I took it that the editors do not sort them by the hour against the subject's own acts in Peking. A default; the owner may rule otherwise.
- Kept with a flag: the death notices printed after the subject's acts (Long Yun, June 27, 1962; Shen Junru, June 11, 1963). They are linked on the presumption, but they are notices of another's death, not the subject's act.
- Not swept: the Public Papers and other volumes outside the Chinese chronologies, and Yang Shangkun's diary (its own default, ruling 55).
