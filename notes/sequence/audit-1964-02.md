# Audit of the sequence pass, February 1964 (R25c, R33b)

The patch I audited is `inbox/2026-11-25-sequence-1964-02.yaml`, written by a Sonnet reader, with its note `notes/sequence/1964-02.md`. I read all 29 days of the month from the day sheet (`day_order.py 1964 --from 1964-02 --until 1964-02 --full`), each day's entries together. For each of the reader's links I read the two entries in full. Where the lines did not settle the order, I read the pages the link rests on. The corrected patch is in place: 22 `follows` ops and 4 `edit` ops. `./bib patch-check` gives 0 errors and 3 warnings. Each warning is an edit that sets only `n`, which is expected, since each one adds a sentence giving the basis of a link. I disregarded nothing under R0, because the batch brief set no limit.

## The two kinds of basis the brief asked about

- The Congressional Record within one day's debate. Two links rest on the Record's page order: Feb. 7, Harris's amendment (2489–92) after Rains's remarks (2465), and Feb. 8, the age amendment (2596–99) after the "sex" amendment (2577–84). The Record prints a day's proceedings in the order they happened, so page order within one day is the order of the debate. This is not a collection's print order of the kind R25c sets aside. Both links stand. Risen agrees on Feb. 7 (159–60: Harris's amendment in the Title VI debate).
- Yang Shangkun's diary. Several links, the reader's and the tree's, rest on the order in which Yang writes the items of one day. I read the diary pages and applied the test the April 1964 audit used. The diary is one man's account of his own day, written in parts of the day. On these pages every clock time and every part of the day stands in the diary's order. So the order is a basis wherever the items' own words agree with it and nothing goes against it. Those links are the weakest kind, and I list them below.

## Links in the tree

- Feb. 19, the budget report (china-leadership-7) after Yang's quarter hour at the Public Security auditorium (china-leadership-6). The reader flagged this link as resting on print order alone. The diary (II, 353) reads: "下午去公安部礼堂，在放陈总访非报告录音之前，讲了1刻钟的话。参加财政预算的汇报。晚间去外办看了电影". The budget report has no time word of its own. It stands between the afternoon item and the evening item, and nothing goes against that order. By the diary test above, the link stands, as the weakest kind. The note's words "下午……参加财政预算的汇报" join two sentences of the diary. The "下午" belongs to the sentence before.
- The other Yang links that rest on the diary's order within one part of the day, read on the page and kept: Feb. 9, Tian Jiaying after Zhou's call ("晚间恩来同志来电话，田家英同志来谈话", II, 350); Feb. 10, Feng Xuan and Ji Pengfei after Li Shuhuai, in one afternoon clause (II, 351); Feb. 17, Kang Sheng's call after the 8 to 10 p.m. talk (II, 353); Feb. 22, Wang Ruilin and Tian Chou after the funeral talk, both afternoon (II, 354). On the clock: Feb. 6, the ball after midnight after the evening report (II, 350); Feb. 12, Mao's reception after Liu's 4 to 6:30 dinner (II, 351); Feb. 18, the Soviet ambassador "以后5时" after Peng Zhen (II, 353); Feb. 23, An Ziwen after the 3:30 call at the hospital (II, 354).
- Feb. 10, Johnson's call to Rauh and Mitchell (civil-rights-bill-2) after the passage. The entry's own words ("Minutes after the House's vote", Mitchell's account) show the link, so it stays. The record is in doubt, however. Whalen (119, citing the President's Appointment File), Purdum (198) and Risen (162: the call while Rauh and Mitchell watch "the final sputterings of the debate", before the committee rises and "a few hours" before the vote) all put the call before the vote. The entry already carries Checks on this. If the Appointment File is read and gives about 4 p.m., the link comes out and the line is corrected.
- Feb. 10, civil-rights-bill-3 (Johnson and Robert Kennedy, 6:30 and 9:07 p.m.) after the passage. The vote came after 8 p.m.: Halleck's call "just after 8:30" is on the vote, and Purdum has the 9:07 call less than an hour after it. So the 6:30 call may have come before the vote. The Miller Center's page for it lists only Senate strategy and cloture. The entry holds two calls and should be split (R25b). The 9:07 call follows the passage; the 6:30 call needs its own reading. I left the link.
- Feb. 7, economy (the Senate's vote) after economy-2. Economy-2 holds Johnson's 11:36 a.m. call to Mills "before the Senate's vote" and his 7:25 p.m. call to O'Brien, which came after the vote. The link holds for the first call. The entry should be split (R25b).

No link in the tree is shown wrong by the record, so there is no `remove` op.
