## The reader's links: 6 checked, 4 kept, 2 struck, none turned

- May 7, china-leadership-3 (Zhou with the Sichuan delegates; Chen Boda reads the Center's Decision) after china-leadership-5 (the Center issues its ten decisions on Sichuan). **Struck.** The nianpu (III, 147) says only that Chen Boda read the Decision at the meeting (会上，陈伯达宣读). Yang (I, 409) says only that the Center issued it May 7 (中央發布了). Neither puts the issuing before the meeting, and the reading may itself be how the Decision was issued. Nothing says which came first.
- May 15, vietnam-congress-2 (Mansfield, Morse) after vietnam-congress (Cooper). **Kept, on a better basis than page order.** Mansfield opens (12,593): he has read Cooper's speech "and I also listened to what he had to say and the colloquy which ensued subsequent to his deliverance of the speech"; later, Cooper's proposal "this afternoon". Morse at 12,607 says Mansfield "has performed a great service here today". An "Order:" sentence added to the note.
- May 16, vietnam-air-war (Johnson approves bombing the Hanoi power plant) after vietnam (the Tuesday lunch). **Struck.** FRUS V, doc. 174 n.5 says only that the decision "presumably" came at the lunch; Gibbons (IV, 568) says "On May 16, he approved", with nothing on the lunch. A presumption is likelihood, not an order. And if the approval was taken at the lunch, it is the lunch's own act and comes at the same hour, not after it.
- May 18, china-leadership-8 (the editorial "A Great Historical Document" published) after china-leadership (Mao corrects its draft at 2:30 a.m.). **Kept.** Mao's line says he corrects the draft "for the publication" and sends it "back at once"; the correction of a draft comes before the printing of the text. The words show it.
- May 23, vietnam-congress (Morton) after vietnam-congress-2 (Pell). **Kept, on a clock.** Pell speaks at 13,498–502; at 13,533 "The hour of 2 o'clock having arrived", the Chair lays the unfinished business before the Senate; Morton speaks at 13,534, after a conference report. So Pell spoke before 2 p.m. and Morton after. Morton's entry had no time field; the edit sets the record's range, 2 p.m. to the adjournment at 5:21 p.m. (D249), and puts the basis in the note.
- May 24, middle-east-six-day-war-2 (Fulbright, "The Situation in the Middle East") after vietnam-congress (Mansfield, "The Security Council Should Debate Vietnam"). **Kept, on the Record's page order alone.** Both are in the Senate's proceedings of the day, Mansfield at 13,728, Fulbright at 13,788, in one run of the day's business (13,685–806), with no section of statements printed apart. The Record prints a day's proceedings in the order they happened; the audits of February 1964 and June 1965 took one chamber's page order within one day the same way. Nothing in the two speeches goes against it. This is the one link of the month that rests on page order alone; see the default below.

## A fault found on May 24

The two Senate entries of May 24 said "The Senate sat from noon to 1:49 p.m." and ended their ranges at 1:49 p.m., citing D252. That hour is the House's. The Senate adjourned "at 3 o'clock and 4 minutes p.m." (113 Cong. Rec. 13,806; Daily Digest, D251: "Adjourned at 3:04 p.m."). Both entries are corrected by edit: the sentence, the Digest page, and the range's end (19:04Z).

## A default taken, for the owner

My brief said that ruling 55 already speaks of the Record's order of a day's debate. Ruling 55 as it stands in `notes/owner-rulings.md` says nothing of the Congressional Record; it speaks of a collection's printed order, serial numbers and a diary. Two earlier audits (February 1964, June 1965) took the Record's page order within one chamber's day as the order of its debate. I took the same default for May 24 and wrote it under ruling 55, so it can be struck by rule: the Record is the primary record of the sitting, not a compiler's arrangement, and its pages within one chamber's day count as the order of the day, unless the pages show a section printed apart (statements submitted, executive business) or anything else goes against it. Of the reader's three links on page order, two (May 15 and May 23) now rest on the Record's words and clock; only May 24 rests on the pages alone.
