I read the entries in full (`./bib show`) wherever the lines did not settle an order, and opened three sources: the FRUS texts in `sources/frus-text/` (V docs 168–170, 174, 176) and the Congressional Record for May 15, 23 and 24 (govinfo, fetched by curl into a scratch directory, since deleted).

## The links

- May 7: china-leadership-3 follows china-leadership-5. Zhou's talk with the Sichuan delegates ends with Chen Boda reading the Center's Decision on Sichuan; china-leadership-5 is the Center's issuing of that Decision. The words show it.
- May 15: vietnam-congress-2 (Mansfield, Morse) follows vietnam-congress (Cooper). Basis is the Record's pages: the Cooper colloquy runs 12578–84; Mansfield's "If we remain silent" is at 12593, Morse's reply at 12608–09. I read the pages.
- May 16: vietnam-air-war (Johnson approves bombing the Hanoi power plant) follows vietnam (the Tuesday lunch on the question). Basis is a presumption: FRUS V, doc. 174 n.5 says the decision to authorize Rolling Thunder 56 "presumably" came at the lunch, and the Bundy memorandum of that doc, May 16, puts the attack as still open. If the owner holds that a presumption is not an order, strike this link; the approval then sorts by its kind range, before the lunch.
- May 18: china-leadership-8 (the editorial "A Great Historical Document" published) follows china-leadership (Mao corrects its draft at 2:30 a.m.). The author's last correction comes before the printing.
- May 23: vietnam-congress (Morton) follows vietnam-congress-2 (Pell). Basis is the Record's pages: Pell begins at 13498, Morton at 13534 in the same sitting. I read the pages.
- May 24: middle-east-six-day-war-2 (Fulbright, "The Situation in the Middle East") follows vietnam-congress (Mansfield, "The Security Council Should Debate Vietnam"). Basis is the Record's pages: Mansfield's text is at 13728, Fulbright begins at 13788. I read the pages.

The three Record links rest on a default I took: the Record's pagination of one sitting is the order of the sitting. It is the primary record of the sitting, not a collection printing a day's items; but R25c is silent on it, so the owner may overrule it. If the owner does, strike the links of May 15, 23 and 24.

## Days with no link, and why

- May 1: no link. The two May Day rostrum entries (Zhou with Yao Dengshan; Chen Yi) are of one hour. The two Vietnam-program memoranda (Enthoven; William Bundy) have no order in their lines.
- May 2: no link. Mansfield on *Today* and Johnson's breakfast with Mansfield and Morse are both May 2, but the lines give no hour for either.
- May 3 and 4: no link. On May 4 Johnson's note on Fulbright and the *Newsday* interview have nothing to say which came first; the note concerns Rusk's refusal to testify in public, not the interview.
- May 5: no link between Zhou's letter to Chen Yi and the others and his talk with the same men. The Zhou nianpu prints the talk as "the item after" the letter, but a collection's order is no order (R25c). The letter says it is "a warning, lest it be said I did not tell you before", which would put it first, but that is not enough.
- May 7: no link for china-leadership-4 (Mao asks how the Sichuan document "was revised"). It may come before or after the Decision's issue. The Zhou entry takes the Decision as read, which is why china-leadership-3 is linked.
- May 8: no link among Zhou's items (before-dawn talks; Tibet; the forum of military-training cadres). Only the first is timed, "before dawn"; the nianpu's "item after" for the others is collection order. Zhou's Tibet answer says he settled it "last night"; that fixes nothing among the day's items.
- May 9: no link among the five air-war entries, after reading FRUS V, docs 168 to 170. Rostow's memorandum of 11 a.m. already summarizes the Vance and Bundy papers and says he will send them up "later today"; doc. 169 n.1 says Rostow sent all three to the President that evening. So the sending of Vance's and Bundy's papers came after 11 a.m., but the papers themselves were written before it, and their entries record the memoranda, not the sending. The entries are not told apart by their lines.
- May 11: Morton's Senate speech on pacification comes after Bunker's press conference in Saigon by their time ranges (the Saigon morning is the earlier part of the day in UTC). No link needed. Likewise Zhou's evening talk with the foreign-affairs rebels after the morning storming of the Foreign Ministry, and Johnson's news conference of May 18 after Mansfield's speech: the times place them.
- May 12: no link. The lunch (1:24) and the dinner with Russell are placed by hour.
- May 13: no link among china-leadership-3, -5 and -6. See below.
- May 14 to 15: no link beyond the one above. trade-2 already follows trade.
- May 18: no link for Rusk's remarks to reporters (vietnam-diplomacy) after Mansfield's speech, though the entry says "See" it: nothing shows the hour of Rusk's remarks. Marcy's "Summary Proposal" and Fulbright's lunch with Russell "on Marcy's points": the Summary Proposal's entry itself says "perhaps the paper behind" the lunch, and Gibbons cites Marcy memoranda of both May 16 and 18, so the paper may not be the one. Left as a Check.
- May 19: no link for the Hanoi power-plant strike. The strike was on the morning of May 19 in Hanoi, which is the evening of May 18 in Washington; the Washington meeting of 5:38 p.m. May 19 comes after it, though FRUS V, doc. 176 n.2 presumes the meeting took the decision. The strike entry's own Check already holds the conflict (Gibbons has the approval May 16 and the strike after the May 19 meeting); the dates and clocks differ, so no link within one day can be written. The Hanoi defense entry (Hồ's birthday) says the shoot-downs came "that morning"; I did not link it to the strike, since the two entries date a raid and its defense by different clocks.
- May 20: air-war-2 already follows program-5 (McNamara forwards the Chiefs' memorandum). No link between the Chiefs' memoranda and McNamara's request for replies to "Bombing Options in NVN" (Drea has the Chiefs writing before they knew of the draft).
- May 21 to 22, 25 to 29, 30: no link. On May 27 Chen Yi's two evening entries ("the same day") are of one hour; Zhou's liaison-meeting decision (May 27) and Mao's "do it" (May 28) are on two days.
- May 24: the NSC (12:35 to 1:25 p.m., Johnson on the Symington and Fulbright statements that the United States cannot manage two crises) and Fulbright's speech in the Senate (from noon to 1:49 p.m. sitting, his speech from p. 13788) overlap. The line does not say that the "statements" the President speaks of were this speech; no link.
- May 26: no link between Zhou's talk with Xu, Nie, Ye, Yang and Su on Inner Mongolia and his meeting with the Hohhot delegates. The nianpu has the first under May 25 and the second as May 26's "item after"; Ye's nianpu puts the talk on May 26. That is collection order, not an order. The two existing links (middle-east-3 after -2) are right.
- May 31: no link among Zhou's items. The Mao entry (Zhou at Mao's "at noon") and the meeting Zhou calls (afternoon, by Ye's nianpu) look ordered, and Mao's entry says the Center issues the decisions "that day" after the report went to Mao; but one entry holds both the hearing and the later issuing, so a link either way would be false for part of it.
