# Sequence pass, November 1960 (R25c)

Read all 30 days of November 1960 as the site orders them (`day_order.py 1960 --from 1960-11 --until 1960-11 --full`), each day's entries together, asking of each: does it come after another entry of the same day? Patch: `inbox/2026-11-25-sequence-1960-11.yaml`, 75 links, `./bib patch-check` 0 errors. I disregarded nothing under R0. Besides the entries' own words I opened only the notes of the entries I linked or doubted; no source copy was opened. The month-only entry `cal.1960-11.soviet-union` and `cal.1960-11.transition-and-staff-cabinet-5` are undated blocks and take no link.

The basis most links rest on: the Senate Commerce Committee's *Freedom of Communications* (Part 1) prints Kennedy's remarks in the order given, and the notes of his stops cite its pages, which run on without a gap from stop to stop (840 to 958). Where the entries' own words agree with that order wherever they speak to it, and nothing goes against it, I followed it. Stops with no page in the volume, and the statements, are left out of the chains.

## The days

- Nov. 1: Kennedy's day in Los Angeles runs USC, the Elks Auditorium, the garment section, Redondo Beach, Long Beach, East Los Angeles College Stadium (the evening), pp. 840–53. Chinese entries are placed by their clock times; no link.
- Nov. 2: the Qinzheng Hall dinner for Hồ Chí Minh (6:30 p.m.) comes before the enlarged Standing Committee (8 p.m.); the stored times put them the other way, since the committee's time is 12:00Z and the dinner's range runs 11:00Z to 14:00Z. Kennedy: Beverly Hilton, San Diego airport, Horton Plaza, San Jose, Oakland, the Cow Palace (pp. 853–66). His "while we meet tonight" reply on the rescue squad follows the Eisenhower–Nixon ride in New York, which his note points back to. Nixon's late night at the Waldorf follows the day's New York events (it is "from eleven at night").
- Nov. 3: Kennedy from Phoenix (airport, breakfast, street rally) to Albuquerque, Amarillo, Wichita Falls, Oklahoma City (the shopping center, then the auditorium), pp. 866–87. Nixon's Columbia stop comes before "From Columbia to San Antonio" (the later entry's own words). The Center's directive on carrying out the Emergency Directive Letter follows the Letter.
- Nov. 4: Yang tells Chervonenko of Liu's airport speech; the word by telephone at night that Moscow has arranged it "as asked" follows. Kennedy: Norfolk, Roanoke, Toledo, then the Chicago torchlight parade (pp. 887–99).
- Nov. 5: Kennedy in New York, from the Bronx (Fordham Road, then the Concourse Plaza) through the Queens luncheon, the Boulevard Restaurant, Nassau County, Flushing, the 90th Street rally, the Commodore, the street speech outside the Coliseum, to the Coliseum (pp. 899–911). The Coliseum speech also follows the Biltmore scene, since Goodwin has Kennedy revise the draft at the Biltmore before it is given. The lost motorcade, the Biltmore scene and the midnight corner have no place in the chain beyond that (see below).
- Nov. 6: Kennedy from Waterbury (3 a.m.) to New Haven, Bridgeport, Commack, Newark, Teterboro, Jersey City and Lewiston (pp. 911–29). The Paderewski statement is not a stop and is left out.
- Nov. 7: the Moscow parade follows Hồ's call on Liu "before the parade". Nixon: Madison and Detroit, then the telethon (the Madison entry's own words); the midnight flight from Chicago follows the pre-telecast scene in Chicago. Kennedy: Providence airport, Providence City Hall, Springfield, Hartford, then the Manchester and Boston entry (pp. 930–58).
- Nov. 8: Kennedy votes, then to Hyannis Port (his entry's own words); the afternoon telephone network at Hyannis follows; the evening entries follow it (afternoon, then "7:15 p.m.", then "the evening"); Kennedy's question to O'Donnell about Ohio follows Johnson's Ohio remark on the telephone (O'Donnell 251, 252). Nixon's talk with his wife follows the conference on a statement (Six Crises 384–86, on consecutive pages).
- Nov. 9: Klein's reading of the concession is reported (Hagerty, shortly before noon), then Kennedy hears of it and says "no class", then the Armory statement (Salinger 50–53); the lunch at Joseph Kennedy's and the Secret Service and O'Donnell talk follow the Armory, as their own words say. The Chinese delegation's report to the Center follows the afternoon call of Suslov, Kozlov and Mikoyan.
- Nov. 10: nothing added. The Moscow entries already link; the two Hyannis Port entries (mid-morning and the reply to de Gaulle) have nothing to put one first.
- Nov. 11: the Hanoi Politburo's cable of the night of the coup follows the coup it reports.
- Nov. 12 and 13: nothing added; the clock times and ranges place the entries.
- Nov. 14: Alia's praise of Deng's speech follows the speech. Kennedy's breakfast at Palm Beach (Sharon's report) comes before the flight to Key Biscayne.
- Nov. 15: the delegation's meeting that night, on "the first round", follows the conference's afternoon session.
- Nov. 16: not linked: Zhou's telephone call to Moscow and the Huairen Tang meeting (see below).
- Nov. 17 and 18: nothing added.
- Nov. 19: Liu's order of pardon follows the Standing Committee's decision.
- Nov. 20 and 21: nothing added.
- Nov. 22: Yang's putting the Chinese down for a second speech comes at the close of the debate, after the speeches in the Moscow entry; the evening weighing of Khrushchev's speech follows it. The delegation's receiving the Center's answer follows the Peking meeting on the telegram that the answer replies to.
- Nov. 23: the Chinese ask to speak at the close of the session, after Khrushchev's speech; the evening meeting on the milder speech follows it.
- Nov. 24: Lukman's speech "after the break" comes after Deng's speech.
- Nov. 25: Hồ's signed letter to Liu arrives after the delegations are gathered to write it.
- Nov. 26: Yang's evening visit to Hồ, with the news of the six delegations' call on Khrushchev, follows that call.
- Nov. 27: nothing added.
- Nov. 28: the Center's answer to the request of the 25th, read that night, follows the Center's cable (11:30 p.m. Peking) and the afternoon meeting that records that Peking has not answered.
- Nov. 29: Deng's telling Kapo "of the Sino-Soviet talks" follows the Peng–Kozlov talk of the noon.
- Nov. 30: Yang's planning of Liu's visit (five days, aircraft on the night of Dec. 2) follows the settlement of the visit at the Lenin Hills talks.

## What else the reading showed

- Nov. 7, `elections-1960-kennedy` (Providence airport). Kennedy says "we started at Waterbury, Conn., at 3 o'clock this morning" and names Connecticut, New York, New Jersey and Maine "that day", which is Nov. 6's itinerary; the volume prints the airport after Lewiston. The entry is dated Nov. 7. Either he spoke after midnight and meant the day before, or the entry belongs to Nov. 6. I linked it as the first of Nov. 7 and changed nothing.
- Nov. 1, `elections-1960-kennedy-5` (Long Beach) has Kennedy speak of Nixon's ride through New York, which the tree dates Nov. 2; the remarks are probably ahead of the ride ("will take"), not the ride itself. Worth one look at the note.
- Nov. 5, `elections-1960-2` ("midnight, a street corner"): its note says the remarks were outside the Coliseum, with Johnson, and that the sources give no midnight, no street corner and no Stevenson suggestion. Its record range (11:30 p.m. to 12:30 a.m.) rests on "midnight" and sorts it ahead of the unplaced entries around it. I linked it by the volume's pages; the range needs an audit's look.
- Nov. 6, `soviet-union-2` (Khrushchev's dinner) is "in the evening" in Wu and the entry's range, but Yang's diary has the Presidium's lunch from 2 p.m.; the Check is in the note.
- Nov. 22, `china-leadership-6` and the afternoon of `soviet-union` record the same act (the Chinese put down for a second speech).
- Nov. 23, `china-leadership-5` (the Chinese ask to speak, Khrushchev proposes the next day) is a second asking after Nov. 22's `-6`; the sources would show whether it is one act or two.
