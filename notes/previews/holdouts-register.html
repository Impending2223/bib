Holdouts, 1960: a register

Two things to look at: how a standing condition shows on the paper's own day, in the day's run of entries; and the register that gathers every such report by person, with the day it ended where the calendar holds one.

1. On the day: Wednesday, Sept. 7, 1960

As it would read in the calendar page, among the day's other entries (two neighbours shown). The standing condition is set off by a rule and the word "Standing", so it does not read as an act.

Wednesday, Sept. 7
1960 elections: Kennedy's campaign
Kennedy at Portland, the Civic Auditorium: the fight for peace.
New York Times, Sept. 8, 1960, at 1, 24.
Standing · 1960 elections: Kennedy's campaign
As the Times reports it: Byrd of Virginia has not endorsed the Democratic ticket; neither of Georgia's Senators, Russell or Talmadge, has spoken for Kennedy.
New York Times, Sept. 7, 1960, at 1, 35 ("Democrats Face a Setback in South, Survey Indicates," by Claude Sitton). Names: Byrd, Russell, Talmadge, Kennedy.
1960 elections: Kennedy's campaign
The Times prints Claude Sitton's report of two weeks of interviews in the South. Battle, of the Virginia campaign: "There's no doubt about it—we're at our low point now." Hollings forecasts an "uphill battle"; Hodges: "a little too close." Collins and Milligan: Florida lost unless the State's officeholders join.
New York Times, Sept. 7, 1960, at 1, 35.

Built from the calendar's standing entries (tag form:state), a row a person by their name tag; each report a dated link to its entry; the end, the event that closed it (an endorsement, a first appearance), when the calendar holds one.
