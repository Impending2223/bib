# Entries

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

```yaml
- id: kja.beschloss-crisis-years       # permanent; never rename or reuse
  s: 'Kennedy, Robert F.'              # optional bold subject line (people, memoir subjects)
  c: Michael R. Beschloss, *The Crisis Years* (1991)   # citation; a list for several works
  r: Attorney General, 1961–64         # people (Part III): the role
  n: The note. Free prose.             # optional
  tags: [topic:berlin, todo]           # optional, stored tags
  aliases: [kja.old-id]                # ids folded into this one by ./bib combine
```

A list entry for a work points to its card instead of writing the citation (`work: w.beschloss-crisis-years`, or a list for several) and keeps its own note; "The works catalogue" below. A new entry may still be written with `c`: `./bib merge` and `./bib works adopt` make the card.

Calendar entries have no `s` (the build makes the rubric, "Cuba, Apr. 15–19", and files the entry under its first day):

```yaml
- id: cal.1961-04-15.cuba              # cal.<date>.<thread>, -2 if taken
  when: Apr. 15–19                     # shown as written
  date: '1961-04-15'                   # sort and range key; '1961-02' for month-only entries
  thread: cuba                         # primary thread (slug of a cal.thread.* entry)
  also: [space]                        # other threads it belongs to
  short: State of the Union            # an address that sets several threads going: its name, and the threads,
  to: [economy, {health: cal.1963-02-05.program}]   # each resolving to its next entry, or the entry named
  c: What happened.
  n: Sources. See [[cal.1961-02-20.school-aid]].
  gloss: {title: Style, text: [A paragraph., Another.]}   # a note on the entry, not an entry: under it, smaller; the title run in
```

A `gloss` is for what the entry rests on and does not say: the record behind a name or a date, laid out in the calendar's voice, quotations marked and cited, and what each piece of the record is and is not, stated plainly and without a side (Jan. 30, 1961: the Address to the Joint Session, its *Style*). Rare.

Spans (the owner's ruling, Oct. 20, 2026): an entry is an event-day. Spans of two or three days are good, under a week tolerable; anything longer deserves close scrutiny, and an entry never bundles several events (an announcement and the act it announces, hearings and the vote, a note and its reply): each gets its own entry, dated to the day its record bears, the first keeping the event's bibliography and the rest pointing back. A day's "Continuing" line names what is under way; it should say something. 1961 is the model.

One event, one entry (the owner's ruling, Oct. 28, 2026: "One entry, listed in both threads"): a meeting, a call, a document or an address whose matter falls in several threads is one entry, filed under its chief thread (`thread:`) and listed in the others (`also:`), never one entry a thread. Its bibliography is in one place; each thread's index and ⟨⟨ ⟩⟩ line find it by `also:`. (Several events on one day stay several entries, as above.)

Subthreads: a thread in `th.yaml` may name a `parent:` ("Defense: McNamara to Clifford", `id: cal.thread.defense-clifford`, `s: McNamara to Clifford`, `parent: defense`). Its entries are the parent's too (the ⟨⟨ ⟩⟩ line, the highlight, the index's years on `cal.html`); the rubric, the ⟨⟨ ⟩⟩ line and the Continuing line give its full name. A subthread without a statement (`c`) is a ghost: named where it is under way, and in the thread index by a line under its parent: its full name ("Vietnam: Pennsylvania") muted, not underlined, as the A–Z bibliography's ghosts are, then its dates as any thread's (its rubric links there; STYLE 9 in `tools/bib/calsplit.py`); give it a `c` to promote it. In the index a parent's dates are its own entries only: one filed under a subthread (`thread:` the subthread, a ghost's too) is dated on the subthread's line, not the parent's, unless `also:` lists it in the parent (volume.md §2: "The ⟨⟨ ⟩⟩ line and the highlight keep the family together as now; only the index is pruned"; STYLE 15 in `tools/bib/calsplit.py`, `index_members` in `tools/bib/build.py`). `check` warns on a thread with more than 150 entries of its own in one year's page, a prompt to split it, never a rule [thread-size]; the splits come by patch, for the owner to rule. A renamed thread may keep its old name in the index, muted, a link to the thread, naming it ("Desegregation: schools: School desegregation"; `s:` the old name, `see:` the slug), or, where the old name went to several threads, the old name in the text's color and the threads muted, linked, a middle dot between ("Legislatures: Districting and apportionment · State legislatures"; `see:` a list; STYLE 10). A hidden `sort:` files a thread under other words, the name unchanged ("1960 elections" as "Elections of 1960"; "Special elections" as "Elections, special", a redirect left at S); `check` holds th.yaml to it. A statement may end in "*See also* X · Y" (`see_also:`, slugs; set right, in the thread names' type; STYLE 11), for related matter elsewhere in the index, not for a parent, its subthreads or an alphabetical neighbor (the owner's rules, Oct. 21, 2026). A university's desegregation is a subthread of School desegregation, in its State's thread too by `also:` (Ole Miss, University of Georgia). No thread or subthread name begins with "The" (the owner's rule, Oct. 21, 2026: "Jupiters", "Air war"). Use one where a thread holds two or more events at once, or where the thread's name does not say what is going on. In `th.yaml`, after its parent (alphabetical by full name). `thread_name`, `entry_threads` in `tools/bib/build.py`; `thread_line`, `is_ghost` in `tools/bib/store.py`.

The thread index (Part I of the calendar) is generated from `thread` and `also`; never write dates into `th.yaml`. A new thread is a new entry in `th.yaml` (`id: cal.thread.<slug>`, `s:` name, `c:` one-line scope).

### Markup inside text

- `*Title*`: italics. `[text](https://…)`: an outside link. A link out of the site (a DOI, a record, a newspaper) shows in the accent blue; a link within it (an entry, a work's A–Z entry, a list's section, a date) as a name link does, the text's own color with a faint underline (`:where(main) a` in `templates/list.html` and `series.html`; a link a class styles keeps its own). A work's first citation in full links its author and title to the A–Z entry without an underline (`a.xf`), the faint underline shown only under the pointer, while pressed (`:active`; on a phone, the touch before it lifts: the templates listen for touches so iOS Safari shows it), or at keyboard focus. An article's link spans the volume, the journal and the first page: `[27 J. Latin Am. Stud. 1](https://…)`.
- `[[w.id]]`: a work of the catalogue, linked to its entry on `works.html`: in the calendar, its full citation at its first appearance in each year's page (FIRST CITE), its short form after ("Beschloss, *Crisis Years*"; SHORT FORM in `tools/bib/works.py`; a card's `short` overrides); elsewhere the short form. `[[w.id|full]]` the full citation wherever it stands; `[[w.id|text]]` shows "text". A merged card's old id still answers. `check` fails on a card that does not exist.
- `[[id]]`: a link to another entry, shown as its label (a calendar date). `[[id|text]]` shows "text". Use these for "See …" in calendar notes, so a corrected date updates every reference. The build adds the year when the date linked to falls in a different year from the entry linking to it ("Dec. 21–22, 1961"); don't write the year in.
- Cross-list references stay plain prose ("(K–J Adm. II.D)", "Also Opp."). The build links them and `check` verifies the sections exist and that a cited title is in the cited section.
- YAML: quote a value in single quotes when it holds `: ` or ` #` or starts with `*`, `[`, `'` or `"`; double an apostrophe inside: `'Kennedy''s'`. This goes for the paragraphs in `list.yaml` too; `check` reports one that loaded as a mapping.
- "87th Cong." and the like are Congresses, not the list: the reference reader skips an abbreviation that follows an ordinal number.
- Quotation marks and apostrophes: type the keyboard's `'` and `"` in every hand-kept file (lists, executive/, the hand-kept sources); the build curls them in the pages' text, not in tags, URLs or scripts (`tools/bib/smart.py`, its STYLE notes), so a search or a match in the YAML needs only the one form. An inch is a prime (″), not a quotation mark. `check` warns on a curly mark in those files [straight-quotes]. Generated files keep what their sources print.

### House style (keep it)

Markdown files (`.md`, the notes, briefs, memos, listings, READMEs) are not hard-wrapped: a paragraph or a list item is one line, so the in-app reader and GitHub break lines only at the screen's edge (the owner, Nov. 5, 2026: "unwrap them all, fix the scripts"). A generator of a `.md` passes its text through `unwrap` in `tools/bib/mdwrap.py` (its STYLE); `python3 -I tools/notes/unwrap_md.py FILE.md` unwraps one by hand. Code and YAML keep their wrap.

Short declarative sentences. A calendar note points back ("See [[id]]"), not forward; where an earlier entry must name a later one, it says what it is ("Reply: [[id]]", "The order: [[id]]", "Bibliography: [[id]]" where a thread's first entry is excepted, `ok:thread-first`). A law the entry reports enacted is cited in `c` after its name, linked, as a case's U.S. Reports cite is ("Equal Pay Act signed, [77 Stat. 56](…)": the volume and page, linked; no Public Law number), and the note keeps the list's pointer ("Statute (K–J Cong. II.J.2)", as "Opinion (K–J Cong. II.J.1)"); a treaty by its U.S.T. volume and page, linked to the Library of Congress's scan (its collection's table gives the TIAS number's volume and page), and its U.N.T.S. cite, linked to the UN's copy, at its signing, the Senate's consent, and ratification. A Congressional Record cite: the year after the link, not in it, and a page of five digits with a comma ("[106 Cong. Rec. 13,819](…) (1960)"; the owner, Nov. 5, 2026; `tools/works/record_cites.py --unlink`, `tools/works/record_commas.py`; the tables' pages by `bills.rec_page`). Books already in the catalogue are cited by their cards (`[[w.id]]`), shown by short form and linked to the A–Z bibliography (no list or section); works found only here are cited in full. "Check" marks a detail to verify. In the calendar the first entry of each event carries its bibliography and later ones point back with `See [[id]]`; a thread's first entry is its first event's. Bibliography sections are chronological by publication unless their intro says "Alphabetical".

Election results in `c` (the owner's rule, Oct. 23, 2026: "fix the form to a single, prescribed standard"; "the simplest forms here, e.g., 'Anderson (R) over Governor Docking (D), 511,534 to 402,261, 55.4 percent', are perfectly fine. Keep those everywhere"). One form, commas only, no colon or semicolon: a race, "Winner (P) over Loser (P), W to L, p percent", the winner's count first, p the winner's share to one decimal (in a primary no party: "Kennedy over Humphrey, 476,024 to 366,753, 56.5 percent"); a margin, where the entry gives one or the race was close, after the share, "..., 50.3 percent, a 1,988-vote margin", never "by 1,988 votes"; a measure, yes first, p the yes share: "Proposition 1 carries, 3,008,328 to 2,834,384, 51.5 percent", "Proposal 2 fails, 1,876,185 to 3,408,090, 35.5 percent", never "yes ..., no ..." or "p percent yes"; a third candidate or more after the two, in the same sentence, "Name N, p percent". Always both counts and the percentage. The denominator: all the votes cast for the office (for a measure, yes and no) where the source prints that total (as the Clerk's figures and CQ's specials do); where it gives only the two leading counts and others ran, their sum, "p percent of the two". Where a rule differs (a measure needing a majority of all voting at the election, or two-thirds; a runoff below a majority), a few words after the figures say so ("a majority of all who voted at the election needed"). Several races or measures in one entry: each in the form, a sentence or a clause each. A missing count or share comes from the entry's own source, the series' data (`elections/`, `congress/specials*.yaml`) or a source read then, cited in `n`; else what the entry has stands, and the note says "Check: the count" (or "the percentage"). `check` warns on a result not in the form, on a share the counts do not give, and on a margin that is not W - L [result-form] (`tools/bib/resultform.py`, its STYLE); a quotation is not read, and an entry the form does not fit (several places on one ballot, a crowd's count) takes `ok:result-form`. Not results: a convention's ballot of delegates, electoral votes, the polls, other countries' parliamentary elections.

Leads (the owner's standing rule, Oct. 18, 2026; restated Nov. 5, 2026): a lead is what cannot yet go into the calendar on a source in hand: a source not reached or a host refused, an event no source read dates, a figure no source read gives, a thing recalled and not verified. It is written into `notes/leads.md` under its heading (States and cities, National, Foreign, the Executive roster), with the entry it concerns and where to look; never into the calendar unchecked. Not a lead: an event a source in hand dates, however loosely ("ten days before the convention"), which is an entry ("Check: the day" where the day is loose); a source in hand that disagrees with an entry, which is a variant (R41 of the reader's brief) or a "Check:"; an item passed over as minor, which is a row of "Passed over" with R18's ground, for the owner to rule (R44). A lead leaves the list when it is entered or settled. Every drafting brief asks its agents for their leads, and they go into the file when their work is merged.

A run of calendar dates, wherever the series prints one (the thread index, the pointers under each member and holder in the Congress and Executive rosters, the Names' "In the series"), is written by year: "Cal. **1961** Jan. 20, May 16. **1962** Jan. 22–31.", the year in the sans, a little heavier, unlinked, a full stop after each year's dates. One function writes them all (`tools/bib/datelist.py`, its STYLE notes); a new list of dates calls it.

Places in the calendar's entries (`c`): a city or town of the United States under 75,000 people at both the 1960 and the 2020 census takes its State by the Bluebook's abbreviation at its first mention in each thread, in each calendar (an entry in several threads takes it where it is the first in any): "Anniston, Ala.", "Muncie, Ind.", "Concord, N.H.". Not Gettysburg, Atlantic City, Key West or Palm Beach; Huntington, W. Va. (83,627 in 1960) takes it all the same. An unincorporated place takes it too ("Parchman, Miss.", "Longdale, Miss.", "Hye, Tex."), Hyannis Port excepted. So too, anywhere, a named military base, a cape, and Liberty Island ("Fort Benning, Ga.", "At Andrews, Md.", "West Point, N.Y.", "Homestead, Fla.", "Cape Cod, Mass.", "Cape Canaveral, Fla."), Camp David excepted; not a hospital (Bethesda Naval Hospital), nor the Oakland Army Terminal. Two places of one name take their States wherever either is meant ("Oxford, Miss.", "Oxford, Ohio"; "Albany, Ga.", "Albany, N.Y."; Athens; "Hot Springs, Va."; "Cambridge, Md."), but Philadelphia is Philadelphia (only the Mississippi town takes its State). Columbia is not the District. A county is left to the writer's judgment (those tagged so far: the Deep South's, and "Prince Edward County, Va."). A neighborhood of a city named goes without (Harlem, Watts, South Boston, Hollywood). An entry in one State's thread (Alabama, Georgia, Mississippi, South Carolina) whose places all lie in that State names no State at all: the rubric does it. Washington (the White House, the Oval Office) is no place elsewhere. Not the thread statements, not the notes, not a quotation ("in Selma, Alabama"). The census figures: Wikipedia's tables of each place's censuses, read Oct. 12, 2026 (Muncie 68,603 and 65,194; Albany, Ga. 55,890 and 69,647; Rock Hill 29,404 and 74,372; Homestead 9,152 and 80,737).

Vietnamese names and places carry their diacritics (Ngô Đình Diệm; Diệm, Khánh, Kỳ; Huế, Đà Nẵng), except where the verbatim prints none: a quotation, a title, the byline of a work printed without them (Ky, *Twenty Years and Twenty Days*; a Vietnamese-language work's byline has them), a writer who publishes without them (Lien-Hang T. Nguyen), the FRUS and APP forms. English names stay: Saigon, Hanoi, Haiphong, Cholon, Dalat, Vietnam, Tonkin, the Mekong, Tet, Viet Cong, Viet Minh, the Ho Chi Minh Trail. A person's entry in `sources/name-forms.yaml` lists the plain form first, for the sources that print it; `check` warns on a plain form in the series' own prose (the calendar, every list's notes and roles, persons' subject lines; [diacritics]; places: `VIET_PLACES` in `tools/bib/check.py`). Keys and anchors fold the marks, Đ to d (`ngo-dinh-diem`).

## Standing conditions and subentries (the owner, Nov. 12, 2026)

- A condition of public importance a paper reports as such (a senator who has not endorsed the ticket; a bill held in committee) is an entry on the paper's day, `c` "As the *Times* reports it: ...", tagged `form:state` (notes/briefs/batch12-press.md). On the day it is set off by a rule and the label "As reported"; the calendar's index gathers every such entry by thread, "Standing conditions, as reported" (`tools/bib/standing.py`; the owner: "As reported. Every standing condition, grouped by thread.").
- One event with several matters (a news conference, a session with several decisions): a parent entry states what is true of all (who, where, when; the common bibliography), and each matter is a subentry, `part_of: <parent id>`, with its own thread, statement and sources, set a hair indented under the parent; `follows:` between siblings gives their order (`tools/bib/subentries.py`). A parent carries its subentries' threads for the highlight. Converted so far: the news conferences of Feb. 17 and Sept. 7, 1960, and July 19, 1961.
