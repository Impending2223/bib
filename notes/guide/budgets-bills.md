# Federal budgets, the debt limit, and bills

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

`tools/bib/budgets.py` and `tools/bib/bills.py` (STYLE notes at the top of each):

- A January budget entry tagged `budget:<FY>` (fiscal 1961–70) carries two tables, stacked (the owner's request, Oct. 22, 2026: no sideways scrolling, no empty columns, titles that name the year). First "Fiscal 1964: the administrative budget, proposed and first reported" (the unified from fiscal 1969), receipts by source then expenditures by function: Proposed (that January's *Budget*), First reported (the *Budget* two Januaries later; fiscal 1967's expenditures from the Treasury's *Annual Report*; fiscal 1968's not found, so that year has no such column, its note saying why). Then "Fiscal 1964 on today's unified basis (OMB, *Historical Tables* 2.1, 3.1)", receipts then outlays, today's functions, one column headed by the edition read ("FY2025 ed."), never summed with the first. "Today" never stands alone in a title or head. Tables under each January entry, not indicator series filed in June: the proposal belongs to the message; the then and now classifications share no rows (indicators STYLE 8), so a June row a line would not align them; and twenty lines a June would swamp the month tables. `budgets/budgets.yaml` is read off FRASER's scans (each column's document, table, printed page and PDF page); a line shares a row with another column's only where it means the same thing. `check` holds each column's lines to its printed total (half a unit a line). Today's: `python3 -I tools/budgets/make_today.py` (needs the network; the build does not).
- The entry tagged `debt-limits` (the Debt limit thread's first, June 8, 1960) carries the table of the thirteen acts (OMB Table 7.3, each act read in the Statutes); every act has its signing entry, the House and Senate roll calls entries of their own (thread `economy-debt-limit`).
- The entries tagged `bill:<key>` carry the bill's course (`congress/bills.yaml`): the entry of its first introduction in either chamber (one made where none told it), and on each later year's page its course runs into, that year's first entry concerning the bill; never twice on a page (the owner, Nov. 4, 2026; `check` holds them; bills.py STYLE 6-7). The small-caps title alone; the chamber "H" or "S", unheaded, the President's rows empty. Each step dated by the bound Record's daily parts (the History of Bills prints pages, not dates), its page linked to that chamber's day on govinfo, its roll calls linked to Voteview. Now the Revenue Acts of 1962 and 1964, the Trade Expansion Act, the Civil Rights Acts of 1964 and 1968, the Economic Opportunity Act, the Southeast Asia (Gulf of Tonkin) resolution, the Voting Rights Act, the Social Security Amendments of 1965, the Elementary and Secondary Education Act, the Immigration and Nationality Act Amendments, the Tax Adjustment Act and the Revenue and Expenditure Control Act. A page turns to its day by `tools/congress/make_record_pages.py OUT YEAR...` (needs the network; the build does not): each bound part's MODS on govinfo, every granule's first and last page, day and chamber, written as `pages-<YEAR>.tsv` (also the input `tools/executive/make_source_links.py` reads); the History's page is looked up in the chamber the step names. A page the History misprints (another day's, another chamber's) is replaced by the page the Record's day shows, the step's note saying so; a step the History does not give (the message, a roll call on an amendment) is dated by its own record (the Public Papers, Voteview). A day of many roll calls on amendments is one row, the first and last linked.
- A session's adjournment entry tagged `appropriations:<Congress>-<session>` (86-2 to 90-2) carries the session's appropriation bills against the President's estimates (`congress/appropriations.yaml`, `tools/bib/appropriations.py`, its STYLE): one table a session, read off the table printed in the bound Record at the session's end (the House or Senate Appropriations Committee's, found by the Record's Index under Appropriations, Tables), each figure as printed in whole dollars, shown in $m: estimate, enacted, change, in the source's groups (the fiscal year's bills, the supplementals) and with its printed totals; loan and contract authority in parentheses, uncounted, as printed. The sources differ session to session (the estimates to the Senate, or the House's; the 1964 summary prints no estimates, the 1965 table "savings"): each table names its source's columns. `check` fails where a row's estimate and change do not make its amount or the rows do not add to the printed total, unless the row or total carries the source's `misprint`, shown under the table.
- Revenue effects of the tax acts: Tempalski, OTA Working Paper 81 (`[[w.tempalski-revenue-effects-of-major]]`), by table and page, in the signing entries of the Revenue Acts of 1962 and 1964, the Tax Adjustment Act and the Revenue and Expenditure Control Act; the paper has no figures for the excise act of 1965 or the investment credit's suspension and restoration.
