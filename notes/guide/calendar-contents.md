# The calendar's contents, and its tables and maps

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

The test width (the owner's, Nov. 4, 2026: their phone renders pages 523 CSS px wide): every table and map, in the calendar and on every page, is checked at a 523px viewport (Playwright, `viewport: {width: 523, ...}`): its columns kept, the page never scrolling sideways (a table wider than its column scrolls in its own box). The older "fits 400px" rules stay as a floor; 523px is the width the owner reads at. Screenshots for the owner at 523px.

Nothing folded by default (the owner's rule, Oct. 30, 2026: "I'm not a fan of the collapsed military tables, for example. I want that reversed and not followed as a norm"): a table or map in the calendar is shown open; a fold only where the owner asked for one (the day's materials, Oct. 22); the forces tables' folds reversed (Nov. 3, 2026); the other folds now in the build kept (the same day: "Keep the other folds for now") (`notes/extensions/memos/volume.md`, §1). Two standing rules (the owner's rules, Oct. 21, 2026; `tools/bib/contents.py`, its STYLE notes):

- The contents at the head of each calendar page (and the Outline drawer, a copy of them): the Threads line, then the months in a grid, a year cell spanning the year's rows and six months a row (Jan–Jun, Jul–Dec), each month in its three-letter form ("Sep"; this form here only, the Bluebook's everywhere else), linked to its section; a month the page lacks leaves its cell empty, a year takes only the rows it has months in (1969 one; the primary seasons of 1972 and 1976 after a gap). January 1966's two sections are one cell, the first's. Every year's page, and the index, carry the whole calendar's grid, each month linked through to its year's page; a year's page lists its own tables and maps, the index the whole calendar's (volume.md §3; contents STYLE 6; the owner, Nov. 4, 2026): another year's cell in the year column links to that page's top, muted.
- After the grid, under "Tables and maps", every table and map of the page (the index: of the calendar), year by year, in the order it first appears, each linked to its anchor, grouped by year in the grid's year column: the Executive Branch at each term, each Congress at its opening, the leaders and committees, the November elections, the governors, the legislatures, State finance, the budgets, the debt limit acts, a bill's course, the session's appropriations, each party's primaries, the conventions' delegations and roll calls, the maps of Southeast Asia. Not the one-off elections (a special, a single primary day), nor the month's indicators. And each thread's statement in the thread index lists the tables and maps its entries carry on the page, in order, in a muted line set right above the See also line ("*Tables and maps* Election of Nov. 8, 1960 · Governors, Nov. 8, 1960"); in the reader, each part's index its part's.
- Each listed table or map is titled by its label, centered in small capitals ("ELECTIONS OF NOV. 8, 1960"), as the Executive roster's parts are (the owner's request, Oct. 23, 2026: "a distinct title style to visually demarcate them from the calendar day entries"; all-caps titles "must be unusually brief", so the labels are): STYLE 5 in `tools/bib/contents.py`. One title a table (the owner, Nov. 4, 2026: "Remove doubled titles"): the block's own heading gives way (DROP_HEAD; the Executive's, the roll calls' and Armed Forces Day's in their modules), but for the Southeast Asia table, whose indicators-style caption is its title. A title ending in a bare year drops its comma ("Democratic primaries 1960", "Armed forces 1962", "Southeast Asia 1966"); one ending in a month and year or a day keeps it ("Southeast Asia, Sept. 1966", "Executive Branch, Jan. 20, 1957", "State finance, fiscal 1960"); a bill's course is titled by its act alone. A November election day is one block, "Elections of Nov. 8, 1960" (`election_day` in `tools/bib/congress.py`): "Presidential electors", "Congress" (with the unexpired terms filled that day), "State elections" (the governors chosen that day, the legislatures it chose); the governors' and legislatures' entries point to their parts. The governors' tables shade a party's gain in the winner's color and a new governor gray, as the Congress's tables do (STYLE 8 in `tools/bib/governors.py`).
- Every table or map an inject sets in a calendar entry goes through `contents.put(page, eid, blk, kind, key)`: it puts the block at the end of the entry's `<li>` and its anchor (`tb-<kind>-<key>`) before it. A new type of table or map registers its kind in `KINDS` there, with its label ("Election of Nov. 8, 1960"), or `None` for one the contents leave out; `put` refuses an unregistered kind, and `contents.finish` (after the injects, in `build.py`) lists it in the contents and the threads with no more code. `check` warns on a tag whose label cannot be made [contents].
- A table set under a date, not in an entry (a day block, `tools/bib/dayblocks.py`: the Southeast Asia table and the year's map above its first, Armed Forces Day) takes its anchor from `contents.day_mark(kind, key, iso, threads)`, which names the day and the threads in the entry's place; the contents and the threads list it the same way. Listed: the first Southeast Asia table of each year on a page ("Southeast Asia, Jan. 1965", thread Vietnam, untitled: `titled=False`) and each Armed Forces Day ("Armed forces 1965", Defense and Vietnam); the other Southeast Asia tables, like the month's indicators, not.
