# State finance

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

`statefin/states.yaml`, written by `tools/statefin/make_statefin.py` from the Census Bureau's historical finance databases (the figures its annual *State Government Finances* printed; Access files read with mdbtools; the build needs no network; never edit): each State government, D.C.'s exhibit and the U.S. total by Census's fiscal year, 1959–68 (revenue, five taxes, federal aid, expenditure by character, aid to local governments, long-term debt, population, personal income); the State and its local governments together (`local`); the 43 large cities in the census years 1962 and 1967 (`cities`); and each year's printed report, its tables and first pages (`reports`, kept in the script). `tools/bib/statefin.py` renders them (STYLE notes there):

- A calendar entry tagged `statefin:<YYYY>` carries that fiscal year's block: a summary, a map of the taxes in force (general sales and individual income, either, neither; the year's adopters in the full color) and a quantile map of taxes per $1,000 of personal income, then, folded, the State governments' tables (taxes: per $1,000 and a head, five shares; revenue, expenditure and debt), the State and local governments together, and in 1962 and 1967 the large cities; the caveats (Census's classification, not the States' budgets; fiscal years differ); the database and the report cited by table and page. Now the nine "state-and-city-finance-us" entries of June 30, 1960–68 (thread "State and city finance").
- Taxes per $1,000 of personal income take the calendar year before, as Census's tables do, with BEA's revised figures as the database carries them; the regime follows Census's columns (any revenue from the tax). The acts are calendar entries of their own, dated by the session laws.
