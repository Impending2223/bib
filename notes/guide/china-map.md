# The map of China

The owner, Nov. 22, 2026: "We should probably give ourselves a China map. Like our Southeast Asia/Indochina map. The minimum viable product would give us the provinces, counties, places. Later we could extend the map with whatever other information is available and relevant to the threads we're following." Built Nov. 26, 2026, from the brief `notes/briefs/china-map.md`. The default taken on where the map is reached from, which the owner may overrule: `notes/owner-rulings.md`, 57.

`tools/bib/cnmap.py` (STYLE notes at the top): a map of China on a page of its own, `build/china.html`, showing every calendar entry whose `place:` is a place on the map: a dot a place, larger by the year's entries there, a tap listing them, each linked; a year selector (All years, 1960–69, and any other year with a placed entry), each year anchored (`china.html#y1966`); under the map, folded, the placed entries. The provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities as of 1966 (the default), or the borders alone, or land and water. Boxes: Names; Counties (the counties and cities of the end of 1964). Then the gazetteer as a table and the sources and licences (`tools/bib/china.py`, its STYLE notes). `./bib build china` builds the page alone.

- One client for every map. The map is drawn by the maps of Indochina's client, `tools/bib/vnmap.js` and `vnmap.css`, the same library and font (`sources/vendor/`), the same figure and classes. The two maps differ by data alone: the figure's own data (`map`, the key its choices are kept under; `mode`, the first kind of map; `eu`, the file of entries) and the manifest's `opt` (`shown`, the countries whose units are drawn; `dot`, the dots' scale; `unit`, the words after a unit's kind in a tap). Write no second client and no projection, gesture or label placement: the library does those. A change to `vnmap.js` or `vnmap.css` changes both maps: look at both pages after it.
- The build computes no geometry (CI has only pyyaml). It copies `sources/china/`'s files beside the pages under names that carry their hash (`build/geo/cn-admin-<hash>.geojson`, `cn-counties-<hash>.geojson`) and writes the manifest (`build/geo/cn-manifest-<hash>.json`) and the entries (`build/geo/cn-entries-<hash>.json`). The entries are in a file of their own because they are thousands: inline they would put the page over the page budget.
- The data, made once by `tools/cnmap/` and committed (each script's docstring names its downloads; they need shapely >= 2.1, pyproj, pyogrio, pypinyin, opencc-python-reimplemented): `counties.py` (the record of the 1964 counties and the crosswalk, `sources/china/crosswalk.json`), then `base.py` (one coverage: the countries, the provinces of 1966 and the counties of 1964, `admin.geojson`, `counties.geojson`, `units.json`, `counties.json`), then `places.py` (`places.json`). `sources/china/README.md` states every adjustment, source and licence, each kind of case among the counties, the counts, and the places not on the map. The scripts run in that order, and each reads the one before's output, so a change to `counties.py` means all three run again. The downloads are large (about 400 MB); `counties.py --fetch` gets the record's pages at a page a second.
- The boundary file misnames some units, and the record's changes are sometimes misread. Three tables in `counties.py` settle these by hand: `NAMED` (a unit named by its place, checked against Wikidata's point for the county), `PROVINCE` (a unit the CGAZ province file leaves out) and `BACK` (a walk back to 1964 settled from the record's own words). A county found wrong on the map is mended there, with its ground, and the scripts run again. Do not edit `counties.json` or the GeoJSON by hand.
- The places are the calendar's own gazetteer, `sources/places.yaml` (`tools/bib/places.py`): every row whose `in:` is `prc` or `roc`, and Hong Kong and Macau. The map keeps its own file, `sources/china/places.json`, keyed by the gazetteer's key: Wikidata's coordinates (never guessed; Wikipedia's for one place, `WIKI` in `places.py`, where Wikidata's point is wrong), the 1966 province and the 1964 county by point in polygon, and the rule the item was chosen by. A point within 5 km of the county of its own name takes that county. Do not edit `sources/places.yaml` for the map.
- A place added to the gazetteer later: `./bib check` warns that it is in China and not in `places.json` [places]. Run `python3 tools/cnmap/places.py CACHE_DIR` (a directory under /tmp, deleted after): it asks Wikidata for the new names only. A place the rules do not settle is left unplaced with the reason; settle it in the script's `HAND` table from the entries' own words, with the Wikidata item read from Wikidata, never typed from memory, or leave it unplaced. The rules can choose wrongly (a town of the name in another county, a river of the name): after a run, look at each new row's `wiki` and county against the entries' words.
- A province named as a place (`place: sichuan`) has no dot. Its entries are listed by a tap on the province (`AREA` in `places.py`).
- `check` fails on a map file missing or over 50 MB, on the files over 60 MB together, and on a row of `places.json` that names nothing (no such gazetteer key, 1966 unit or 1964 county) or has a point without its source. The vendored library's files are checked with the maps of Indochina's.
- Where the page is reached from: the front page's list (`tools/bib/build.py`, `templates/series.html`) and a line at the head of `indochina.html`; `china.html` has the same line to `indochina.html`. No mark in the top bar and no map inside the calendar's pages: both are the owner's to rule on (`notes/owner-rulings.md`, 57).
- Tests: `tests/unit/test_cnmap.py` (the figure and its data on the fixture, `check`'s rules, the record's reading and the walk back to 1964).
- To look at the page: `./bib build china`, then serve `build/` (`python3 -m http.server` will do: the map of China reads no tiles) and open `china.html`. After a change to `vnmap.js` or `vnmap.css`, build and open `indochina.html` too, served with range requests (`npx http-server build`).
