# The map of China

The owner, Nov. 22, 2026: "We should probably give ourselves a China map. Like our Southeast Asia/Indochina map. The minimum viable product would give us the provinces, counties, places. Later we could extend the map with whatever other information is available and relevant to the threads we're following." Built Nov. 26, 2026, from the brief `notes/briefs/china-map.md`. The layers were added the same day from the brief `notes/briefs/maps-3-overlays.md` (the owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Which of the SEA map overlays can we also use on our China map? Bring them over."): below, "The layers". The owner's ruling of Nov. 25, 2026 on where the map stands (`notes/owner-rulings.md`, 57) was built the same day from `notes/briefs/maps-2.md`: "Both. In each calendar under the day of the first China entry. 図 for both SEA and China. Put them on the relevant threads top, scoped to the thread's content and the page's range. Show the later units. Give Formosa and Free China their ROC subdivisions. Default each calendar page to its range. Could we have a span filter, with two text fields entered by default but modifiable by hand?"

`tools/bib/cnmap.py` (STYLE notes at the top): a map of China, showing every calendar entry whose `place:` is a place on the map and whose day falls within the map's span of dates: a dot a place, larger by the span's entries there, a tap listing them, each linked; under the map, folded, the placed entries. It stands in three kinds of place: on a page of its own, `build/china.html`, with a year selector (All years, 1960–69, and any other year with a placed entry), each year anchored (`china.html#y1966`); in each page of the calendar that holds a placed entry; and at the head of the pages of `china` and every thread under it. The span and its two fields, the calendar's pages, the threads' pages and the page of the two maps are the same for both maps: `notes/guide/indochina-maps.md`, from "The span" on. The provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities as of 1966 (the default), or the borders alone, or land and water. Boxes: Names; Counties (the counties and cities of the end of 1964; a unit not traced back to 1964 is shown as the later unit it is, and a tap says so: the owner, Nov. 25, 2026, "Show the later units"). Taiwan, the Pescadores, Kinmen and Matsu are drawn by the Republic of China's own units: its two provinces, Taiwan and Fujian, among the provinces, and with Counties its counties and cities of the 1960s. The island's one name stays "Taiwan". Then the layers' boxes, the maps of Indochina's own: Relief, Elevation, Contours, Rivers (with lakes), Climate, Rain (by month), Population (1960, 1970, modelled) and Cities (the UN *Demographic Yearbook*'s figures). Then the gazetteer as a table and the sources and licences (`tools/bib/china.py`, its STYLE notes). `./bib build china` builds the page alone.

- One client for every map. The map is drawn by the maps of Indochina's client, `tools/bib/vnmap.js` and `vnmap.css`, the same library and font (`sources/vendor/`), the same figure and classes. The two maps differ by data alone: the figure's own data (`map`, the key its choices are kept under; `mode`, the first kind of map; `eu`, the entries' files, one a year; `sp`, the span it opens on; `th`, a thread's entries alone) and the manifest's `opt` (`shown`, the countries whose units are drawn; `dot`, the dots' scale; `unit`, the words after a unit's kind in a tap). Write no second client and no projection, gesture or label placement: the library does those. A change to `vnmap.js` or `vnmap.css` changes both maps: look at both pages after it.
- The build computes no geometry (CI has only pyyaml). It copies `sources/china/`'s files beside the pages under names that carry their hash (`build/geo/cn-admin-<hash>.geojson`, `cn-counties-<hash>.geojson`) and writes the manifest (`build/geo/cn-manifest-<hash>.json`) and the entries, a file a year (`build/geo/cn-entries-<year>-<hash>.json`; `tools/bib/maps.py`, STYLE 1). The entries are in files of their own because they are thousands: inline they would put a page over the page budget. The client reads the files of the years the span touches.
- The data, made once by `tools/cnmap/` and committed (each script's docstring names its downloads; they need shapely >= 2.1, pyproj, pyogrio, pypinyin, opencc-python-reimplemented): `counties.py` (the record of the 1964 counties and the crosswalk, `sources/china/crosswalk.json`), then `base.py` (one coverage: the countries, the provinces of 1966 and the counties of 1964, and the Republic of China's units by `roc.py`'s table, `admin.geojson`, `counties.geojson`, `units.json`, `counties.json`), then `places.py` (`places.json`). `sources/china/README.md` states every adjustment, source and licence, each kind of case among the counties, the counts, and the places not on the map. The scripts run in that order, and each reads the one before's output, so a change to `counties.py` means all three run again. The downloads are large (about 330 MB); `counties.py --fetch` gets the record's pages at a page a second. `base.py` alone needs only the three boundary downloads and the committed `crosswalk.json`, not the record's pages: it was run again so on Nov. 26, 2026 for the Republic of China's units, and the PRC's data came out the same to the last digit.
- The boundary file misnames some units, and the record's changes are sometimes misread. Three tables in `counties.py` settle these by hand: `NAMED` (a unit named by its place, checked against Wikidata's point for the county), `PROVINCE` (a unit the CGAZ province file leaves out) and `BACK` (a walk back to 1964 settled from the record's own words). A county found wrong on the map is mended there, with its ground, and the scripts run again. Do not edit `counties.json` or the GeoJSON by hand.
- The places are the calendar's own gazetteer, `sources/places.yaml` (`tools/bib/places.py`): every row whose `in:` is `prc` or `roc`, and Hong Kong and Macau. The map keeps its own file, `sources/china/places.json`, keyed by the gazetteer's key: Wikidata's coordinates (never guessed; Wikipedia's for one place, `WIKI` in `places.py`, where Wikidata's point is wrong), the 1966 province and the 1964 county by point in polygon, and the rule the item was chosen by. A point within 5 km of the county of its own name takes that county. Do not edit `sources/places.yaml` for the map. The one exception was made on the owner's word (Nov. 25, 2026: "Add the cities to the gazetteer"): the 34 cities of the UN *Demographic Yearbook* that no entry names were given rows in `sources/places.yaml` on Nov. 26, 2026. `places.py` holds each one's Wikidata item (`CITY`), so a later run makes their rows again.
- The Republic of China's units (the owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Give Formosa and Free China their ROC subdivisions"): `tools/cnmap/roc.py` holds the whole table, each unit of the 1960s with the townships and districts of today's file it is made of, and its docstring names the download. A unit found wrong is mended in that table, and `base.py` run again, then `python3 -I tools/cnmap/places.py --roc`, which gives the places in the Republic their unit and county again by point in polygon and reads nothing from Wikidata. `sources/china/README.md` states each adjustment.
- A place added to the gazetteer later: `./bib check` warns that it is in China and not in `places.json` [places]. Run `python3 tools/cnmap/places.py CACHE_DIR` (a directory under /tmp, deleted after): it asks Wikidata for the new names only. A place the rules do not settle is left unplaced with the reason; settle it in the script's `HAND` table from the entries' own words, with the Wikidata item read from Wikidata, never typed from memory, or leave it unplaced. The rules can choose wrongly (a town of the name in another county, a river of the name): after a run, look at each new row's `wiki` and county against the entries' words.
- A province named as a place (`place: sichuan`) has no dot. Its entries are listed by a tap on the province (`AREA` in `places.py`).
- `check` fails on a map file missing or over 50 MB, on the files over 60 MB together (the layers' files count, as Indochina's do), on a row of `places.json` that names nothing (no such gazetteer key, 1966 unit or 1964 county) or has a point without its source, and on a city of `cities.yaml` without a place or a point of its own, or a figure without its Yearbook and page. The vendored library's files are checked with the maps of Indochina's.
- Where the page is reached from: the top bar's 図, by way of `maps.html`, which lists the two maps; the front page's list (`tools/bib/build.py`, `templates/series.html`); a line at the head of `indochina.html` (`china.html` has the same line to `indochina.html`); and the muted line under each map of China in the calendar and on a thread's page.
- Tests: `tests/unit/test_cnmap.py` (the figure and its data on the fixture, `check`'s rules, the layers' boxes, legend, files and cities, the layer scripts' numbers, the record's reading and the walk back to 1964) and `tests/unit/test_maps.py` (the span, the map in the calendar and on a thread's page, the Republic of China's table).
- To look at the page: `./bib build china`, then serve `build/` with range requests (`npx http-server build`; `python3 -m http.server` does not answer them, and the relief's and the climate's tiles need them) and open `china.html`. After a change to `vnmap.js` or `vnmap.css`, build and open `indochina.html` too.

## The layers

The owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Which of the SEA map overlays can we also use on our China map? Bring them over." `cnmap.py`, STYLE 7.

- The map of China has every layer the maps of Indochina have, in the same boxes, in the same order, with the same looks and the same legend parts. The corps zones' and the regions' tints are the one exception: they have no like here, and none is to be made (the owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Don't tint by regional bureaus or military region").
- One code for both maps. The boxes are `vnmap.bar`'s, the legend's layer parts `vnmap.layer_key`'s, the legends' data `vnmap._legends`'s, the cities' points `vnmap.cities`'s, and `check`'s rule for the cities `vnmap.city_problems`'s. `cnmap.py` gives them its own directory, its files (`FILES`, under `vnmap.FILES`' keys), its credits and the few words of the legend that state the data's grain (`WORDS`). Write no second copy of any of these. The client has no line for China's layers: it draws what the manifest names.
- A layer whose file is missing has no box, no legend part and no line in the page's text. So the map builds with any of the files.
- Loading. Nothing of a layer is fetched before its box is ticked. The GeoJSON layers and the cities start as empty sources and take their file in the client's `load`. The tiles are read by the library when their layer is first shown. This holds wherever the map stands: its page, the calendar's pages, the threads' pages.
- The data is made once by the scripts that made Indochina's, `tools/vnmap/terrain.py`, `climate.py`, `population.py` and `layers.py`, each run with the word `china` (their docstrings give the command and the downloads). Every number that differs between the two maps is in `tools/vnmap/frames.py`: the frame, the tiles' deepest zoom, the lakes raised to their surface, the contours' levels, the bands, the grids, the rivers' and lakes' least size, the deltas' rings, the simplifying. Indochina's numbers there are the ones its files were made with. Do not copy a script for a third map: add a frame.
- The detail. China's layers are about four times as coarse as Indochina's, since the map opens about four times as far out. `sources/china/README.md`, "The choice of detail", gives each choice and its reason, and each file's bytes. The layers are 44.9 MB together and the largest file, `dem.pmtiles`, 30.8 MB. The elevation tiles go to zoom 6 (the owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Make the relief sharp!"). The first build stopped at zoom 5 to keep the file under 20 MB. Zoom 6 shows about all the 30″ grid holds, and zoom 7 would put the file over `check`'s limit. The map's files are now 50.4 million bytes, and `check` allows 62.9 million (60 MB at 1,048,576 bytes each). So a new layer of any size needs one of them made smaller first.
- The order of the scripts for the rain: `layers.py DL sources/china china climate` writes `climate.json`, then `climate.py KOPPEN_TIF LEGEND_TXT china` writes the climate's tiles and `climate.geojson` from it (`climate.py grid china` writes the squares alone).
- The cities: `sources/china/cities.yaml`, kept by hand, in the form of Indochina's file. Every city is at its gazetteer place (`place:`), and the file holds no point of its own. A city added later goes into the gazetteer first, with its Wikidata item in `places.py` (`CITY`), read from Wikidata, never typed from memory. Then `places.py` is run, and the city's row here names the key. `as:` is the Yearbook's own name for the city, and a tap shows it. Figures printed for a city that could not be placed are kept under `unplaced:` ("Malibo", the Yangmingshan Administration). Each row has its date, its Yearbook, table and page. Three readings of the Yearbook's names carry a "Check:" in the file's head.
- The deltas (the owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Draw the river deltas"). In seven deltas the rivers are SWORD's channels, not HydroRIVERS's one line, as in the Mekong's delta on the maps of Indochina: the Pearl River's, the Han's at Shantou, the Yangtze's, the Yellow River's, the Red River's, the Irrawaddy's and the Ganges's and Brahmaputra's. Each has a ring in `frames.py` (`delta`), drawn by hand so that it crosses each river where the two sources' lines lie together. `delta_min` is the narrowest channel drawn (50 m), `delta_to_sea` the rivers whose name is carried to the sea by the widest branch. To add a delta, add a ring there and run `layers.py DL sources/china china hydro` again. Then look at the ring's edge on the map: a line that stops short there means the ring crosses where the sources differ. `sources/china/README.md`, "Rivers", gives each ring, the names and the faults.
- The page's text names the layers and their sources (`china.py`), and carries HydroSHEDS's statement in full, as `indochina.html` does (`indochina.HYDROSHEDS`).
- What differs from the maps of Indochina in looks, and why. The elevation has ten bands, not seven, and the contours run to 5,000 m: Tibet. The cities' circles are at half Indochina's scale (`cnmap.CITY`, the manifest's `opt.city`; the client's own scale stays the default): China's cities are larger and closer, and at half the scale Shanghai's circle is the size Saigon's is there. The legend's words on the relief's grid, the contours, the rain's cells and the cities' years are the map's own (`WORDS`). Nothing else differs.
- Two changes made with the layers touch both maps. The cities' file is now fetched when the Cities box is first ticked, not with the map. In the legend, a swatch's rule is now `.vnm-key s>i`, not `.vnm-key i`: the old rule also caught the italics of *Demographic Yearbook* in the Cities line and set the title one letter a line, on the maps of Indochina too.
- The tiles' first 16 KB (a PMTiles file's header and directory) are read when the map starts, as on the maps of Indochina. The tiles themselves are read when their layer is first shown.
- Lakes in the relief (the owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Mend Lake Baikal."). The model holds the floor of Lake Baikal, not its surface, and the lake showed as a basin. `terrain.py` now raises the ground under such a lake to the lake's surface, by HydroLAKES's height, before it makes the tiles and the contours (`fill_lakes`; `lake_fill` in `frames.py`; the README, "Relief", gives the rule and the count). So the script needs HydroLAKES's `.shp` on its command line for China. Baikal is the one lake it changed. The step is off for the maps of Indochina, where no lake meets the rule.
- Known faults are in the README: Baikal stands at HydroLAKES's 449 m, not the 456 m usually given; the deltas are today's channels, and the Yellow River's mouth is today's, not that of the 1960s; six more deltas in the frame are not drawn from SWORD; the Red River has no name in its delta; the Yarlung Tsangpo has no name; the relief is still soft at the map's deepest zoom, where a zoom 6 tile is drawn about seven times its size.
