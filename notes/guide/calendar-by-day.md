# The calendar by day

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

Each dated section of the calendar (a month) is laid out by day, in both calendar pages and the series reader alike: the month's undated entries, then every day from the section's first to its last. A day shows its date, "*New York Times*" under it (a TimesMachine link by date only; "No *New York Times* (strike)" from Dec. 8, 1962 to Mar. 31, 1963), the entries that begin that day, each under its thread as a rubric (a line of muted small capitals above the entry, each thread's name linked, in the rubric's own color, to its entry in the thread index at the top; the date added for a range or a month-only entry), under an address that sets several threads going (`to:`, `short:`) a "To:" line naming each thread and the entry it leads to, and under each such entry a "From:" line back to the address (`program_links`), then a line a thread linking the entries before and after it in the thread ("⟨⟨ Testing ⟩⟩", the brackets the links, not underlined, the date on hover; its `also` threads too; `thread_nav` in `tools/bib/build.py`), a "Continuing" pointer under the date on each later day of a ranged entry, and the day's materials in two folded blocks, each closed by default and shown only where it holds something (the owner's request, Oct. 22, 2026): "Documents" (APP, FRUS, the opinions, the recordings) and "Other records" (the committees' meetings, the *Bulletin*, and any list added later), each summary counting its own lists; "Expand all" and "Collapse all" under each month heading open or close both (STYLE 12 in `tools/bib/daybook.py`). The Sunday programs and the *New York Times* line are not daily materials: they stay under the date, in neither block. Above each Sunday, a heading a level under the month's, the MMWR week ("Week 12"; Sunday to Saturday, week 1 the week of Jan. 4; its year added on a Sunday of late December whose week is the next year's), the week's span on hover; the Sunday wash of Nov. 4 taken off (the owner's ruling, Nov. 5, 2026; STYLE 16 in `tools/bib/daybook.py`, `mmwr_week`). A day block (the Southeast Asia table on the first Monday, the forces on Armed Forces Day: `tools/bib/dayblocks.py`) stands after the day's entries and before its folded blocks: `section_days` sets a placeholder (`dayblocks.marker`), filled after the indicators by `dayblocks.inject` in both build chains.

The two blocks are filled when opened (`tools/bib/lazydays.py`, its docstring; daybook STYLE 14): the build keeps each day's closed summaries in the page and writes the blocks' contents to a file a month beside it, `build/days/<page>-<YYYY-MM>-<hash>.json` (named by its content's hash, so a cache never serves an old month; a page's stale months removed at each build), so a calendar page sheds some two thirds of its weight (the second of the three parts of Oct. 2026, 18.8 to 6.6 MB). The first opening of a block, by a tap, Expand all (the months from the button's on, two at a time; in the reader the list's own blocks), or a link to an anchor inside one (the page's `lzmap` names the file that holds each; the reader's `jump` asks `window.lzGo`, and opens the folded blocks around its target, as `list.html`'s `openTo` does), fetches the month once and fills all its blocks; "loading" after the summary meanwhile. `lazydays.split` runs on the finished page, after the injects and the routing, and curls the contents as `smart.py` curls the page; `./bib links` reads each page with its month files. Opened as files (not through a server), the blocks stay empty.

The day's order (the owner's rulings, Oct. 29, 2026: "Alphabetic sort, first by the name of the entry's (primary) thread, then by the text of the entry"; Nov. 5, 2026: "Yes, add the ranges and sort by time first"): at build, a day's entries by their place in the day (a `time`, or the middle of a `time_range`, in UTC), then by the primary thread's key in the thread index (th.yaml's `sort:`; a subthread under its parent), then `c` as plain text, both folded; the entries with neither time nor range after the placed ones, by thread and text; the month's undated block by thread and text alone. No file is reordered, so where an entry sits in its month's file no longer matters. Optional, hidden: `follows: [<id>]` (the entry follows those named, the same day; a topological sort, the key breaking ties; its basis in the note), `time:` (a UTC instant, '1961-10-17T00:30Z', the local time turned by the place's IANA zone; not yet shown), `time_approx: true` (the source's time is loose: "about 7"), and `time_range: [<from>, <to>]` (the span of the day the record allows where it gives no hour: the chamber's sitting, "that evening", a clock in doubt an hour wide; its basis in the note; never beside a `time`), with `time_band:` and `time_zone:` naming the band it was drawn from ("evening", "workday afternoon", in the place's zone: BANDS, `band_range`), and `range_basis: kind` where no record gives the span and the event's kind places it (an office act or paper, the workday daytime of its place; a dinner, the evening; a newspaper's story or column, the early morning where it was published; an election, election night in the constituency, from the polls' closing by law where found: KINDS_PLACE; the owner, Nov. 5, 2026: "where their contents or placement suggests so"; Nov. 6: "Newspapers are early morning events in the location where published. Elections are late evening or night events in the constituency where they're held"), nothing added to the note, giving way to any record found later; never a made-up hour. An unplaced entry that a placed one follows sorts at the earliest such place, the link putting it first (a meeting with no hour before the 8 p.m. address it decided). An election's kind range (the band "election night") sorts at 11:59 p.m. of its date in its `time_zone`, not at the range's middle (the pin; the owner, Nov. 11, 2026: "keep the range ... But leave the pin one minute before midnight"), until a record of when the results were known (a `time` or a record range) replaces it. `follows` trumps a kind place (the owner, the same evening: "That should trump the range time, right?"): a follower placed by its kind sorts no earlier than what it follows, a leader placed by its kind no later than a follower placed by a record; check's follows test compares record ranges and times only. Optional, shown: `polls_close:` on an election's entry, one line, "Polls close 6 p.m. CST: <basis>." (the owner, Nov. 11, 2026: "report the poll closing. 'Polls close XYZ.'"), the closing hour by law from `sources/poll-hours.yaml` where its rows give it for every State of the entry (the law's clock where it names one, else the State's civil time that day, "local time" for a State of two zones or a clock in doubt; a span of hours, or several States, the earliest and latest), its basis the register's sources, the compilation's page linked; nothing where the register gives no hour; not in `n`, and it places nothing (dayorder STYLE 7). A chamber's hours, where a note gives them, in one form: "The Senate sat from noon to 5:26 p.m.: [Daily Digest, D3](…), [D21](…).", the meeting hour's page first, a special order's start named ("from 10 a.m. (its order of June 12)"); `tools/times/digest_sentences.py` (rerunnable) puts them so. `python3 -I tools/times/day_order.py YEAR [--from YYYY-MM] [--until YYYY-MM]` prints each day as the build orders it, for a reader of the order (round 4 of `notes/briefs/times.md`). STYLE and check [day-order]: `tools/bib/dayorder.py`.

```
tools/daybook/make_daybook.py   writes daybook/<YYYY-MM>.yaml (needs the network; the build does not)
tools/daybook/make_recordings.py  writes daybook/recordings/<YYYY-MM>.yaml from PRDE's catalogue (needs the network)
tools/daybook/make_sunday.py      writes daybook/sunday.yaml from the Library's inventory, the TV listings, and the
                                  Face the Nation index (--ftn PATH; without it, its rows already written are kept)
tools/daybook/fetch_daily_diary.py  for the owner to run on his own computer: Johnson's Daily Diary from the LBJ Library,
                                  a PDF a day, the busiest days first, politely; stops at a challenge (its docstring)
tools/daybook/make_committees.py  writes daybook/committees/<YYYY-MM>.yaml from the bound Record's Daily Digest
                                  (--cache DIR; downloads each year's part, ~200 MB, reads it, deletes it)
tools/bib/daybook.py            lays the calendar out by day (STYLE notes at the top) and checks the data
tools/bib/committees.py         the committees' list and "Testimony before Congress" (STYLE notes) and checks the data
```

- The President: APP's documents by date (Public Papers items, executive orders, proclamations), in APP's order; each title linked to the item of the Public Papers that prints it, in GPO's volume on govinfo, its cite in grey ("1961 Pub. Papers 1") and " (APP)" after it, linked to the row's APP document, and to APP alone, marked "APP", only where none does (below, "The Public Papers"; STYLE 13 in `tools/bib/daybook.py`).
- FRUS: every volume of the 1958–60, 1961–63 and 1964–68 subseries (the last for Nov. 1963 on), microfiche supplements included, from the TEI files at github.com/HistoryAtState/frus; dated by `frus:doc-dateTime-min`. Editorial notes carry only their volume's span, so each is filed under the document before it in its volume. Within a day, by volume and number.
- Authors are persons where the sources name one, read through each FRUS volume's list of persons (the `corresp` links on names): the heading's sender ("Director of Central Intelligence (Dulles)"), else, where the heading names an office ("Mission at Berlin"), the signer (Lightner), else the drafter of a memorandum of conversation. Initials and first-name signatures ("LLC", "Bob K.") count only through a link. Where FRUS's link is wrong, the heading's office words (Attorney General → Robert F. Kennedy) or a signature naming another listed namesake (John S. D. Eisenhower) correct it. Intelligence estimates go to the board their note says concurred (U.S. Intelligence Board). A person shows by surname ("Rusk") unless the lists or the Presidents hold another by that name; then by full name ("McGeorge Bundy", "John F. Kennedy"). An office shows where nobody signed. Rules and order: "authors as persons" in `tools/daybook/make_daybook.py`. Titles are the sources' own; nothing is read or summarized by the generator.
- FRUS citations carry the volume's title: "FRUS 1961–63, XXIV: Laos Crisis, doc. 4".
- Sunday programs: a muted line under a Sunday's date, "*Meet the Press*: Hubert H. Humphrey", each guest linked to his name entry, the network and the source on hover. The record: the Library of Congress's inventory of Spivak's photographs of each Meet the Press television program (Prints and Photographs, pp020019, LOT 13025), by show date, its guests written as Part III writes names, 1945–80 (radio to 1950); the finding aid is in `sources/loc/`. The Classic TV Archive's episode guides (from the TV listings, 1947–Aug. 25, 1963) fill a Sunday the inventory lacks; where they name another person, the line carries "Listings: … Check." *Face the Nation* (CBS), Nov. 1959–1970: the index volume of *Face the Nation: The Collected Transcripts* (Holt, 1972), its chronological list: the date and the guests' names only (its topics and offices are CBS's text); the copy read is the owner's, not kept in the repo. *Issues and Answers* (ABC) is not yet in. A day's programs share one line, Face the Nation first, a semicolon between, no closing stop ("*Face the Nation*: John F. Kennedy; *Meet the Press*: Hubert H. Humphrey"). Each name entry lists the person's programs ("Television interviews"), and under the list, on one line, a sentence a program for its source ("*Face the Nation* interviews from *Face the Nation, 1954–1970: Index* (1972)."), not a cite on each item. An office the index gives a guest goes into the Life only where asked (`FTN_ROLES` in `make_sunday.py`: Percy, 1960), cited to the index by page. Guests and recording speakers are matched to persons by `person_named` in `tools/bib/lives.py`.
- Recordings: the White House tapes, Kennedy's from July 1962 and Johnson's, a third list after APP and FRUS. Two catalogues: the Presidential Recordings Digital Edition's (PRDE: titles and speakers; its transcripts by subscription) and the Miller Center's Secret White House Tapes (free: a page and the audio for each; the LBJ Library's tape and citation number), written by `tools/daybook/make_millercenter.py` from the site's public JSON:API. A PRDE row shows PRDE's title linked to the free page, the time, the tape cite ("Conversation WH6407-11-4288") and "PRDE" linked to the Edition's page ("PRDE (PRDE only)" where the Miller Center has no copy); a recording only the Miller Center gives, its title in title case. The same recording (`free` in `tools/bib/daybook.py`, STYLE 8): Johnson's by citation number; else the day, the time within twenty minutes and a name in both titles; else the day and the same title (Kennedy's); else Kennedy's tape and item. 93 percent of PRDE's rows match; machine noise and PRDE's daily introductions are left out. The catalogue only: the transcripts and the editors' introductions are the Edition's text, which its site shows to registered users only (a 48-hour trial, or purchase); its search API returns them too, in its full results (found Nov. 1, 2026), and the script keeps the catalogue fields alone; they are not taken or paraphrased; what was said comes from published transcripts (Beschloss, *Taking Charge*, *Reaching for Glory*) and the studies. As a calendar entry's primary record, the free page: `[Johnson and Robert Kennedy, 12:25 p.m.](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/ secret-white-house-tapes/…), Conversation WH6407-11-4288 (Miller Center)`; PRDE's page only where the Miller Center has no copy, cited "(PRDE only)".
- The printed record (`tools/bib/printed.py`, STYLE notes; daybook STYLE 9 and 11): under each FRUS document, where its own text was printed then ("Printed: *Bulletin*, Jan. 15, 1962, p. 93") and where the texts its notes cite were ("In the notes: …"), read from FRUS's notes by `tools/daybook/make_printed.py` (1,392 documents; the *Bulletin*'s pages linked to the Boston Public Library's copies on archive.org, a quarter a scan, by printed page; *Current Documents* unlinked, no open copy found; a date no issue bears, or a page outside its issue, "Check"). The *Bulletin* as a fifth list, "Bulletin": each issue's own index, item by item, filed by the release date its page prints, else the issue's (`tools/daybook/make_bulletin.py YEAR`: 1960 from Jan. 6 to 1969 to Jan. 20, 7,071 items; its docstring logs the rules, RULES 1-10, and the accuracy read against the page images: 94 percent of the items found, a title in six with an OCR misreading; `check` holds the rows to the span and their issues, `tools/bib/bulletin.py`). The issues and the copies' pages: `tools/daybook/make_dsb_index.py` (vol. 59, July–Dec. 1968, has no open copy: HathiTrust's are full view but bot-checked here; `notes/leads.md`). A calendar entry on a private document FRUS shows printed later carries "Made public: [[w.department-of-state-bulletin]], Nov. 19, 1973, 640–645." (`printed.made_public`).
- Committees: the Daily Digest's "Committee Meetings" (the Senate's, the House's, the joint committees' and the conferees'), a fourth list after the recordings, one line a meeting: "Senate Foreign Relations, Subcommittee on American Republics Affairs: PANAMA, executive session; George W. Ball", the Digest's subject heading as printed in small capitals, the measures, the witnesses by name as printed (office on hover), the Digest's page linked to the PDF's page, and the day hearings continue. The source: the bound *Congressional Record*'s Daily Digest on govinfo, one part a year (1960 pt16, 1961 pt18, 1962 pt19, 1963 pt21, 1964 pt20, 1965 pt23, 1966 pt23, 1967 pt29, 1968 pt26, 1969 pt32: `VOLS`), a scan with an OCR text layer, read word by word with its box (`pdftotext -bbox`) and laid out column by column; the rows keep the paragraph as printed (`text`), and the PDF's pages against the printed folios are in `daybook/committees/volumes.yaml`. Committees by the Directory's names; a standing committee's `code` is Garrison Nelson's (`congress/committees.yaml`). A witness is linked only where one person fits by surname and office (a Member by the chamber and the day; an officer by the Executive roster on the day; anyone else by name and a telling word of his office): `witness_person`, STYLE 2 in `tools/bib/committees.py`. Checked by eye against January 1964 (203 meetings, 359 witnesses): the misses left are the OCR's (a name or a stop misread). `check` fails on a row out of the calendar's span, without its page or link, or with a "continue" day not after it. A meeting held on a day the chamber did not sit is reported in the next day's Digest ("On Friday, June 3, subcommittee concluded ..."): it is filed under the day it was held, the Digest's day kept (`digest`). The day headings, the chambers' headings and the "Committee Meetings" heading the OCR loses or misreads are read by rule (STYLE 1-2 in `tools/daybook/make_committees.py`: a heading run together, "Tuesday.June14, 1966"; a chamber's heading within one column; a day whose heading is lost, dated by the page's running head); the gaps of six days and more that are left are recesses (Easter 1968, Labor Day 1966, Lincoln's Birthday 1967). The committee is linked to its table on `congress.html` in the Congress of the day, the subcommittee to its row where that Congress's list holds it, and a bill whose course `congress/bills.yaml` gives to the entry that carries it (STYLE 4 in `tools/bib/committees.py`); a subcommittee's name the OCR cut short is put whole where one name settles it (`settle_subs`: the Staff Directory's list, else the Digest's own longer forms), the name as read kept (`sub_printed`).
- The President's Daily Diary (Johnson's; Oct. 23, 2026): `daybook/diary.yaml`, written by `tools/daybook/make_daily_diary.py` from the National Archives Catalog, which gives the Diary's page images for 50 days only (Nov. 22-30, 1963; Tonkin Gulf, Selma, the signings, the trips, Glassboro, Apr. 4-7, 1968): a row a day, its item's address, its page count and the Catalog's title, nothing of the text. The whole Diary is on discoverlbj.org (`item/pdd-YYYYMMDD`), which refuses this environment and whose robots.txt (Cloudflare's managed file) bars the AI crawlers by name, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai and Claude-Web among them, and leaves other agents to its general rule (allowed: search=yes, ai-train=no, use=reference): not read. As a calendar entry's primary record, after the document of the act and before the bibliography: "[President's Daily Diary, Aug. 4, 1964](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/192446), images 3, 5 (LBJ Library; National Archives Catalog)", an image the page's number in the Catalog's item; where the Diary disagrees, "Check: the Daily Diary gives ..." and a line in `notes/diary-discrepancies.md` (the owner's request, Oct. 23, 2026). Not yet a line in the day layout. `check` [diary]: STYLE and checks in `tools/bib/diary.py`.
- Opinions (the owner's request, Oct. 23, 2026: the opinions of the Attorney General and OLC "logged with the documents in the calendar"; 1960–69, Oct. 24): `daybook/opinions.yaml`, kept by hand (its header the schema), a list after FRUS in the Documents block, rendered by `tools/bib/opinions.py` (its STYLE notes; check [opinions]): every opinion of the *Official Opinions of the Attorneys General*, vols. 41–42, of 1960–69 (44; Google's scans on archive.org, item `op-atty-gen`, by leaf), the Office of Legal Counsel's of the period in its *Supplemental Opinions*, vol. 1 (2013; the Office's PDF on justice.gov, by PDF page), and the Department's legal memoranda FRUS prints; each the signer, the title linked to the copy read, the cite ("42 Op. Att'y Gen. 69", "1 Op. O.L.C. Supp. 195"), the addressee and the holding in one line. Nothing from memory: an opinion another document names and no copy read is under `known`, with its Check, and not shown. In the calendar (`notes/extensions/memos/ag-olc-opinions.md`): an opinion that shaped or was the decision is an entry of its own, dated to the opinion, the opinion its primary record ("[42 Op. Att'y Gen. 69](…) (1961)"); one that speaks to an entry is a pointer in its note ("Opinion: [42 Op. Att'y Gen. 229](…) (1963), Robert Kennedy to Rusk, Oct. 9: …"); the rest stay in the day's list.
- Abstracts go in `daybook/abstracts.yaml` (`key: sentence`), never in the generated files, so a rerun keeps them. Keys: `app:<slug>`, `frus:<volume>/<dN>`. `check` fails on an abstract whose key is not in the daybook.
