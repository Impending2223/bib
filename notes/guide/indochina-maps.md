# Maps of Indochina

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides. Changed Nov. 26, 2026, for the owner's ruling of Nov. 25 (`notes/owner-rulings.md`, 57): the span and its two fields, the map in every calendar page and on the threads' pages, the one mark in the top bar. The sections from "The span" on are new, and they hold for the map of China too.

`tools/bib/vnmap.py` (STYLE notes at the top): a map of Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia, showing every calendar entry tagged with a place whose day falls within the map's span of dates: a dot a place, larger by the span's entries there, a tap listing them, each linked; under the map, folded, the span's placed entries; names placed by the library's collision, the span's places first, then the gazetteer's rank. It stands in three kinds of place: on a page of its own, every year (`build/indochina.html`); in each page of the calendar that holds a placed entry; and at the head of the pages of the threads of Vietnam and Laos (below, "The span" and the two sections after it). Until Nov. 26, 2026 the calendar held a map a year, 1960–68, on the year's first Monday (the owner, Nov. 4 and 11, 2026). The provinces as of late 1966, tinted by corps zone (the default), today's region, or plain; or the borders alone; or land and water. Boxes: Names; North Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia (their provinces, today's); Relief, Elevation, Contours, Rivers, Climate, Rain (by month), Population (1960, 1970: modelled, later) and Cities (the UN *Demographic Yearbook*'s figures).

- Drawn by MapLibre GL JS (the owner's spouse, Oct. 22, 2026: "Use libraries and don't reinvent the wheel"), vendored in `sources/vendor/` at a pinned version (its README: versions, licenses, how to update) and copied to `build/vnmap/`; no CDN. One client for every place either map stands, `tools/bib/vnmap.js` and `vnmap.css` (copied hashed to `build/vnmap/`, one `<script type="module">` a page), which loads the library only when a map nears the screen. Its gestures, label collision, hillshade (`raster-dem`, `hillshade`) and colour relief (`color-relief`) are the library's: write no projection, gesture or label placement of our own. The build computes no geometry (CI has only pyyaml): it copies the files and writes a manifest (`build/geo/vn-manifest-<hash>.json`).
- The data, made once by `tools/vnmap/` (each script's docstring names its downloads; they need shapely >= 2.1, pyproj, pyogrio, rasterio, mercantile, contourpy, pmtiles): one border geometry for everything, `sources/indochina/admin.geojson`, from geoBoundaries CGAZ (`base.py`: the countries, Vietnam's districts, Laos's and Cambodia's provinces as one coverage, simplified with shared edges, so neighbours meet; the 1966 units dissolved from the districts; each line drawn once at its rank). Rasters as PMTiles (`dem.pmtiles`, `koppen.pmtiles`; `tiles.py`), vectors as GeoJSON. Serving PMTiles needs HTTP range requests: GitHub Pages answers them; locally use `npx http-server build`, not `python3 -m http.server`. `check` fails on a map file over 50 MB, the map's files over 60 MB together, or a vendored file missing.
- Tags, hidden: `place:<id>`, a place in `sources/vn-provinces-1966/places.json` (the gazetteer: name with its diacritics, plain form, kind, Wikipedia's coordinates, 1966 province, country, rank for names); `province:<id>`, a 1966 unit, where an entry names a province and no finer place. An event in Indochina or off its coast takes them; a decision about it made in Washington, Honolulu or Manila does not. `check` fails on a tag that names nothing.
- A place not in the gazetteer: add it, with Wikipedia's coordinates (never guessed) and its province by point in polygon. The lines are approximate (the README there); the gazetteer's notes are working notes, not the series'.
- A city's population (`sources/indochina/cities.yaml`, kept by hand): each figure as the Yearbook prints it, with its table and page; `check` fails on a row without them.
- The map on a page of its own (the owner's request, Oct. 22, 2026): `build/indochina.html`, reached from the top bar's 図 by way of `maps.html` (below), the reader's Pages and its front page; `./bib build indochina` alone. The same figure and script (vnmap STYLE 6), as tall as the screen, every placed entry, with a year selector (All years, 1960–68), each year anchored (`indochina.html#y1965`), and the span's two fields, which a year's button fills; then the gazetteer as a table (place, plain form, kind, 1966 province, corps zone, the entries tagged there by year, each row anchored `pl-<id>`) and the sources and licenses, HydroSHEDS's statement in full, the software's licenses (`tools/bib/indochina.py`, its STYLE notes). Each map in the calendar and on a thread's page closes with a muted line to it.

## The span (both maps)

The owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Default each calendar page to its range. Could we have a span filter, with two text fields entered by default but modifiable by hand?" The code: `tools/bib/maps.py` (STYLE 1 and 2) and the client, `tools/bib/vnmap.js`.

- Every figure of either map has two text fields, From and to, in a bar of their own above the map's controls. They are plain text fields. There is no date picker and no slider.
- The build fills them with the page's own range of dates, in the calendar's form: "Apr. 1, 1961" and "June 30, 1961". On a calendar page the range is the page's (`calsplit.part_range`). On a thread's page divided by date it is the part's. On a thread's page not divided, and on a map's own page, it is the calendar's whole span.
- The map, a tap's list, the folded list and the count show the entries whose day falls within the span, both ends in. An entry's day is its place in the calendar (`loose.place_iso`): its first day where it runs several; a month's first day for an entry dated by month, or the day its `at:` gives; Jan. 1 for an entry dated by year. A day before the calendar's first day is taken as that first day, so "1960" and "January 1960" stand within the first page's range.
- What a field reads. A plain ISO date is always read: "1961-04-15". The calendar's own form is read: "Apr. 15, 1961", with the month in full or cut to three letters or more, with or without its point and its comma, in any case. The day may come first: "15 Apr. 1961". A month alone ("1961-04", "Apr. 1961") or a year alone ("1961") reads as its first day in the first field and its last day in the second.
- What a field does not read. A date in figures alone ("4/15/1961") is not read, since the month and the day cannot be told apart. A day the month lacks is not read ("Feb. 30, 1961"). A date half typed is not read ("Apr. 15", "196").
- What does not read changes nothing: the map stays on the last span that read, and the field keeps what was typed. The span is taken at each keystroke, as soon as both fields read. When the reader leaves a field (Tab, Enter, a click elsewhere) a date that reads is written out in the calendar's form. A first day after the last day shows no entry.
- On a map's own page the year buttons stay. A year's button fills the fields with that year's first and last day. "All years" fills them with the calendar's whole span. A span typed by hand that is no year's leaves no button marked.
- The entries are not in the page. Each map's placed entries go beside the pages a file a year (`build/geo/vn-entries-<year>-<hash>.json`, `cn-entries-<year>-<hash>.json`), and the client reads the files of the years the span touches when the map nears the screen. So a figure adds a few kilobytes to its page, whatever it shows. A wider span typed by hand reads the other years' files then.

## In the calendar's pages (both maps)

The owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Both. In each calendar under the day of the first China entry." `maps.py`, STYLE 3.

- Each page of the calendar that holds an entry placed on a map carries that map. It stands under the day of the page's first such entry that stands on a day, at the day block's place: after the day's entries and above the day's other blocks (`tools/bib/dayblocks.py`, STYLE 1). Where no placed entry of the page stands on a day, it is set at the end of the page's first placed entry.
- The map of Indochina stands above the map of China where both fall on one day.
- Each is titled as a listed table is, by the page's span, and listed in the page's contents and in its thread's "Tables and maps": "Southeast Asia, Jan.–Mar. 1966", "China, Jan.–Mar. 1966"; a whole year without the comma. Its anchor is `tb-vnmap-<first day>-<last day>` or `tb-cnmap-…`. The contents' kinds are `vnmap` and `cnmap` (`tools/bib/contents.py`, `KINDS`).
- In the reader (`series.html`) each part's block carries its maps as its page does.
- A map needs its data. Where the gazetteer, the borders or the vendored library is missing, as in the tests' fixture, no map is set and no entries' file is written (`ready()` in each map's module).

## On the threads' pages (both maps)

The owner, Nov. 25, 2026: "Put them on the relevant threads top, scoped to the thread's content and the page's range." `maps.py`, STYLE 4; `tools/bib/threads.py`, STYLE 5.

- A thread's page carries its map at the head, under the statement and the parts' bar and above the entries. It shows the thread's own entries alone: those the page lists, the thread's own and those it holds by `also:`.
- Which threads. The map of China goes on the page of `china` and of every thread under it. The map of Indochina goes on the pages of `vietnam`, `laos`, `cambodia` and `southeast-asia` and of every thread under one of them. A thread is chosen by its line of parents in `lists/cal/th.yaml` (`maps.ROOTS`, `maps.of_thread`), never by a list of names. So a new thread under `china` carries the map with no change to the code. On Nov. 26, 2026 there is no `cambodia` and no `southeast-asia` thread; the rule holds if one is made.
- A page whose range holds no placed entry of the thread carries no map. A thread divided by date carries a map on each part that has one, on the part's dates, and on its own page, on the whole span.
- A link in the map's lists goes to the entry on the thread's page where the page holds it (`t-<id>`), and to the calendar where it does not.

## The page of the two maps

`build/maps.html` (`maps.page`): a heading, one line of what the maps are, and the two maps in the front page's own markup for a list of pages (`ol.e.lists`; its five rules are copied from `templates/series.html`, since `templates/list.html` does not have them). The top bar's one mark 図 leads to it (`templates/list.html`). `./bib build maps` builds it alone; it is also written with either map's page. The two map pages keep their addresses.
