# Armed forces a year

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

The owner's request (Oct. 20–22, 2026): the forces and the machines a year, apart from the economic tables, under Armed Forces Day (`tools/bib/afday.py` calls `forces.afd_block(y)`: the figures of the year before; `forces.block(year)` renders a year). `forces/us.yaml` and `forces/world.yaml`, kept by hand, every figure read off a page (the headers are the schema); `tools/bib/forces.py` renders and checks them (STYLE notes at the top). A table a kind, each open, its date and unit in its title line, its sources linked under it; a column only where the year has it ("Printed then" beside "DMDC today", never empty); wide tables split to fit 400px.

- The United States: men on active duty by service, June 30 (the *Statistical Abstract*, 1965 and 1969 eds., beside DMDC's June files today); where they were, Dec. 31 (1960, 1965, 1968: Abstract 1969, table 375); by place, Sept. 30 (DMDC's reports as *troopdata* transcribes them; not published then); major forces, June 30 (Abstract 1969, table 369: divisions, ships, air groups, squadrons, aircraft by service); the Budget's strategic and general purpose forces (ICBM squadrons, Polaris boats and missiles, bomber wings, divisions, warships; 1964–68, the Abstract's reprints); ships by class, active and reserve (1960–65); Air Force aircraft by type and missiles by design (Atlas, Titan, Minuteman, Thor, Jupiter, Hound Dog, Quail; the *USAF Statistical Digest*, classified then, DTIC's copies on archive.org); nuclear weapons (DOE's 1994 table; FAS's estimate).
- The world, then and later apart: men by service as the *Military Balance* printed them (the Abstract's reprints, 1966–68); ACDA's first table, spending and men, 1966; Jane's warships by type, 1967–68; SIPRI's first yearbook (1969), the NATO and Warsaw Pact countries' military spending a year at 1960 prices, 1960–68 (its NATO sources NATO's own published figures among them; its bracketed estimates count in the sums); an ally's own count, Britain's regular forces at June 30 by service, 1960–68, as the *Annual Abstract of Statistics* first printed them (a decimal as printed, `tol: 0.05`); and, made since, the Correlates of War's military personnel every year and FAS's warheads.
- `check` holds every total to its parts (`sum` rows, `sumcols`), every column to a source, and allows a printed figure that does not add only with its `printed` note. No armor yet, nor NATO's own tables: notes/leads.md, Foreign.
- The estimates of the Soviet forces are calendar entries, a document each, in the subthread "Defense: Soviet forces" (NIE 11-8 and its kin, FRUS 1958–60 III, 1961–63 VIII, 1964–68 X).
