# Leaders and committees

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

`congress/committees.yaml`, written once by `tools/congress/make_committees.py` from Garrison Nelson's committee files on Charles Stewart III's data page (House `hrc8097.mit`, Senate `snc80102.mit`, codebook `cb9999`; never edit the output): for the 87th to the 93rd Congress, each chamber's standing committees, their members in Nelson's order (by side, the *Journals*' listing at the first session by rank, then the additions to an enlarged committee and the replacements by date of assignment), the chairmen and ranking minority members, the dates of assignment and of leaving and why; the leaders; and each roster member's whole years in the chamber at the opening (congress-legislators' terms). Members join the rosters by ICPSR id through unitedstates/congress-legislators (the rosters' source). Its `committees:` list (Nelson's code, chamber, the name the *Congressional Directory* prints, the Congresses it has members in) is the series' committee list: the hearings calendar (daybook/committees/) keys to it. Nelson's files for these Congresses hold the standing committees only (no select, special or joint committee, no subcommittee), and code only the Senate's floor leaders and whips (code 620); the House's leaders (Speaker, floor leaders, whips, caucus and conference chairmen) and the Senate's President pro tempore and conference chairmen are in `congress/committees-hand.yaml`, read from the House's and the Senate's own lists (its `sources`), each change dated as the list dates it, and the script stops where Nelson's Senate leaders differ from the Senate's lists. The hand file also holds an ICPSR id congress-legislators carries differently (Kenneth J. Gray) and any row correction, each with its reason. Rerun the script after changing the hand file (it needs the Nelson files and a congress-legislators checkout; the build does not).

`tools/bib/committee_rosters.py` renders them (STYLE notes at the top): on `congress.html`, under each Congress at its opening, "Leadership" (a table a chamber: office, holder linked to his name entry and roster row, party, the dated changes) and "Committees" (a collapsible table a chamber, a tbody a committee anchored `cm<NN>-<h|s>-<code>`: the ratio, chairman and ranking member, Democrats and Republicans side by side in rank order, each linked to his name entry and his roster row, the changes with their dates); in each roster row, a column "Yrs" (years in the chamber at the opening) and a muted line of his committees and rank; in each Names entry, "Committees" across the Congresses ("House Ways and Means: 1st (chairman), 87th–93rd Cong."). A calendar entry tagged `committees:<NN>` carries the Leadership and Committees blocks, as `congress:<NN>` carries the roster (now the five openings, 87th-91st). `check` fails on ranks not consecutive within side and group, a member who is neither in the roster at the opening nor his seat's successor in `congress/changes.yaml`, a leader not in the roster, a code not in the list; warns on a committee with no chairman and a leader with no name entry [committee-rosters].

The select, special and joint committees, and the subcommittees (`tools/bib/committee_more.py`, its STYLE notes):

- `congress/committees-cdir.yaml`, written by `tools/congress/make_cdir_committees.py` (its RULES) from each Congress's first-session *Congressional Directory* on govinfo (Apr. 1, 1961 to Mar. 10, 1973), read page by page from the scan's OCR (`pdftotext -layout`): the Senate's and the House's select and special committees and the joint committees (not the party committees, the House's housekeeping committees, the commissions and boards), each member in the order printed with his role (chairman, vice chairman, cochairman), joined to the rosters by surname, chamber and State; the party is the roster's (the Directory prints none in these lists); the printed page of each committee, linked to the PDF at its page. A committee created later in the Congress (the House's Select Committee on Government Research, 1963) from the second-session edition. 28 committees, 1,319 places, 87th–93rd. Their codes are the hearings calendar's (`code_of` in `tools/daybook/make_committees.py`: `j.atomic-energy`, `s.special-committee-on-aging`), and they join the committee list (`committee_rosters.names`), so the hearings' rows link to them; so do, by name alone, the committees the Digest holds and no Directory read lists (`DIGEST_ONLY`: the Senate's Select Committee on National Water Resources, the House's on Export Control) and a lead too garbled to name one (`<ch>.unread`, "committee (name unread)", make_committees.py STYLE 7).
- Subcommittees: the *Directory* prints none in any edition from 1961 to Mar. 1971, and from Dec. 3, 1971 only each one's chairman and ranking minority member. `congress/subcommittees.yaml`, written by `tools/congress/make_subcommittees.py` (its RULES), comes from the *Congressional Staff Directory* (Brownson; the 1961, 1963 and 1971 editions, the Digital Library of India's scans on archive.org, read by their OCR): every standing, select and joint committee's subcommittees, their chairmen and members by surname, each matched to his committee's members, the printed counts held against the names read, each name and page linked to the leaf. 87th, 88th and 92nd only: the 1965, 1967 and 1969 editions were not found open (`notes/leads.md`; searched again Oct. 23, 2026). 726 subcommittees, about 6,500 places (the 92nd's 267 and 2,641, added Oct. 23, 2026); what the rules could not settle (a count not met, a name unread) is the subcommittee's `check`, shown muted.
- Rendered after the standing committees, under each Congress on `congress.html` and in the calendar's `committees:<NN>` blocks, each folded and closed: "Senate (House) select and special committees", "Joint committees" (two columns, a tbody a committee, anchored `cm<NN>-<s|h|j>-<code>`), and "Senate (House) subcommittees", "Joint committees' subcommittees" (one column of text, a row a subcommittee, anchored `sc<NN>-<ch>-<code>-<n>`, so nothing is wider than the column at 400px); a Congress with none read says so. A member's roster line goes on with his select, special and joint committees; his Names entry's "Committees" with them and his subcommittees, a Congress an item. `check` fails on a code not in the list, a committee without members, a subcommittee without its committee or page; warns on a member not joined and on the hearings' committees the list lacks [committee-more].
