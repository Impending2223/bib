# Economic indicators

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

`./bib build` puts a table at the head of each calendar month: the figures that report on that month, filed by the period they cover, not the date they were issued (quarters in the quarter's last month, fiscal years in June, calendar years in December). Each row has the figure as first reported, its release date, and the figure as revised today.

The tables that recur carry "〈〈 Title 〉〉": the same kind's before and after in the calendar, on any page, in the thread line's link style, the date on hover: the month's set (one pair, on Economic indicators; its divisions, Money and credit, the exchange rates, Oil, the approval table, none), the Southeast Asia table, the budget, Armed Forces Day (the owner's requests, Nov. 4, 2026; STYLE 18 in `tools/bib/indicators.py`); every caption centered but the month set's divisions' headings (Money and credit, Exchange rates and gold, Oil, Presidential approval), left-aligned (the owner, Nov. 5, 2026). The set's order (the owner's ruling, Nov. 10, 2026: "The real rates and yield spread block should follow the nominal rates block in the 'money and credit' subdivision of the economic indicators table. The 'money and credit' subdivision should come immediately after the headline prices, employment, and output indicator subdivision. Then 'Exchange rates and gold'. Then 'oil'."): the indicators table (Prices, Employment, Output, Federal finance, Federal debt, International); Money and credit, the nominal rates (Rate | Percent), then in the indicators' four columns the real rates and the yield spread ("Real rates") and the money stock and free reserves ("Money stock and reserves"); Exchange rates and gold; Oil; Presidential approval (STYLE 19 in `tools/bib/indicators.py`; ORDER and GROUP follow it, and with them the servants and the master page). "As first reported" heads a column only where another holds a later figure (the exchange rates' column goes unheaded).

Concepts and sources (the owner's rule, Oct. 21, 2026; standing): what each series measures, then and today, and where its figures come from, is written once, in the master, `indicators/concepts.yaml`, each sentence or clause tagged with the span of reporting periods it concerns; never in a module, a table or a page. Each calendar page (each year's, and the reader's blocks alike) writes a servant of it right after the month table where a series first appears on that page (Southeast Asia's after its own table, under the first Monday: below): of the series first used there, grouped under the tables' subheadings (Prices, Employment, Output, Federal finance, Federal debt, International, Money and credit, Real rates, Money stock and reserves, Exchange rates and gold, Oil, The war, Opinion), only the pieces whose span holds a period that page files for the series; no series the page does not show, nothing about periods outside it. Each servant ends "See the master entry for a full statement of indicator concepts and sources used in the series", linked to the master. Every row's name, in every later table on the page, links to its servant (`#ind-<page>-<series>`); a series with no servant on its page links to the master, and `check` warns [indicator-servant]. The full tables, every row of every series, then and today, live on the master page, `build/indicators.html` ("Indicators": the statement by group, then the tables; `./bib build indicators`), listed in the series index, the README and the top bar (数; at first kept out of the bar, then put in it, the owner's request, Oct. 22, 2026). A new series: its statement goes into the master with its spans; `check` fails on a series without one, a span it cannot read, or an unknown `{VAR}`.

- ALFRED initial releases: CPI, unemployment, payrolls, industrial production, GNP, real GNP; the deflator is GNP over real GNP in the same release.
- Transcribed from the next *Economic Report*, of January (February in 1968), the Reports of Jan. 1962 to Jan. 1969 (page images on govinfo and FRASER; values and table/page in MANUAL in the script): wholesale prices, the administrative and cash budgets (actual through fiscal 1967; the Jan. 1969 Report prints the unified budget alone), the unified budget (fiscal 1968 on, its own series), the federal sector of the national accounts, the balance of payments. For these "first" means as reported then.
- Today's values: FRED. Budget figures today are on the unified basis (fiscal 1969 on), not the old concepts; the over-all balance of payments is no longer published; the gold stock has no first-reported column.
- Output gap: the CEA's estimates then (from the Reports) and the CBO's now (FRED GDPPOT against GDPC1, with its vintage), in separate rows: the concepts are not comparable, so each row leaves the other's column "—".
- The balance of payments changes concept as the Reports did: the over-all balance (1961–63), the balance on regular transactions (the January 1965 Report: 1963–64), then the liquidity balance and the official reserve transactions balance (January 1966: 1964–65), each its own series, never one row (STYLE 8). Real GNP and the deflator move to 1958 prices with the benchmark revision of Aug. 19, 1965 (`BASE` in the script).
- Federal debt and balances (DEBT in the script, `--only debt`; the owner's request, Oct. 20, 2026): the public debt monthly, as the *Treasury Bulletin* first printed each month-end and its change (p. 1, then Debt Outstanding, Table 1, from Feb. 1968), today FRED's GFDEBTN; debt over GNP and net interest over GNP quarterly, each computed within one Economic Report (no report printed the ratios); the federal primary balance quarterly (the national accounts' surplus or deficit, net lending or borrowing, with net interest added back); and OMB's debt-to-GDP by fiscal year, today's only. Today's net interest counts imputed interest on federal pensions: not the 1960s measure (the statement in `indicators/concepts.yaml`). Other numbers worth keeping: `notes/extensions/memos/indicators-suggestions.md`.
- Money and credit (`kind: rate`; RATES and DISCOUNT in the script, `--only rates` to write them alone): a division of its own right after the indicators table, under the same rule, the rates first in two columns (Rate | Percent), the real rates and the money stock after them: rates are not revised, so one figure each, with its change in points. The federal funds rate, the prime, the 3-month bill and the 10-year Treasury from FRED; the New York Reserve Bank's discount rate kept by hand from the Board's press releases and the *Bulletin* (FRASER, by page): in a month it changed, the Board's announcement and the day it took effect in grey; otherwise `since` (the date it took effect, on hover). STYLE 13 in `tools/bib/indicators.py`.
- Money, the foreign accounts and the debt's terms (the measures `notes/extensions/memos/indicators-suggestions.md` marked "No"; `tools/indicators/make_finance.py`, its docstring the sources, each figure with its table and page in READ blocks; `tools/bib/finance.py`, its STYLE; indicators STYLE 17). As first printed, read off FRASER's page images: the exchange rates (the pound, the mark, the franc: the *Federal Reserve Bulletin*'s "Foreign exchange rates", 1960–68, the issue of the month after; one figure, a table of their own, "Exchange rates and gold"); the FHA new-home yield and the FHLBB's conventional mortgage rate, contract (Apr. 1963–Jan. 1968) and effective (from Feb. 1967), as rates (the *Bulletin*, from July 1964); the debt's computed interest rate (the *Treasury Bulletin*, Debt outstanding, Table 2, 1960–68) and its average length for 1963–65 (the same, Public debt operations, Table 4). As the next January's *Economic Report* tabled them (their first printings not yet read: `notes/leads.md`): the money stock M1 and M1 with time deposits, free reserves, reserve assets (gold, currencies, IMF position), the balances on merchandise and on goods and services (quarterly, annual rates), the debt's holders (Federal Reserve, trust funds) and its average length for 1961–62 and 1966–68. Moody's Aaa and Baa (FRED, rates, one figure). Today: FRED (M1SL/H.6, NFORBRES, TRESEGUSM052N, BOPBM and BOPBGS times four); today's M2 and unit labor costs and hourly compensation (nonfarm business) as rows of their own, no figure then. Groups: the money stock and free reserves "Money stock and reserves", Money and credit's second block, after the real rates; the exchange rates "Exchange rates and gold"; the public debt, its ratios and the debt's terms "Federal debt". The London gold price has its id (`gold-london`) and waits for a source. Rerun: `python3 -I tools/indicators/make_finance.py [--only IDS]`.
- Oil (the owner's request, Oct. 21, 2026; `tools/bib/oil.py`, its STYLE): U.S. crude production and products supplied monthly, imports and exports monthly, as the *Survey of Current Business* first printed the Bureau of Mines' figures (p. S-35, FRASER, the issue its "Released"), in millions of barrels a day, with the change from the month before and a year earlier (percent, day for day; trade, net imports in m b/d; reserves, bn bbl; oil.py STYLE 2); today EIA's (products supplied from 1963; trade by year, in December, its own row); proved reserves at Dec. 31, the API's as the *Minerals Yearbook* printed them, beside EIA's. The Survey's misprints stand, the correction in grey. A table of its own after Money and credit and the exchange rates, without vacant columns (the owner's ruling, Oct. 21, 2026): the rows with both figures in four columns, those with one in Indicator | Figure | Reported (oil.py STYLE 5). Today's crude production is the Survey's to its rounding in most months: the data, not a bug (the statement says where they differ). Its calendar thread: Oil. The world a year (the owner's request, Oct. 22, 2026; `tools/bib/oilworld.py`, its STYLE; `sources/oil-world.yaml`, kept by hand): crude production by country, 1960-68, as the Bureau of Mines' *Minerals Yearbook* for the year printed it (its world table, by page, archive.org's copies; each year's column held to the table's printed totals), in m b/d with the change from the year before as the same table revised it and the share of the world; proved reserves by country where the *Oil and Gas Journal*'s year-end table is open (1960, 1961: the Journal on archive.org runs June 1959-Dec. 1961 only). No today column: no open series found counts crude by country in barrels for the 1960s; the U.S. figure today (EIA) is noted where it differs. Carried by the December entries tagged `oilworld:<YYYY>` (`cal.1960-12.oil` ... `cal.1968-12.oil`). The posted prices of Arabian light at Ras Tanura and Venezuela's Oficina, each change dated (the *Journal*, the CIA's memoranda), a table under Esso Export's cut of Aug. 9, 1960 (`oilprice:ras-tanura`). Neither is an indicator series: no statement in the master; each table says under it what it counts.

- Changes use one basis in both value columns (CHANGE in the script): monthly percent, not annualized; quarterly percent at an annual rate; points; persons. CPI, WPI, industrial production and the GNP deflator also carry the change from a year earlier (YOY in the script; `yoy`, `yoy_now`), each column from its own figures: the year-earlier value in the same release or Report (for the WPI, `year_ago` in MANUAL, transcribed from the same tables; the deflator's quarter a year earlier in the same release), and today's series.
- Formatting and style are settled in the STYLE notes at the top of `tools/bib/indicators.py` (periods "Q1 1961", "FY1962"; "$bn" and "m"; one scale per group; stacked receipts/expenditures/balance right-aligned; "—" versus "n.a."; separate rows for incomparable concepts). Follow them, and fix any table that drifts from them. Each series needs a statement in the master (`indicators/concepts.yaml`, above), with pointers; `check` fails without one.
- Edit the script, not the YAML, and rerun; the key comes from the environment and is never written to the repo. The build needs no network.
- `check` fails on a bad period, a first-reported figure without a release date, or a transcribed figure without a source.
