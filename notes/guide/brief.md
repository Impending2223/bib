# The brief (read before adding anything)

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

The owner's standing instructions; the messages they come from, verbatim, are in `notes/owner-brief.md`.

**Prose.** "Compact. Plain. Compressed. Springs and Autumns, but without the subtle words." Record what happened, not what it meant. No praise, blame, irony, or color; no "for Moscow's benefit," "a good midterm," "the movement reads it as a defeat." Short declaratives and fragments. Drop articles and verbs where the line still reads. Match the original lists, not your own register: read a few entries of the section you are adding to before writing.

**The calendar.** In the owner's words: "a calendar of executive and legislative events, and any other events that left a trace, or should have left a trace, on the political and policy histories of the Kennedy and Johnson Administrations, their Congresses, or those of their members. ... at least a month-by-month granularity. ... a brief statement for each calendar entry and, where events span multiple calendar entries, for each thread. ... The purpose of the statements for calendar entries is not a narrative, but a memory aid and a bibliographic record, or a set of bibliographic pointers. ... I want the calendar to supply a bibliography for each event, if not each entry. (That is, you might have several entries for the Vienna Summit or the Berlin Crisis, but you might only have a full bibliography entry for the first.) At the very least, I want the calendar entries to point to the relevant material elsewhere in the bibliography. Names, events."

What is an entry, and how a reading runs: the reader's brief, `notes/briefs/reader.md`, in force from Oct. 30, 2026 (the owner: "I approve the memo. I approve R5."): the test (R5: what the work being read dates and treats as an event), the informal record, the papers of government, the parties and the members (either party, in or out of the White House), the counterparts and their followers before the radar, the organized interests, the parties to an event, the press, military matters, trips and stretches, what is not an entry (R18: out of the brief only outside the span or with no bearing on politics or policy anywhere; "minor" and "below the level" as it defines them), the raw material, the citing rules, the records and the witnesses, Passed over and the leads, the mechanics. Each rule's source is the table at its foot; the owner's messages, verbatim, `notes/owner-brief.md`. A ruling on reading is written there, not here. The paragraphs it replaced here, as they stood: `notes/briefs/superseded/claude-md-brief.md`.

So every calendar entry has:
- `c`: what happened, in one to three clipped sentences. Who, what, the number.
- `n`: the pointers, in this order:
  - the primary record, linked: the statute (Statutes at Large on govinfo, "77 Stat. 56"), the Federal Register, the Congressional Record, the Public Papers (GPO's volume, "1961 Pub. Papers 1"; APP where they do not print it: "The Public Papers"), the presidential library's file, the FRUS document, the case. The owner asked for these (Oct. 2, 2026); where the record is found and the event's bibliography lacks it, add it there too.
  - the event's bibliography. An event is not a thread: the Bay of Pigs and Mongoose are two events in the Cuba thread; the Vienna Summit and the Berlin wall two in Berlin and Vienna. The event's first entry carries its full bibliography (each work by its short form, "Surname, *Main Title*", the leading article dropped, linked to its entry in the A–Z bibliography; no list or section; a run of works printed in order of publication, then by the first author's surname or the short title, then the short title: `ordered` in `tools/bib/works.py`, at build); later entries of the same event point back to it with `See [[id]]`. An entry that opens a new event within a thread carries its own. This is how the calendar points "to the relevant material elsewhere in the bibliography" (the owner's words); it no longer names the themed lists' sections that treat the event (the owner's ruling, Oct. 13, 2026, with the move to one A–Z list). Write a work as its card, `[[w.beschloss-crisis-years]]` (a pinpoint after it: `[[w.gallup-poll]], III, 1862`): the build prints the full citation at the work's first appearance in each year's page of the calendar, the short form after (the owner's request, Oct. 13, 2026; FIRST CITE in `tools/bib/works.py`), the full citation's pinpoint in the Bluebook's place, after the title and before the year, the parenthetical after the year ("Zelizer, *Taxing America ... 1945–1975*, at 257 (1998) (corporate payments speeded)"; a second pinpoint "; *Id.* at 260 (...)"; the owner, Nov. 5, 2026; BLUEBOOK FIRST CITE there); the data keeps writing `[[w.x]], 257 (...)`. A work with no card yet: make one first (`./bib works new`, which refuses where a card already matches; `works/`, or `./bib works adopt`): every work a note prints is its card, read or behind "To check:", so readers working at once never write one work two ways (the owner, Nov. 5, 2026).
  - for an event done in private and documented in records nobody saw at the time (a recording, a memorandum, an FBI file), where and when it became public: "Made public: Drew Pearson's column, May 24, 1968." The brief's "left a trace, or should have left a trace": such events belong in the calendar, dated by the record.
  - `Names: …`, every person in `c` who has a name entry ("Names, events"), by surname, commas between: "Names: Heller, Tobin." No list or section after the names (the owner's ruling, Oct. 14, 2026; taken out of the data Oct. 15). Each name is found by its `name:` tag and the name index (`who_named` in `tools/bib/build.py`) and linked to the name entry; a person with a name entry but no Part III entry (from the rosters or FRUS's lists) by his tag alone (`tagged_named`). Where the tagged persons share a surname, write the given name ("Martin Luther King", "Frank Johnson"); a son or father with his suffix ("Harry F. Byrd Jr.", "Martin Luther King Sr."); a name in its own order as it stands ("Ngô Đình Diệm", "Satō", "Haile Selassie"). Nothing after the names but a sentence of its own ("Check: …"). `check` warns on a list or section written in the line [names-section], a name no person fits [names-unknown], and one several fit that the tags do not settle [names-ambiguous]. A justice is named only where the entry quotes his opinion or is about him, not for writing or joining one.
- `tags`: a hidden `name:<key>` (the name entry's anchor: `name:mcnamara-robert-s`) for every person the entry names (every name of `Names:` among them) or whose act it records and who has a name entry, the President included where the entry is his message, address, press conference, signature, order, proclamation, NSAM, meeting, or a poll of his approval. Tags are not shown; they put the entry in the person's life (Names), as do the persons of `Names:`. A namesake is not tagged (Edward McCormack is not the Speaker; Lucius Clay of Berlin is not his son), nor a name in a case's title. `check` fails on a key that names no person.

What each source stands for (the owner's rule, Oct. 23, 2026): a source cited under an entry stands for the whole of `c`, its dates, numbers, names, quotations and facts, unless the note says otherwise; where it does not, the entry and the note together say which claim each source is for: a parenthetical after the cite ("[[w.x]], 123 (the vote)"), a label before it ("Votes: ..."), or the cite in the sentence of `c` it supports. Omission implies, determinately: under an entry ABCD, "X; Y; Z (C)" says X and Y stand for A, B and D, and Z for C and C's tie to the rest (the same person, act or thing), not for A, B and D. A source read that does not bear out a claim is no cite for it: the claim takes "Check:" or goes.

Hard and soft (the same day): the sources read (the primary record, linked; a work cited with its pinpoint, read off the page) come first; the works not yet read stand behind "To check:", a sentence of its own before "See" and "Names:" ("To check: [[w.a]]; [[w.b]]."), the event's bibliography among them. A reading run that reads one moves it out, with its pinpoint and, where it stands for part of the entry, its parenthetical; a source found and not yet read (a book, a hearing, a file) joins the To check. "Check:" marks a detail to verify; "To check:", sources not yet read. A pinpoint names a number (a page, a volume and page, a document, a chapter, a note) or links the place read; a pointer in words ("[[w.x]], on Rusk", "for the arithmetic", a chapter's title) is recalled, and the work stands behind "To check:", the words its parenthetical (the owner, Oct. 30, 2026; swept that day, `tools/works/unpin_words.py`: 31 cited with words, 22 bare; `check` fails on a new one [claims]). Swept Oct. 24, 2026: every work cited without a pinpoint went behind "To check:" (2,439 notes; a work inside a sentence of prose, some two dozen, left as written).

Claims and their sources (the owner's request, Oct. 28, 2026: "break the entries down into (invisible?) sentence-by-sentence, clause-by-clause units, and accord to each the appropriate (invisible?) pincite", in a form "readily and programmatically transformed into a 'verbose' citation format ... footnotes or inline citations"; invisible for now): each entry's `c` is split into units at its sentences' ends and its semicolons, never inside a quotation, parentheses or brackets, lettered A, B, C (the ABCD of the rule above; a finer unit is a semicolon, nothing is marked in `c`). Its claim map says which source grounds which units at which page: worked out from the note whenever wanted (a work read and no parenthetical, all; with one, `?`, to be turned into letters by a reader), and stored only where a reader maps it, as the hidden `src` (`{c: <hash of c>, rows: ['w.caro-passage-of-power 492: A', 'w.dallek-flawed-giant 70: B-E', 'via w.dallek-flawed-giant 70 n.36: B, E', '<a link's address>: all']}`), written by patch. Every row names an item the note (or `c`) prints; the note stays the visible record, and `check` holds the two together [claims]. `./bib claims show ID [--inline]` prints the units and the entry with its sources as footnotes (or inline), `./bib claims seed ID` the mechanical map to start from, `./bib claims stats` the count. The STYLE: `tools/bib/claims.py`.

What the works rest on (the same day: the agents to log "the materials and records cited for the claims in the secondary sources they read"; Oct. 30: "a trace/map of *all* the *ultimate*, or *root*, materials that bear on a fact or event"; "Don't drop the first rule"): the witness registry, `sources/witnesses/<year>.yaml` (a row a witness: a document, recording, transcript, print, statement, study, memoir; its upstream edges and, at a root, why it ends) and `sources/witnesses/by-work/<card>.yaml` (a work's edges, page and note to the witness its text rests on); schema, STYLE and check [witnesses]: `tools/bib/witnesses.py`; the memo, `notes/extensions/memos/witnesses.md`; the readers' rules, `notes/briefs/reader.md`, R35-R43. A `via` row of the entry's map ties a witness to the units it grounds (`via x.1965-02-07.bundy-to-johnson: B, E`, or through the work's edge, `via w.dallek-flawed-giant 70 n.36: B, E`); `./bib stemma ID` prints each unit's tree to its roots, counting roots and agents, never works; `--leads` the open branches. The unread record joins "To check:" with the work it comes from ("To check: Bundy to Johnson, Feb. 7, 1965, NSF, LBJ Library (cited by [[w.dallek-flawed-giant]], 251 n.14)."). Converted Nov. 3, 2026 from `sources/cited-records/` (the rule of Oct. 28, now on the shelf: `tools/witnesses/convert_records.py`, once); witnesses that are one FRUS document joined by `tools/witnesses/join_frus.py` (rerunnable); the near matches left for a reader, `notes/witnesses-near-matches.md`. Readers at work together write new witnesses to `inbox/witnesses/<patch>.yaml`, never the shared year files; the merger folds them in (`tools/witnesses/fold.py`).

Calendar entries on the States and the cities take hidden `state:<postal code>` and `city:<slug>` tags (`state:ny`, `city:nyc`) and the threads in `notes/threads.md` ("The States and the cities").

Each thread in `th.yaml` has a brief statement (`c`), as the owner asked: what the thread is, and its stations, in one or two clipped sentences. Every thread is its matter as Washington and its counterparts met it, but the statements don't say so; that framing, and the scopes taken out of the statements, are in `notes/threads.md`. `th.yaml` keeps the threads in alphabetical order of their names (War on poverty under "poverty"; `check` warns otherwise). The statement is written once, for every part of the calendar however it is divided (volume.md §4, built Nov. 4, 2026; `tools/bib/statements.py`, its STYLE): `c`, the scope, true of every part; a sentence that belongs to some years only, tagged with a hidden span, the indicators' syntax (`{{1961..1962-06|...}}`, `{{..1962|...}}`, `{{1966..|...}}`, `{{1961-04|...}}`), shown untagged on each part its span meets; then, made by the build, the names of the thread's events in the part, in date order, a clipped sentence of names, plain text, unlinked ("Bay of Pigs; Mongoose."; the owner, Nov. 4, 2026): each event's `short:` on its first entry (an entry filed under the thread or a ghost subthread of it; not one held by `also:`, nor a subthread's that has its own statement). No dates shown, no label, no "Stations:". A thread still holding the old `c2`, `c3` (a statement a part) keeps them until converted: `python3 -I tools/statements/apply.py FRAGMENT.yaml` writes its `c` and the events' names and drops `c2`, `c3`; `python3 -I tools/statements/show.py SLUG [--c '...'] [--short ID=NAME]` shows a draft a year at a time. `check` [statements] fails on a span it cannot read, warns on an untagged sentence naming a year, a tagged one naming a year outside its span, and counts the threads not converted.

`./bib audit-cal` checks the dated entries against these rules and writes `notes/calendar-audit.md`: persons in `c` in neither `Names:` nor a `name:` tag, entries with neither a bibliography nor a `See`, entries with no primary record or with one named but not linked, and the thread statements.

**Bibliography notes.** One or two fragments: what the book is, whose voice, what to read it for. "Interviews with nearly everyone, Bissell included." "Read the Washington chapters." Not reviews. Most entries need no note: when the title does the work, leave it bare. No awards, no "start here," no side labels ("defense," "revisionist," "from the left"); state a thesis or a source's provenance the same way whichever side it is on. (This came of the owner's objection, Oct. 2, 2026, to Viet. notes "unduly credulous of NVA/NLF/dovish material", which gave the critics "the unmarked default position" and marked their opponents "the defense". The owner also asked where the Vietnamese-language works were: Viet. II.E should hold them.)

A work's note is either the card's (the card note: true of the book wherever it is listed: "Interviews with nearly everyone, Bissell included") or the listing's (why it is in this list: "Read the Washington chapters"). The card note shows first, then the listing's; a listing may write `card_note: replace` or `omit`.
