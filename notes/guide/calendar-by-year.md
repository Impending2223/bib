# The calendar by year

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

One list in the data (`lists/cal/`), a page a year in the build (volume.md §3; built Nov. 4, 2026): `cal1960.html` (Cal. 1960, from Jan. 6) to `cal1969.html` (Cal. 1969, to Jan. 20), `calprimaries.html` (Cal. 1972–76, the primary seasons of 1972 and 1976, one page while they hold only the primaries), and `cal.html`, the index; in the reader, a block each, the index the Cal. block. January 1966 is two sections, `196601` (Jan. 1–9) and `196601b` (from the session's opening), one page. `split:` in `lists/cal/list.yaml` is a list, a part an item: its `page`, the day it begins (`from`; the first, the calendar's first day), its `abbr` ("Cal. 1961"), `called` (the words the other ledes name it by, "the calendar of 1961", linked), title, span, page_title, h1, lede and logic. The list's own page_title, h1 and lede, with `index_logic`, and its `logic`, are the index's alone: a year's page opens with its year ("1965"), one line ("Executive, legislative, judicial, and state events of 1965.") and the ebooks and feeds line, nothing more (the owner, Nov. 4, 2026). A part a line: a year that outgrows some 8 MB splits again by half-year, by the same line. So it did (Nov. 12, 2026; the owner: "Keep the page size rule and split the years"): 1960 to 1968 are two pages each, `cal1964a.html` (Cal. Jan.–June 1964) and `cal1964b.html` (Cal. July–Dec. 1964); the year pages, `retired_parts` in `list.yaml`, are stubs that forward an old link by its anchor to the half that holds it (the month in an anchor's form names its page: `cal.1964-09-01.x`, `d1964-09-01`, `196409`; a retired page's own `cal1964.thread.x`, `th-cal1964`, `ind-cal1964-x` go to the first half holding the like). A dated section goes to the part its first day falls in; the threads section is in all. `tools/bib/calsplit.py` (STYLE notes at the top):

- Each part's thread index lists the threads with entries in it, with that part's statement (the scope, the spans that meet the part's range, the events in it: above, under "So every calendar entry has"; a part's range its `from` to its last section's last day, `part_range`) and its dates by year: the year unlinked, in the sans, a little heavier ("**1961** Jan. 23, Feb. 20."), a date the same as the one before it left out for now (the owner, Nov. 5, 2026; STYLE 16). Across the seams, muted: after its dates, the year and date of the thread's next entry in a later part (". 1962 Jan. 14."), the year linked, not underlined, to the thread in that part's index, the date to the entry; before its dates, its last entry in an earlier part, linked the same way. A thread not yet converted keeps its `c`, `c2`, `c3` by the three parts of Oct. 17, 2026 (`legacy_parts`).
- A part's thread entries are anchored `<page>.thread.<slug>` (`cal1961.thread.cuba`), its threads section `th-<page>`; a rubric links to its own part's index.
- The index: the whole calendar's contents (below), then every thread, each with the years it has entries in, each linked to the thread in that year's index; its lines anchored `cal.thread.<slug>` (STYLE 13).
- At each seam, muted: "‹ Dec. 31, 1960, in Cal. 1960" at the head of a part's first day; "Jan. 1, 1961, in Cal. 1961 ›" at the foot of the part before's last (1969's ends Jan. 20; the primaries' begins Mar. 1, 1972).
- Links: write `[[id]]` as ever. The build routes a link whose anchor is in another part to that page, on the calendar's pages and on every other page (`cal.html#X`, `cal1961.html#X` → `cal1962.html#X`); `./bib links` checks them all. Old addresses land: `cal.html` (the index) and `cal63.html`, `cal66.html` (small pages) send a link's anchor to its year's page by script, the year read from the anchor or from a map the build writes (STYLE 14).
- `./bib build cal` (or any year's page name) builds every year, the index and the old pages.
- A tap on a thread's entry in the index, or on its line under an entry (not on a link), lights the thread: its entries and its index entry on white (a lighter panel in the dark theme), each entry's own box from its rule to the next and an en dash past the rules' ends on either side, the rules as they were; a day's first or last entry takes a rule while lit where it has none. A rubric's thread name, followed to the index, lights it too, never clears it. Tapped again, cleared; kept for the tab (sessionStorage). While it is lit, the left and right arrow keys go to the thread's entry before and after the one at the top of the screen, by its ⟨⟨ ⟩⟩, across the year pages (the owner, Nov. 5, 2026). While it is lit, a mark a lit entry in the thumb's column, its place and height the entry's share of the page (3px at least), no bar behind: amber while the scroll wheel's headings show (they stay 1.5 s after it is let go, `LINGER` in the templates), white after; a click on a mark brings its entry to the top (STYLE 8). The headings the scroll wheel shows are links while they show: a click goes there. On every page the scroll wheel is hidden until the reader scrolls (the wheel, a finger, the keys, the page's scroll bar; not a link followed or a jump: `HAND` in `templates/list.html` and `series.html`), and goes 1.2 s after the scrolling stops. Nothing moves (STYLE 7, `SCRIPT` in `tools/bib/calsplit.py`; `data-threads`, `data-thread`, `data-t` in `tools/bib/build.py`).
- Visibility (the spec, `notes/proposals/visibility-spec.md`; phase 3, Nov. 12, 2026): every year's page, the primaries' and each year's block in the reader carry the panel under the contents, above the threads: a cell a polity (the US, the USSR, China, France, Germany, the UK; Other, folded, the states the page's claims carry), each cycling on, greyed, off, with public and nonpublic under it; the P/A switch; the pin; the summary line; Defaults (the US on, the rest greyed). Each entry's `c` is a span a claim; a claim shows in full if any of its tags is on, greyed if none is on and one is greyed, hidden if all are off; an entry takes its claims' fullest state. The choices are kept in the browser (`localStorage`, one state for every page) where it allows, and the page works without it. `tools/bib/vispage.py` (STYLE), its CSS and script in `build/vis/`.
