# State sources

Moved word for word from CLAUDE.md, Nov. 10, 2026 (the owner: "I approve both"); CLAUDE.md keeps the core and an index of these guides.

`sources/states.yaml` is the register of each State's own official publications of election returns: blue books, legislative manuals, registers, statements of vote, canvasses, abstracts, count books, and the State election databases. Its header is the schema. For every State and D.C. it records, for 1956–1974 first:

- each publication or database: title, publisher, which edition prints which election;
- what it prints: the general election, the specials between Novembers (with votes or not, as checked against `congress/specials.yaml`), State races (governor, statewide, legislature), primaries (official or party);
- every online copy with its `access` from here, tested on the date in `checked`: open, borrow, bot-check, blocked, dead, other;
- `best_for_specials` (where to get vote counts for that State's specials) and `gaps` (what is only on paper).

Start there before searching for a State's returns. The Clerk's *Statistics* stay the source for `elections/`; a State figure goes in as a reading or a note, with its page. Recheck access with `python3 tools/sources/check_access.py [--write] [--st XX ...]`; `check` fails on a State missing from the register, an access outside the legend, or an online copy without a url or a checked date.

- Bot checks (Cloudflare, AWS WAF, captchas) are the sites' own: don't try to get around them; a person in a browser can open the page. HathiTrust's pages are bot-checked here, but its catalog data (the Bib API, `catalog.hathitrust.org/api/volumes/brief/htid/<id>.json`) answers: holdings and rights, not text.
- archive.org works: item pages, OCR text (`/download/<id>/<id>_djvu.txt`), page images (`/download/<id>/page/n<N>.jpg`). Many States' runs are there (CT, NH, MA, IL, CA, NC, HI, MT, AL, FL).
- A host this environment's network policy refuses ("CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403") is `blocked`; the owner can allow it in the environment's network settings.
- The probe reads the page, not the proxy (Oct. 23, 2026): the proxy's "200 Connection Established" is no status (it once made a failed TLS handshake read as open); a 200 carrying a challenge (Anubis, Turnstile, Imperva, AWS WAF) is `bot-check`; a TLS failure, a firewall's "Request Rejected" and a proxy 502 are `other`. Google Books lines are kept by hand, as `borrow` is (their preview). A failure is retried an hour later before it goes in the register. A site that sends the wrong intermediate certificate ("unable to get local issuer certificate") is read with the issuer its certificate names (its AIA), added to the system's roots, as a browser does: verified, not bypassed (`aia_bundle` in `tools/sources/check_access.py`; Alaska's elections site, whose returns of 1960–74 are open so).
- The States' session laws and journals, where a lead needs an act's day: Alabama's *Acts* and both Journals, 1961–70, every session, and Kentucky's Journals, 1959–68, are on archive.org (`alabama-acts-1966`, `jsencoky1960`); the revisors' sites give Idaho's, Minnesota's (with the vetoes, lrl.mn.gov), Nevada's (leg.state.nv.us/Statutes) and Washington's session laws; Texas's, the Legislative Reference Library (its bill search names the chapter); Missouri's *Laws* and Journals, Missouri Digital Heritage's CONTENTdm (`molaws`, full text by `dmQuery`).
- Kansas's *Election Statistics*, 1899–2010, every issue, are in the State Library's KGI Online Library (CONTENTdm, `p16884coll129/83`); Washington's statewide figures, 1898–2006, in the Secretary of State's archive workbook; a State library's CONTENTdm is searched by its API (`dmQuery`, `dmGetCompoundObjectInfo`), its pages being scripts.
- The basis of belief (the owner's request, Oct. 23, 2026: "note the basis for your belief that a named source exists"; "a (then-effective) law or regulation obliging a person to create a record, to preserve it, to transfer it, to hold or keep it, to publish it ... would probably be the best authorities"): every publication, archival series and county or city record (`local`) carries `basis`, one ground a line, `seen` (a copy read), `catalog` (a catalog or finding-aid record, not the text), `law` (the provision obliging an officer to make, keep, transfer or publish it, as then in force where found, read or only cited by the publication), `cited` (a source naming it), `assumed` (no ground yet: what was searched); and `cited_as`, the patterns by which the series' files cite it. `check` fails on an item without a basis or with a ground outside the legend [sources].
- The listing (the owner's request, the same day: "Make it a .MD and link it in the readme"): `notes/state-sources.md`, written by `python3 tools/sources/make_listing.py` (deterministic; its STYLE notes): a section a State, each item's editions, contents, copies with access and checked date (open; closed; paper only), basis, and what the series has used it for, by its `cited_as` and its copies' addresses; then the Check pointers to State records in the calendar, the specials, the governors' races and the readings, each ruled *found* (it names an item with a ground), *assumed* (the item named has none) or *unmatched*. So "Check: the State canvass" or a pointer to the State archives says there whether it names a source found, and where, or an assumption. Rerun after every change to the register; `check` warns when either listing is stale [state-sources], and on a register title with a hyphen in a span of years.
- The law register (the owner's request, Oct. 23, 2026: "create and maintain a similar listing for sourcing State constitutions and laws ... take care for the legal authority for the recording, compilation, and publication of the constitution and the laws. Identified authority is strongly preferred"): `sources/state-law.yaml` (its header the schema), every State and D.C., 1959-69, at least the session laws: each item's title (the volume's own words where read), officer, `authority` (the law, as then in force where found, with where it was read, or only where it is cited) or `authority_searched` (what was searched; one or the other always), basis, `cited_as`, and copies tested from here (HathiTrust's records verified by its Bib API, their pages bot-checked). Listed in `notes/state-law-sources.md` by the same script (`tools/sources/law_listing.py`), with the calendar's Check pointers to a State's laws or journals; checked by `tools/bib/state_law.py` [state-law]; access rechecked by `python3 tools/sources/check_access.py --law`. Readable law of the period found so far: Minnesota Statutes 1961 (the Revisor's site), the Code of Laws of South Carolina 1952 and its 1960 supplement, the Revised Codes of Montana 1947 and the Election Laws of Montana (archive.org), the United States Code of 1964 (the Library of Congress's scans), the constitutions as the 1960s blue books print them (California's in the Statutes, North Carolina's, Pennsylvania's, Connecticut's).
