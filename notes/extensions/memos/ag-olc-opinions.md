# The Attorneys General's and OLC's opinions, 1960–69: what there is, and where it goes

The owner's question (Oct. 23, 2026): "I'm not sure what the AG and OLC got up to in this period. Are their opinions of
sufficient interest to make their way above the line? Below the line?" The period is the calendar's span, 1960–69
(the owner, Oct. 24). This memo answers from the copies read; the register is `daybook/opinions.yaml` (every row read,
with its page and its link), rendered as the list "Opinions" in each day's Documents block (`tools/bib/opinions.py`).

## What there is

**The Attorney General's formal opinions.** The *Official Opinions of the Attorneys General* print 44 for 1960–69:
vol. 41 (to Dec. 31, 1960) has six of 1960; vol. 42 (Jan. 1961–June 1974, with one of Nov. 30, 1960) the other 38.
That is four or five a year: Rogers 6, Walsh 1 and White 1 (as Acting Attorney General), Robert Kennedy 16, Cox 1 (as
Solicitor General, Kennedy approving), Katzenbach 8, Ramsey Clark 9, Mitchell 2 (1969). Read in Google's scans on archive.org (item `op-atty-gen`, files 041
and 042), page by page. Several were released for publication years later (the Executive Order 10925 opinion of Sept.
26, 1961, on Oct. 26, 1965; the Savannah opinion of Nov. 30, 1960, on May 23, 1966: the volume's footnotes).

Most are the routine law of the departments: guaranties as general obligations of the United States (the Development
Loan Fund, AID, FNMA, the Export-Import Bank, the Farmers Home Administration), cargo preference, public lands, veterans'
widows, the Customs and the census. A handful speak to the calendar's matters:

- presidential inability (Aug. 2, 1961), the ground of the Kennedy-Johnson agreement of Aug. 10;
- Executive Order 10925 and the contract-compliance program (Sept. 26, 1961, to Johnson as the committee's chairman);
- women in the federal service (June 14, 1962), overruling the opinion of 1934; Kennedy's memorandum of July 24 follows;
- the Comsat incorporators and directors not officers of the United States (Oct. 25, 1962): the question Morse put to
  the Senate in April 1963;
- wheat to the Soviet Union on commercial credit not a loan under the Johnson Act (Oct. 9, 1963, to Rusk; released with
  Kennedy's letter);
- the President's power to impound federal-aid highway funds (Feb. 25, 1967), after the cuts of Nov. 29, 1966;
- participation certificates outside the debt limit (Feb. 3, 1967), the issue of the House's recommittal motion of Feb. 8;
- the billboard compensation required by the Highway Beautification Act (Nov. 16, 1966);
- Executive Order 11387 on capital transfers valid under the Trading With the Enemy Act (Feb. 3, 1968);
- Laird's appointment under the Ineligibility Clause (Jan. 3, 1969);
- the statement of interpretation on expatriation after *Afroyim v. Rusk* (Jan. 18, 1969).

**The Office of Legal Counsel.** Its opinions of these years were not published then. The Office's foreword to its
*Supplemental Opinions*, vol. 1 (2013), pp. vii–viii: as the Attorneys General's duties grew after the war, "the rate of
publication of Attorney General opinions declined"; the OLC opinions "steadily increased" and, "with occasional
exceptions," were "not ... publicly released"; the Office's own series began with 1977 (1 Op. O.L.C., 1980). The 2013
volume is a selection: thirteen opinions of 1961–69 and, among its "other memoranda," two of the period (the unsigned
blockade memorandum of Oct. 19, 1962; Katzenbach's, as Deputy Attorney General, to Johnson on Mississippi, July 1,
1964). How many the Office wrote in the decade the volume does not say; its daybooks, it says, hold them from 1945.

Of the thirteen, the Cuban ones are the matter of the calendar: Kramer on a blockade (Jan. 25, 1961); Katzenbach on
a neutral's duties against armed expeditions (Apr. 12, 1961, five days before the landing); Schlei on international law
should the Soviets put missiles in Cuba (Aug. 30, 1962). The rest: the Vice President's part in the executive branch
(Mar. 9, 1961), the Federal Power Commission's chairmanship (Feb. 28 and May 11, 1961: Swidler for Kuykendall), lobbying
by officials (Oct. 10, 1961: Moyers and the Peace Corps bill), the States' wiretapping (Feb. 26, 1962), Kennedy's honorary
Irish citizenship (May 10, 1963), government films for the Democratic National Committee (Dec. 26, 1963), the Customs
Court's marshals (Oct. 3, 1967), and two of Rehnquist's in 1969, after the calendar ends (the committee-approval clause;
impoundment of impacted-areas school funds).

**Printed elsewhere.** FRUS prints six legal memoranda of the Department: Katzenbach (OLC) to Bundy on the succession
to Hammarskjöld (Oct. 20, 1961); his note on the draft memorandum to the Director of Central Intelligence (filed Jan.
15, 1962); his memorandum as Deputy Attorney General on the quarantine proclamation (Oct. 23, 1962); and three of his as
Attorney General to Johnson: the troops for Vietnam without further approval by Congress (June 10, 1965), the economy
car (Sept. 23, 1965), the stockpile copper (Nov. 15, 1965). The State Department Legal Adviser's memorandum on the
legality of the defense of Vietnam (Meeker, Mar. 4, 1966; the *Bulletin*, Mar. 28, 1966) is in the register, marked
the State Department's.

**Known of, not read.** The Public Papers name four opinions or advice not in the volumes read (Eisenhower's budget
message, Jan. 18, 1960; the refugee act, July 14, 1960; the copper releases of Mar. 21 and Dec. 2, 1966): `known` in the
register, each with its Check.

## The answer

Above the line, as entries of their own: few, as the owner's examples are few in a century. The opinions that shaped
a decision the calendar records, or that are the decision: the three Cuban OLC opinions and the blockade memorandum of
Oct. 19, 1962; presidential inability (with the agreement that followed); Executive Order 10925; women in the federal
service (with the President's memorandum); Katzenbach to Johnson on Mississippi, July 1, 1964; Katzenbach on the troops,
June 10, 1965; the impoundment opinion of 1967; the expatriation statement of 1969. Thirteen entries in the patch
`inbox/2026-10-24-opinions.yaml`, eleven of them opinions.

Below the line: the others that bear on an entry go into its note as a pointer ("Opinion: 42 Op. Att'y Gen. 229 (1963),
Robert Kennedy to Rusk, Oct. 9: ..."): thirteen entries (the Mutual Security cutoff of Dec. 1960, Comsat, the wheat sale,
the debt limit, the billboards, HUD's first documents, capital controls, Laird, Ireland, the Hammarskjöld succession, the
quarantine proclamation, the copper, the Peace Corps lobbying).

The rest, some forty, stay in the day's Documents and the register: of the departments' administration, not the
political or policy history. Every row is logged there whether or not it reaches an entry.
