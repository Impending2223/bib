# The polls' hours and daylight time, 1960-68: what was found, and how election night should use it

Written Nov. 10-11, 2026, for the owner's request of Nov. 6, 2026: "Elections are late evening or night events in the constituency where they're held. (If poll closing times are fixed by law, we should set an agent or agents on the State law and the relevant session laws to find them, and to find the State and Federal adoption of daylight time.)" The registers: `sources/poll-hours.yaml` (the hours, a State a block, a row a rule) and `sources/daylight-time.yaml` (the Federal law, the zones, each State's daylight time by year, the tz comparison); their checks, `tools/bib/pollhours.py` ([poll-hours] in `./bib check`). Nothing in the build reads them yet.

## What was found, and on what ground

**The hours: every State and D.C., from one compilation of the period.** The Senate Commerce Committee's hearings on election projections, July 18-20, 1967 (`CHRG-90shrg82635` on govinfo; "PPH" in the register), print two compilations by the Library of Congress's Legislative Reference Service: Table A, "General elections: opening and closing of polls (local time)", a State a row with the code section and remarks (pp. 14-16, after the Service's memorandum of Dec. 2, 1966), and the appendix to Elizabeth Yadlosky's report of Apr. 6, 1967, which quotes each State's provisions from its code and latest supplement, with the codes' history notes (pp. 31-75; "The opening and closing times is given for all states", p. 31). Every row of the register cites the page and links it. The history notes give the session law that last set each hour, so most rows reach back before 1966: where the note's last change is before 1960 the row's span begins in 1960; where it falls inside the span (Alabama 1961, California 1961-64, Colorado 1963, Georgia 1964, Kentucky 1964 and 1966, Maine 1961 and 1965, New York 1965, North Dakota 1961, Vermont 1961, Virginia 1962 and 1966, West Virginia 1963, Wyoming 1961), the row begins with the act. Where the two compilations differ (Kentucky's closing, Louisiana's section and closing, California's threshold, Nevada's large counties, Maine's machine places), both are given and the row carries a Check.

**Read in the States' own volumes** (open, and cited by page): Alabama's Acts of 1961 (the 1961 act on § 172) and the Acts of 1962-67, searched (county acts: Blount County 1963; voting-machine acts with 6 and 6:30 p.m. closings); Maine's Public Laws of 1961, c. 360, § 78, and 1965, c. 108 (the closing moved from 7 to 8 p.m., effective Sept. 3, 1965); Minnesota Statutes 1961, § 204.03, and Laws 1965, c. 325; Montana's Election Laws of 1956, 1962 and 1968 (8 a.m. to 8 p.m. throughout); North Carolina's Election Laws of 1959, 1966 and 1967-68 (6:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m., "Eastern Standard Time" until the recodification of 1967); North Dakota's Laws of 1961, ch. 172; the District of Columbia Election Act of 1955 (69 Stat. 702); Michigan's section 720, in a newspaper's legal notice of 1963; South Carolina's Code of 1952 and Acts of 1959-68, searched: **Act No. 260 of 1965 (approved May 6, 1965) moved the statewide closing from 6 to 7 p.m.**, after the supplement the compilation used, so PPH's 6 p.m. is stale for 1965-68 (and Oconee County 7 to 7 from May 5, 1965); Kentucky's Journals, which print the bills: H.B. 198 of 1964 (the general election's closing from 5 to 6 p.m., standard time) and H.B. 7 of 1966 (the primary's); Washington's reenactment of Title 29 (Laws 1965, ch. 9), 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. without the 7 a.m. proviso PPH prints. **New York's hours by year** come from the Kingston Daily Freeman's election-eve pages: 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. in 1960 and 1964, 6 a.m. to 7 p.m. in 1959 and 1962, 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. in 1966 and 1968 (the law of 1965 made 9 p.m. the rule): the older section of the Election Law, with a presidential year's longer hours, is not read. **The States resting on the compilation alone**: AK AZ AR CA CO CT DE FL GA HI IA ID IL IN KS LA MA MD MO MS NE NH NJ NM NV OH OK OR PA RI SD TN TX UT VA VT WI WV WY (the register's `compiled_only: true`).

**The clock in the election law.** Several States fixed the hour by a named clock, not the local civil time: eastern standard time in Georgia (1964 code), North Carolina (to 1967), Pennsylvania and Michigan (a county keeping central time may use it); "Standard Time" in Kentucky; "the customary time in standard use in the locality" in Florida; standard or daylight time "as is in effect at such poll" in Virginia, and "applicable to the polling place" in Alaska; in Minnesota the judges "agree upon some standard of time". A Pennsylvania primary in May closed at 8 p.m. eastern standard, 9 p.m. in a town keeping daylight time.

**Daylight time.** No Federal law advanced the clock from 1946 to 1966; the States and towns chose (the ICC's statement, 1965). The Uniform Time Act of 1966 (80 Stat. 107) advanced every zone from the last Sunday of April to the last Sunday of October from Apr. 1, 1967, a State exempting itself only as a whole, by law; for 1966 it held any place keeping daylight time to the same Sundays (§ 6). Its functions passed to the Department of Transportation (80 Stat. 939-40). The register reads each State's practice, 1960-66, from three surveys printed in the uniform-time hearings (the Committee for Time Uniformity's tables of 1963, twice, and 1965) and from Doris Chase Doane's *Time Changes in the U.S.A.* (1966) and its Supplement of 1969 (an astrologers' compilation, State by State and town by town, with the dates; open on archive.org): its "1966 Time Picture" and its list of the States exempt or remaining on standard time in 1967 and 1968 (Alaska, Hawaii, Michigan to June 14, 1967; Alaska, Arizona, Hawaii in 1968; Indiana's eastern counties by its own statute; Kentucky's "year of grace", 1967). The State laws themselves (Minnesota's fourth Sunday in May to the Tuesday after Labor Day; Iowa's Memorial Day to Labor Day of 1965; Colorado's referendum law of 1965; Virginia's of 1962; Pennsylvania's of 1965; Tennessee's law against daylight time; North Dakota's prohibition) are named by those sources, not read: each is a lead. The zones: the 1970 recodification of the zone rules (35 Fed. Reg. 12,317), its lines a text after the span; the changes of 1967-69 named (Atlantic zone, 1967; Nebraska's line, Jan. 1, 1968; Indiana's, 1968-69; North Dakota's and Kansas's, 1967-68).

**November general elections fall on standard time everywhere, 1960-68.** Every place keeping daylight time had returned to standard time before the general election: the latest return read is the last Sunday in October (Oct. 30, 1960; Oct. 28, 1962; Oct. 25, 1964; Oct. 30, 1966; Oct. 27, 1968), and every election day is later (Nov. 8, 1960; Nov. 6, 1962; Nov. 3, 1964; Nov. 8, 1966; Nov. 5, 1968). The exceptions are the places that kept the next zone's time all year (El Paso, southwest North Dakota, White Pine and Lincoln Counties in Nevada, Indiana's counties on eastern time inside the central zone): those are a matter of the zone, not of daylight time. Daylight time matters for the primaries (April to September), the specials, and New Jersey's and Virginia's November elections of the odd years only through the zone.

## Where the law read disagrees with tz

The tz database represents a zone by its largest city, so a summer hour drawn with its zone is wrong wherever the State did not keep that city's clock. In the register's `tz` list:

- **America/New_York, 1960-66**: daylight time April-October, right for New York, New England, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland and D.C.; **wrong for Georgia, Florida, North and South Carolina** (no daylight time at all), and for most of Virginia (Memorial Day to Labor Day, 1962-65), West Virginia (to September, 1963-65; none before), Ohio (some 240 towns only), Pennsylvania before 1965 (town by town, some to September), eastern Kentucky and Tennessee. The calendar's notes that read Albany's or Atlanta's hours of 1962-64 at Eastern daylight time ("Albany's clock in July 1962 not settled") are, by every source read, Eastern standard: the hour in UTC is one later.
- **America/Chicago, 1960-66**: right for Illinois; **wrong for Alabama, Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Texas, Oklahoma, Kansas, Nebraska, the Dakotas, western Tennessee and western Kentucky** (no statewide daylight time; a few towns), and partly wrong for Minnesota (late May to early September), Wisconsin (to the last Sunday in September through 1964), Iowa and Missouri (local option), the Florida panhandle (Pensacola kept it).
- **America/Denver, 1965-66**: right for Colorado; wrong for Utah, Wyoming, most of New Mexico and Montana (none, or only Los Alamos, Butte and Anaconda, on their own dates). Before 1965 tz has none: right but for Aspen (1964).
- **America/Los_Angeles**: right for California and Nevada; Washington had none in 1960 and returned in September 1961-62; Oregon kept it only locally to 1962.
- **America/Boise**: tz none before 1967; southern Idaho towns kept daylight time 1961-63 (Doane).
- **America/Kentucky/Louisville, America/Indiana/Indianapolis**: tz's histories (Louisville on eastern standard time all year from July 1961 through 1967; Indianapolis on eastern standard all year 1958-68) agree with the 1967-68 accounts read (Kentucky's "year of grace", Indiana's statute); the ICC's orders moving them were not read.
- **America/Detroit, America/Phoenix, Pacific/Honolulu, America/Anchorage**: agree.

## Calendar notes the daylight-time register settles

Ten entries leave a clock unsettled. By `sources/daylight-time.yaml` (for the lead to rule; `lists/` not touched here): Georgia kept no daylight time 1946-66, so the Albany hours of `cal.1962-07-16.georgia-albany` and `cal.1962-08-02.georgia-albany-2`, read at Eastern daylight time, are Eastern standard (an hour later in UTC), and `cal.1964-06-20.georgia`'s Georgia clock is standard; Connecticut kept it statewide, so `cal.1962-06-11.economy`'s New Haven reading at Eastern daylight time stands; Alabama, Tennessee, Mississippi and Arkansas kept none in 1963, so the Central standard readings of `cal.1963-05-07.king` (Birmingham), `cal.1963-05-18.alabama` (Nashville), `cal.1963-05-21.alabama-al` (Montgomery), `cal.1963-06-12.mississippi` (Jackson) and `cal.1963-10-03.assassination` (Heber Springs) stand. Huntsville is a variant: Doane has the city on daylight time Apr. 24-Sept. 25, 1960, so `cal.1960-09-08.space` (Sept. 8, read at Central standard time) would be Central daylight; the Committee for Time Uniformity (1965) says Alabama had daylight time "for the first time in 1964". Check against a Huntsville paper of September 1960.

## What remains (each a lead for `notes/leads.md`, State and cities)

- Sections printed without history (Florida, Mississippi, Missouri, Nebraska, New Jersey, New Mexico, Rhode Island's town-by-town schedule, South Dakota, Texas, Utah, Washington, Wisconsin, Louisiana): whether the hours held 1960-66.
- 1967 and 1968 sessions: searched only in Alabama, South Carolina, Kentucky (the Journal of 1968), Montana (1968 ed.), North Carolina (1967 recodification) and New York (the newspapers). Every other State's rows end in 1966; a change of 1967-68 is not excluded. Washington's 7 a.m. proviso: the act not found (the 1965 extraordinary session?).
- Primaries: the register gives a primary's hours only where the compilation does (Connecticut, Delaware, Hawaii, Idaho, Kentucky, New Hampshire, New York, and the States whose section governs every election). The Southern party primaries, which the parties ran, are not in it.
- Local options: Alabama's county acts (searched in the Acts of 1962-67, not collected), New Hampshire's and Vermont's towns, Massachusetts's warrants, Rhode Island's schedule before 1965, Wisconsin's and Kansas's cities by class.
- Daylight time: the State acts named above (Minnesota 1957-59, Iowa 1965, Colorado 1965 and the referendum of Nov. 8, 1966, Virginia 1962, Pennsylvania 1965, Tennessee's prohibition, North Dakota's, Michigan's exemption of 1967 and the referendum of Nov. 5, 1968, Arizona Laws 1968, ch. 183, Hawaii's and Alaska's exemptions, Indiana's statute of 1967 and its 1969 bill); the ICC's zone orders of 1960-66 (49 C.F.R. pt. 139, not found open); the DOT's orders of 1967-69 moving the lines in Indiana, Kansas, Nebraska and North Dakota (the Federal Register on govinfo: the issues of Nov. 25, 1967, June 22, 1968, Jan. 16 and May 23, 1969 name them); the Book of the States, 1960-61 to 1968-69, which prints a table of polling hours (archive.org: borrow only; HathiTrust bot-checked).

## How the election-night range should use them

The owner's band, `election night`, 20:00 to 26:00 local (`BANDS` in `tools/bib/dayorder.py`), stands where no law is read. Where the register has a row for the State, the year and the election, the range should be drawn from the law, in the constituency's zone:

1. **From**: the latest closing hour the law allows anywhere in the constituency (`pollhours.closing(st, year, election)`: for a statewide race, the latest over the State's rules, local options included: Rhode Island 21:00, New York 21:00 from 1966, Massachusetts and New Hampshire's cities 20:00; for a district, its own class of place where the register names one). Returns are not complete before the last poll closes; the first returns come at the earliest closing, which is the range's floor only where an entry is about early returns, not the result.
2. **To**: six hours after, the span of the owner's band (8 p.m. to 2 a.m.), so a 7 p.m. closing runs to 1 a.m. and a 9 p.m. closing to 3 a.m.; a race called the next day is a record's matter, not the band's.
3. **The clock**: the zone of the constituency (the State's in `daylight-time.yaml`'s `zones`, the western zone for a State in two when the race is statewide, since its polls close last in UTC); the clock the election law names where it names one (eastern standard in Georgia, North Carolina to 1967, Pennsylvania, Michigan: standard time whatever the town kept); otherwise the local civil time, with daylight time as the register reads it for the State and date (`pollhours.clock(st, date)`), never tz's where the `tz` list says it disagrees. In November, standard time everywhere (above).
4. **Basis**: `range_basis: kind`, `time_band: election night`, `time_zone:` the zone, the law's hour noted in the register, not the entry's note (STYLE 5 of `tools/bib/dayorder.py`: a kind range adds nothing to the note); a range drawn from the law could instead take `range_basis: record` with the register's cite in the note, the owner to rule.

A sketch of the reader, for whoever builds it: `band_range(date, 'election night', zone)` with the band's start replaced by `closing()`'s hour and its end six hours later; a State with no row for the year falls back to the band.
