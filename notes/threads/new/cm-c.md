# Comecon: the placing of the entries on the list (cm-c)

What I read: all 307 entries of `notes/threads/new/cm-c-ids.tsv`, each line whole and each note (long notes to their first 260 characters, with links and card names stripped to shorten the reading), against the statement of `cal.thread.comecon`, rulings 58 to 62 and the owner's words of Nov. 26, 2026. The placings are in `notes/threads/new/cm-c.tsv`. Nothing in `lists/`, `th.yaml` or `sources/` was changed.

The rule I used: an entry is in where the act is a member's own dealing (Albania, Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia, the GDR, Hungary, Poland, Romania, Yugoslavia) with China, Moscow, another member or the West, or a member party's congress, a member's ambassador's call or credentials, its national-day reception or cable, its delegation to or from China, or a Comecon meeting. It is out where a member is the place or the route, one item among many in a longer act, a subject of a Chinese article or of the Sino-Soviet dispute, or a name in passing in another country's affair. Ruling 62's fifth default applied: Comecon is the primary thread only where the present thread is a holding place that does not fit.

Counts of the 307:
- In as primary: 1.
- In as secondary (added to `also:`): 189.
- Out: 117.
- Undecided: 0.

Kinds of entry the words found that are not the thread's business:
- 34: another country's affair, with a member named in passing (the Rometsch case, a Polish commissioner in Vietnam, Polish-American voters, Tito named in a poll or an advertisement, the missile-crisis ships).
- 30: China's own business or its dispute with Moscow, where a member is the subject of an article (the Yugoslav commentaries), a name in a list, or the occasion for a speech.
- 28: a member only as the place or route (the Warsaw ambassadorial talks, a stop in Poland, the Bucharest tanker, a visit to Bulgaria as the place of a speech).
- 25: one item among many in a longer act (Presidium sessions with a Comecon, GDR or Polish item among a dozen others, lists of ambassadors or foreign ministers who cabled, a press conference, delegations sent home).

The one primary placing: `cal.1963.europe-and-the-alliance` (Gheorghiu-Dej's visit to Yugoslavia, with Tito's return visit). I moved it to Comecon and dropped Europe and the alliance from `also:`, because the visit is between two members and has no alliance business.

Entries of `china-africa-tour` and `china-polemic` that fit stay under those threads with Comecon added; I did not move any China entry to Comecon as primary, since the China threads are not holding places.

Judgment calls the owner may overrule, applied the same way throughout:
- A Chinese national-day cable or reception for a member is in; a Chinese editorial or commentary about Yugoslavia is out. I first put one of these in, then changed it for the rule; the rows follow the rule.
- Mao's notes on the Yugoslav League plenum (July–Aug. 1962) are in, since the plenum is Yugoslavia's own act.
- Chen Yi's poems written in Albania are out; the rest of Zhou Enlai's Albanian visit (Dec. 31, 1963 to Jan. 9, 1964) is in.
- The Presidium sessions that name Gomułka, Ulbricht or Comecon among many items are out, except May 8, 1963 (Comecon the main item), Oct. 21 and 23, 1963 (Comecon meeting, Hungarian wheat, Zhivkov's letters), Dec. 30, 1962 (talks with Gomułka and Tito) and Sept. 4, 1963 (Yugoslavia).
- Kennedy's news conference of Jan. 24, 1963 is in for its first matter, the most-favored-nation treatment of Yugoslavia and Poland.
- The Presidium of Oct. 22, 1962 on the GDR delegation is in; that of Oct. 12 and 14, 1962, with a GDR item among many, is out.

Settled after the second reading: `cal.1963-10-14.china-leadership-2` (Zhou on the Metallurgy Ministry's summary of aid to Vietnam and 援阿). I read the page of the cited work (Zhou Enlai nianpu, II, 587, PDF page 590): it prints 援越 and 援阿 with no gloss and no footnote. The entry before it, Oct. 13, is Zhou receiving the Albanian friendship delegation and the Albanian delegation on scientific and technical cooperation, and in Chinese usage 援阿 is aid to Albania; Algeria is written out in full. So I read it as Albania and put the entry in as secondary. The reading rests on context, not on a gloss in the book, so the entry's "Check: 援阿, Albania or Algeria" should stay.

Second reading of the notes: I then read every note whole (106 of the 307 were longer than the 260 characters I first read; the rest I had read whole). No other row changed. The long notes only confirmed the placings: for example the note of May 15, 1962 names the meeting of seven parties on Comecon economic cooperation (in), that of July 25, 1962 a Polityka article on Polish farm cooperation (in), those of Dec. 25, 1962 and of Mar. 9, 1963 name the delegations and ambassadors among many (out), and the long Presidium notes list their many other items (out).

Disregarded under R0: nothing in the brief narrowed what is an entry; I judged only the thread's own business, as the brief says. The brief's statement says "associate Yugoslavia"; I applied it to all the dates.
