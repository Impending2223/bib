# The calendar's threads: what the statements leave unsaid

For whoever writes or extends a thread (person or agent). Not shown on the pages.

Every thread is its matter as Washington met it: what the President, the executive departments and agencies, the
Congress, and the courts did about it, said of it, or were told of it (the Public Papers, FRUS, the Congressional
Record, the Statutes). The owner found the phrase redundant in the statements, since the calendar's whole frame (the
Congresses, the Executive, the APP and FRUS lists) says it; it is kept here instead. Write a thread's statement as
what the thread is and its stations, without "as Washington met it", "and Washington", "for the administration".

Scopes taken out of the statements, kept here:

- Soviet Union: the Soviet Union as Washington met it, apart from Berlin, Cuba and testing (each its own thread).
- India and China: South Asia and Indonesia as Washington met them.
- King: Martin Luther King, Jr., and Washington: the President, the Attorney General, the Bureau (the statement
  keeps "The President, the Attorney General, the Bureau").
- Black nationalism: Black nationalism as Washington met it (the statement opens "Malcolm X and the Nation of Islam").
- Civil rights organizations: their campaigns (Albany, Birmingham, the Freedom Rides, Freedom Summer, Selma, the March) are in
  the place and law threads; King has his own; Malcolm X and the Nation of Islam are in Black nationalism.
- Africa: Africa as Washington met it, apart from the Congo (its own thread): South Africa, decolonization at the UN.
- Sit-ins: the student sit-ins of 1960 at lunch counters, an event of their own; other segregation in public places is
  in Desegregation: public places, the Freedom Rides in their own thread.
- Japan and Korea: the alliances as Washington met them: the treaties, the governments, the visits.
- Students: college and university students in politics: their organizations, demonstrations, and manifestos (the
  statement keeps the first clause).
- The States and the cities (Oct. 14, 2026, at the owner's request for State and local elections and politics): each
  event takes the narrowest thread. A race for governor: Governors, also the year's elections thread (Elections of
  1961, 1963, 1965 for the off years). A race for mayor, or for the body that governs a city: Mayors, likewise. A
  legislature's elections, control, apportionment and districting: Legislatures. A State constitution, its amendments,
  conventions, referenda and initiatives: State constitutions and referenda, also the topic's thread. Taxes, budgets,
  borrowing: State and city finance. Committees, laws, oaths and speaker bans against subversion: Antisubversion. Open
  housing, welfare, the schools and the rest: the topic's thread. What none of these holds: States and cities. An event
  of Alabama, Georgia, Mississippi or South Carolina alone takes that State's thread first. Each entry carries hidden
  tags for the States and cities it concerns (`state:ny`, `city:nyc`), for State threads or lists later; none yet beyond
  the four.

Order: th.yaml in alphabetical order of the threads' names, as shown; War on poverty under "poverty", Bobby Baker under
"Baker", Robert Kennedy under "Kennedy" (`THREAD_SORT_AS` in tools/bib/check.py; `check` warns otherwise [thread-order]).
