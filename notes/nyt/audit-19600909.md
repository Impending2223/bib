Audited Nov. 26, 2026, by Claude (Opus). The patch audited is `inbox/2026-11-26-nyt-19600909.yaml`, with its dating rows `notes/nyt/dating-2026-11-26-nyt-19600909.tsv` and the reader's note `notes/nyt/read-19600909.md`. R0 governed: nothing in the batch brief was taken to narrow what is an entry.

## What I read

I fetched all 44 PDFs of the Drive folder "NYT 1960-09-09" with curl, rendered each page at 144 dpi and read every item as an image, column by column, from headline to the last line. Eight files hold two pages. Two wide pages, the Council excerpts and the Democratic memorandum, I read in narrow strips. Nothing was left unread. The copies and images are deleted.

The clippings print no page numbers. A page is cited only where a "Continued on Page" line or a pointer on page 1 shows it: the memorandum's text at 15, the Congo notes at 4, the Council excerpts at 2.

## The reader's doubts, ruled

- The cites. The reader used `primary` with `text:` and no `on:`, so each cite would have stood for the whole line. Most lines say more than the paper does. I rewrote all 15 cites, and wrote nine more for the Kennedy train stops, the Sept. 7 news conference and the speeches the memorandum quotes. Each now names the part it bears out with `on:`, or says in a parenthetical what it does not bear out. Parentheticals no longer hold the paper's time words, the dateline or the reasoning for a date (R26a). Background the reader had put in parentheticals is now in the line, by `add-words`.
- The same-day flags. Every new entry that patch-check flags as "one event?" or "a duplicate of" is a distinct act, and each is to be kept at the merge (`./bib resolve ID --take keep`). The one true duplicate was the reader's `cal.1960-09-06.china-4`, Wang's proposal at the 100th Warsaw meeting. The calendar already holds that meeting as `cal.1960-09-06.china-3`, so I dropped the new entry, released its ledger claim, and added its words to the existing entry.
- The Belgium–UN letters. The clipping holds all four letters and the addendum whole, letter D included; nothing is cut at the edge. I rewrote D from its full text and took out the reader's Check. The cable of Sept. 4 told three acts at once; I split out the Albertville embarkation and the Kitona visit. The reader's quotation of the note verbale of Sept. 8 was not the note's text; it now quotes the addendum as printed.
- The short wire items. All were held to the image. Most stand. I corrected the Cuban expulsion ("By Sept. 8"), Pedraza's title and plot, McClellan's reason, Morrow's words, Keating's hour ("tonight") and the Richmond poll's figures. I took the stray "(AP)." and "(UPI)." out of the notes.
- The Lissner article. It has no dateline, so its undated acts are dated by its report (R15a), and its days come from the article where it gives them. I took out "The *Times* writer reports it," the "Santo Domingo, then Ciudad Trujillo" and the readers' reasoning from the lines. I split the Radio Caribe account into its acts. The observers' view and "no functioning opposition party" are now one standing entry on the paper's day, with no place.
- The UN items. The Wadsworth swearing-in, his credentials and the Dominican debate are the business of the UN, not of Khrushchev's visit. I added a thread, `united-nations`, by an `add` of `cal.thread.united-nations`, and filed the swearing-in and credentials under it. Entries that were already claimed under another thread keep that thread and list `united-nations` in `also`: the Dominican debate, Ortona's consultations, Nasser's plans, the Herter address and Wadsworth's arms remarks under `testing`.
- The missing places. A gazetteer key can be added by a `place-key` op, so I added `dakar` and `albertville`. The Kremlin is `moscow`. Scituate is only the site of WRUL, not where the agreement was made, so it takes no place. The Lissner entries have no place, since the article gives none for the acts but the riot.
- The six Checks. I kept the Checks on Dunsmuir's hour, the Katanga shipment's day and size, the Hays year, the Senate's day and the West Berliners. I reworded them without the paper's time words. The Check on the Atlanta photograph is replaced by the breakfast entry.
- The Brecht award. R18 takes out only sport, art or entertainment with no trace in politics or policy. A prize for the best book on government and politics, given at the political scientists' meeting, is neither, so it is an entry. No thread fits it. I filed it under `elections-1960`, beside Marshall's remarks at the same meeting, and the owner may refile it.
