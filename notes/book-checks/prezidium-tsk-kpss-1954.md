## What the volumes print, by their prefaces

- Vol. I prints every declassified note, not every session. The archeographic preface (Afiani and Panina, I, 8): "В сборнике публикуются все рассекреченные черновые протокольные записи" (the collection publishes all the declassified draft protocol notes) kept 1954–64, thirteen files, and 22 stenograms of 1958–64, declassified for the book; one note of October 1964 stays in APRF's "special file", copies given; some stenograms named in the documents were neither declassified nor transferred to RGANI (I, 8–9). The editor's foreword (Fursenko, I, 6): Malin's notes "охватывают далеко не все состоявшиеся заседания" (cover far from all the sessions held); stenographers were rarely let in. The preface (I, 14): no system in what was taken down can be found, nor which other stenograms were made. So 1960 has nineteen numbers (207–225) and nothing between June 2 and Oct. 11; Jan. 20, Jan. 29, Mar. 12, May 12, June 16, July 17 and Dec. 7, 1960 have no note.
- The resolutions volumes are a selection keyed to vol. I. Vol. II's preface (Afiani and Fursenko, II, 8–10), the only statement of the rule: the task was "выявление и публикация постановлений Президиума ЦК и материалов к ним, которые нашли отражение в черновых записях В.Н. Малина" (to find and publish the resolutions and their materials reflected in Malin's notes), from the signed protocols (RGANI, f. 3, op. 10, 14; also op. 8, 12) and APRF's "thematic files"; some decisions were taken "без записи в протокол" (without entry in the protocol); the search "не всегда давал положительный результат. Ряд документов не был выявлен, часть документов до сих пор не рассекречена" (did not always succeed: some documents not found, some still not declassified), but for "the overwhelming majority" of the notes the resolutions were found and printed (II, 9). Vol. III has no preface of its own: its annotation (III, 4) repeats the rule ("постановления ... по беловым протоколам ... и другие документы, обсуждение которых нашло отражение в черновых и стенографических записях": the resolutions from the signed protocols and the other documents whose discussion the notes and stenograms reflect), and calls itself the third and last volume, a documentary supplement to the first; the editor's foreword (III, 7–8) claims a "последовательной и полной подборке" (consistent and full selection) without restating the rule.
- What that leaves out, by the volumes themselves: every resolution on an item Malin did not note (a session's other items: Protocol 261 of Jan. 28, 1960 reached item XXI; III prints VI and XXI, 75–83); decisions by poll, unless the notes name them; documents still secret, which the notes of III name again and again ("находятся на секретном хранении": III, 1005, 1008, 1020, 1025, 1026, 1028, 1031, 1034–36, 1038, 1054; among them the reply to Kennedy and Macmillan of May 7, 1963, the Warsaw Pact papers of July 1963, Dobrynin's telegram of Sept. 21, 1964 and the instructions to him); and the "особая папка" (special file) decisions, printed only as "Решение – особая папка" (decision: special file: III, 117, 283), their texts never in RGANI's protocol (III, 1001 n.1) and kept in APRF's thematic files, f. 3, op. 65 for Cuba, which the editors cite and do not print (I, 1100 nn.2, 4). Nearly every Cuban record Fursenko and Naftali cite from APRF, f. 3, op. 65, is of this kind.
- Fursenko and Naftali's *Khrushchev's Cold War* cites the protocols it took from these volumes as "AOK" (registered as printed here, `x.1962-10-14.presidium-protocol-59` and the rest); their *"One Hell of a Gamble"* (1997) cites APRF's copies, before the volumes.
