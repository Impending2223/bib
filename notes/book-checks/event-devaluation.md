What the records say about devaluing the dollar, in brief: every public word, 1960–68, is the $35 pledge (Philadelphia, the message of Feb. 6, 1961, the Rockefeller letter, the news conference of July 23, 1962, the Fund in 1963, the message of Feb. 10, 1965, the Business Council, Dec. 6, 1967); in private, the Treasury (Dillon, Roosa, Fowler) against any change, as a reserve currency's devaluation would be matched (Roosa, by Meltzer 363–64) and would "put us in a position similar to Brazil or Argentina" (Dillon, Feb. 11, 1963); the Council and the White House staff willing to weigh it (Tobin, by Gavin 98; Kaysen and Gordon, July 18, 1962; Bator, July 6, 1966); the Federal Reserve's staff drew it up (Plan X, Sept. 21, 1966); the counterparts pressed for a higher gold price (Couve, May 25, 1963; Larre, Nov. 1968; France publicly, by Rostow, Apr. 2, 1968), the Germans and Italians against; the Dillon Committee unanimously against (Mar. 8, 1968); the answer of March 1968 was the two-tier market, not a new price.
