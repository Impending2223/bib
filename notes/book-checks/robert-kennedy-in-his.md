What the book is: the Kennedy Library's recorded interviews with Robert Kennedy, the transcript re-edited against the
tapes (Editorial Note, xi): Schlesinger, Washington, Feb. 27, 1965 (pp. 5–32); Martin, McLean, Feb. 29, 1964 (33–57);
Lewis, Marshall present, New York, Dec. 4, and McLean, Dec. 6 and 22, 1964 (66–230); Martin, Mar. 1 (McLean), Apr. 13,
Apr. 30 and May 14, 1964 (New York) (239–423); Stewart, July 20 and Aug. 1, 1967, on 1952–56, printed apart without notes
(431–53). The book does not mark where each session within the Lewis set or the Martin set begins. The editors' notes
(58–60, 231–34, 424–27) identify persons and date events; their brackets in the text are theirs. Every cite is Robert
Kennedy's account, and the entries say so where it is one man's.

## How it was read, and what remains

The dated sentences of the whole text (374) were drawn out by script and read; the pages they stand on were read for
each event taken, and each event set against the calendar of its day (all threads) and the ledger. Not read page by
page: the bulk of the Lewis interviews on civil rights (pp. 102–230: voting, the judges, the FBI and Hoover, the
committees, housing, Oxford, Birmingham, the 1963 bill, Baldwin, the March), and of the Martin interviews from 1961 to
1963 (pp. 258–393) beyond the dated passages, and the Vietnam and "On His Own" sections beyond 396–413. Those pages are
Kennedy's undated recollection of events the calendar mostly holds; a further run should read them for the cites and
the informal record (R7) they bear.
