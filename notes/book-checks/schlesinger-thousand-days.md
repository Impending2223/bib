## The eight citations

Held, pinpoint added (the card moved out of "To check"): 1961-01-20 (1–5, 165–66, 170), 1961-01-30 (167–68).

Held in part, or the book gives more or another account:

- `cal.1960-11-09.transition-and-staff` (the first appointments, Nov. 9–10). Schlesinger, 124–25, 127: Hoover and Dulles
  kept in the papers the morning after a dinner on the Wednesday night after the election (Nov. 9); Clifford named and his
  memorandum delivered; Sorensen and Salinger announced, O'Donnell a Special Assistant (not appointments secretary). The
  entry's Check (which appointments Nov. 9, which Nov. 10) stays, with Schlesinger's account added to it.
- `cal.1961-02-11.cuba` (his memorandum of Feb. 11). Holds; 240 has it sent the day before he left for Buenos Aires,
  and quotes it ("this would be your first dramatic foreign policy initiative"). The entry's "Check: an earlier release"
  is answered: "Made public, in part: Schlesinger, *Thousand Days*, 240 (1965); in full, FRUS 1961–63, X (1997)."

- `cal.1961-03-30.cuba` (Fulbright's memorandum). Schlesinger, 251: written Mar. 29 with Pat Holt of the committee's staff,
  given to Kennedy the next day; Fulbright invited on the plane to Palm Beach. The Check now says so; the memorandum itself
  (Kennedy Library) still to read.
- `cal.1961-04-28.opinion` (Gallup after the Bay of Pigs). Schlesinger, 292, has "an unprecedented 82 per cent"; the entry,
  from *The Gallup Poll*, 83. The note, which already says "Some compilations give 82", now names him for it.
- `cal.1961-06-29.africa` (Kennedy to Nkrumah on the Volta). Schlesinger, 571, puts the letter in July. FRUS 1961–63, XXI,
  doc. 227 dates it June 29: the entry stands.
- `cal.1961-11-26.transition-and-staff` (the Thanksgiving reshuffle). 443–44 hold for Bowles, Ball, Harriman and Rostow;
  Kaysen is not named.

Points to watch:

- Mar. 31, 1961 (Bowles to Rusk): FRUS, X, doc. 75. Schlesinger (250–51) has Rusk reassure Bowles, leaving him to think
  the project whittled down to an infiltration, and file the memorandum; "profoundly disturbing" is FRUS's, not his.
- Apr. 13, 1961 (Miró at the Century): Miró later claimed Berle promised 10,000 American troops; Schlesinger's notes have
  Berle promise arms for the Cubans who rose (264–65). The entry gives both.
- Oct. 22, 1962 (the congressional leaders): Schlesinger gives five o'clock (812); the Miller Center's recording, 5:30.
  Check in the entry.
- Oct. 23, 1962 (Stevenson to the Security Council): the statement's text, the *Bulletin*, Nov. 12, 1962, 723 (read on
  archive.org's copy).
- Dec. 5, 1962 (the letter to Stevenson): FRUS's microfiche supplement, doc. 569, dates it Dec. 5; Schlesinger gives no
  day for its release. Check in the entry.
- Nov. 24, 1961 (civil defense at Hyannis Port): *Thousand Days*, 748, "the Friday after Thanksgiving 1961"; *Robert
  Kennedy and His Times*, 429, "a Hyannis Port conclave" over Thanksgiving. The two agree on Robert Kennedy's remark,
  nearly word for word ("There's no problem here — we can just station Father McHugh ..."). Check in the entry on the day.
- Apr. 18, 1961 (lunch with Reston): the remark "Bobby should be in CIA" is in both books (*Thousand Days*, 276; *Robert
  Kennedy*, 452, "while the Cubans were still fighting on the beachhead"); they agree.
