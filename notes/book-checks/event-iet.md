### What the books say of the event

- **Gavin** treats the tax in one sentence: after the meeting of Apr. 18, 1963, Kennedy's memorandum to the Cabinet Committee (Apr. 20) has the Treasury prepare opinion for a drawing on the Fund, "In addition, the interest equalization tax was developed to discourage the use of U.S. capital markets by foreign borrowers" (103). The appendix names "the Interest Equalization Act of 1963" as the first of the controls on foreign investment that ended in the program of 1968 (204): the act is the Interest Equalization Tax Act, 78 Stat. 809, signed Sept. 2, 1964, the tax running from July 19, 1963; settled by the record, no Check. Nothing on the Canadian exemption (1963 or 1968), the bills of 1964, 1965 and 1967, or the voluntary program of 1965 ("voluntary" first at 173, of late 1967). The 1967–68 measures: 172–78 (the gold pool after devaluation; the Cabinet Committee in December; the program of Jan. 1, 1968; the officials sent to Europe, Japan and Canada, 178), all but the missions already pinned by earlier readers. The devaluation question: Acheson's cover letter of Feb. 25, 1963 (98 n.22, already a Check on `cal.1963-02-26.economy`); after sterling's devaluation, ending the pool would raise the official price of gold, "validating de Gaulle's monetary policy" (172); the fear of going off gold (174): no dated event not already entered.
