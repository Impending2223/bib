# Herbert Stein, *The Fiscal Revolution in America* (1969): the calendar's citations held up

Card `w.stein-fiscal-revolution-in-america` (1969). Read Oct. 24, 2026, from the owner's copy, which is the AEI Press's
revised edition (Washington, 1990): a republication of the 1969 text (the preface, xi, thanks the University of
Chicago Press "for permitting the republication of this book") with new front matter, pp. xi–xxiv. The contents of
the reprint (ch. 15 at 372, ch. 16 at 385, ch. 17 at 422, the Epilogue at 454, notes at 469, index at 515) are those of
the 1969 text, so the pinpoints are given to the 1969 card; Check them against a copy of 1969 if one is reached.

1959–64; the Epilogue (454–68) passes over 1965–68 in a page. Stein was the Committee for Economic Development's
research director; his account of the CED's statements is an insider's.

1960; the debt limit, June 8, 1960; the Economic Reports of Jan. 20, 1964 and Jan. 28, 1965.

The cases that are not a plain "holds":

- `cal.1960-03.economy` (Burns warns Nixon). Stein, 366: "In February of 1960 Arthur Burns came to the opinion ... that
  the economy was heading for another decline. He urged upon Mr. Nixon ..." The entry's March rests on Nixon's *Six
  Crises*. Added: "Check: Stein, 366, has Burns come to his view in February 1960."
- `cal.1961-01-05.economy` (Samuelson's task force). Stein was cited as "The thread's books"; now with its pages
  (386, 388), which bear the entry out.
- `cal.1961-07-25.economy` (no tax increase for Berlin). Stein, 391, 403: Kennedy "tentatively decided upon a $3 billion
  tax increase" and was dissuaded by "his economic advisers," citing Heller, *New Dimensions*, 32. The entry's "Check
  who argued against it" stands; Heller's page joins the To check.
- The tax message of Apr. 20, 1961 (new entry). Stein, 390, gives the proposed credit as "8 percent of business
  investment in excess of depreciation allowances." The message (1961 Pub. Papers 290) asks 15 percent above
  depreciation, 6 percent between half of depreciation and all of it, and 10 percent on the first $5,000; Zelizer, 181,
  186, has 15. The entry follows the message.
- The CED's statement *Reducing Tax Rates for Production and Growth* (new entry, Dec. 14, 1962). Stein, 418, has it
  released the morning of Kennedy's Economic Club speech, Dec. 14; Zelizer, 198, "On December 13." The entry keeps
  Stein's day with a Check.
- Mills's speech opening the House debate, Sept. 24, 1963 (the "two roads"), quoted by both books (Stein, 449–50;
  Zelizer, 206): a step of the bill's course in `congress/bills.yaml`, not entered.
- The meeting of Nov. 25, 1963 (Johnson, the Treasury, the Council and the Budget: "about one hundred billion
  dollars"), Stein, 452–53, from Evans and Novak: another patch (book-dallek-giant) adds it as `cal.1963-11-25.economy`.
  Stein, 452–53, and Zelizer, 202 (Dillon's and Heller's memoranda of Nov. 25), should join its note after the merge.
