What the book is for the calendar: the Justice Department, 1961–64, by theme, not date (Hoover and the FBI; organized
crime and wiretapping; civil rights and the theory of federalism; the southern judges; the Solicitor General; the Bay of
Pigs ransom; Keogh, Chacharis and Landis; Hoffa). The author's interviews are its main source, unattributed by note
(Author's Note, xi). What it adds: the Department's own papers, printed or quoted at length: the transcripts of Robert
Kennedy's and Burke Marshall's calls with Barnett, Sept. 17–30, 1962, and Marshall's unfiled affidavit; the memoranda of
the reapportionment cases (Terris, Rubin and Glickstein, Greene, Cox, Katzenbach); the documents of the Hoover–Kennedy
exchange of December 1966; the ransom's papers.

How the list was made: every sentence of the text holding a month, a weekday or a year 1959–69 (515 sentences) was read
with its page, and the pages around each dated event read whole; each event set against the calendar's entries of its
day in every thread (a fresh dump) and against the pending patches in `inbox/` (none at the time).
