**The notes.** The chronology has a few footnotes (marked ① etc.), glosses of a person, a place or a play (p. 836, the play *Yūzuru*); they name no work. It gives no archive or source for its items; documents it names with a date (letters, telegrams, treaties, articles) are in the entries' notes; none was registered as a witness row in run 1 (see Leads).

**How the run was made.** Each dated item of the nianpu, in the book's order, was set against the calendar's entries of its day (tree and ledger, all threads, by a script over the calendar files and the inbox's pending patches, the Peng Zhen run 1 patch among them): where the act is in (chiefly the Zhou chronology's and the *Zhou Enlai waijiao huodong dashiji*'s entries of Zhou's receptions, talks and trips, with Chen Yi "在座" or "陪同"; the *Waijiao dashiji*'s telegrams; the Mao and Liu chronologies), a `cite` with the nianpu's page and its detail in the parenthetical (Chen Yi's part, the hour, the place), and an `edit` setting the part of the day or the hour where the entry had none; where the hour disagrees, a `variant`; where not, a new entry (`china-leadership`, `also:` the region's thread, `polity:prc` and the foreign party's polity, `name:` tags for persons with a name entry). Public acts (receptions, banquets, airport welcomes, rallies, signings, telegrams of greeting) carry no visibility ruling; Chen Yi's private talks inside the leadership `vis:only:prc` with "Made public: 1995." Times: a clock hour as `time` (UTC, from the place's zone: Asia/Shanghai, or the zone abroad, Asia/Kolkata, Asia/Yangon, Asia/Kathmandu, Asia/Phnom_Penh, Asia/Bangkok for the DRV, Asia/Ulaanbaatar, Asia/Kabul, Asia/Almaty); a part of the day as a band (上午 morning, 下午 afternoon, 晚上 evening, 夜 night, 中午 workday midday, 傍晚 after-workday daytime, 晨 early morning), with "The words:" in the note. `place:` only where the text names the city or a building of it (the Great Hall of the People, the Beijing Hotel, the Cultural Palace of Nationalities, the Summer Palace); a bare "the airport" or "the embassy" is left unplaced.
