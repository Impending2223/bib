# Harry McPherson, *A Political Education* (1972): a first reading against the calendar

Card `w.mcpherson-political-education`. Read Oct. 29, 2026 (batch 3, staff memoirs) from the owner's copy (Drive; the
Internet Archive's scan of the Boston Public Library's copy, Little, Brown 1972, 488 PDF pages, a text layer): printed
page = PDF page − 12 throughout, the plates (237–44) counted in the folios; checked against the page image at p. 430 and
the running heads at 9, 39, 89, 189, 289, 389. Patch: `inbox/2026-10-29-b3-mcpherson.yaml` (b3-mcpherson, 72 ops: 43
edits, 29 new entries). Records: `sources/cited-records/mcpherson-political-education.yaml` (none: see below).

What the book is: McPherson's memoir, Senate staff 1956–63 (Johnson's, then the Policy Committee's counsel under
Mansfield), the Army and State 1963–65, the White House 1965–69 (special assistant, then special counsel and chief
speechwriter). It is its author's account throughout; every entry from it says so where it matters ("by McPherson's
account"). It has no notes and no bibliography: a handful of footnotes give a newspaper, an article or a book quoted
(Evans and Novak; White; Schlesinger; Rainwater and Yancey; Oberdorfer; Abel and Kalb), none a record. So no `via`
rows and no cited-records file entries. McPherson quotes his own memoranda at length, dated to the minute (Sept. 9,
Oct. 5, Nov. 8, 1966; May 14, 1966; Apr. 19, 1967; June 14, 1968; Sept. 12, 1966; Aug. 1, 1967): each is now an entry
of its own (the owner's ruling on the papers of government), the book its only source read. Where FRUS prints the
memorandum (IV, doc. 136; V, doc. 197; VI, doc. 152), FRUS is cited first.

## Where the book differs (each noted in the entry, "Check:")

- `cal.1964-01-09.latin-america`: McPherson gives eighteen Panamanians and six or seven soldiers dead (221); the entry,
  about 22 and four (Johnson 20, Dallek 24 and four).
- `cal.1965-03-26.civil-rights-the-executive`: McPherson recalls writing "hooded band of bigots" (330); the Public
  Papers print "a hooded society of bigots."
- `cal.1968-02-17.vietnam-tet`: McPherson lands the party on the *Constellation* the night of Feb. 17, the remarks the
  next morning (425–26); the entry has Feb. 18 aboard.
- `cal.1968-03-22.vietnam-205000`: McPherson's "extended lunch in the Mansion" names Wheeler, Helms and (he thinks)
  Taylor, not Clark, Fortas or McGeorge Bundy (431); FRUS VI, doc. 149 (Christian's notes) gives the entry's list.
- `cal.1968-04-07.civil-disorders-king`: the entry (from Longley) has Johnson tell McPherson that any new man would get
  a honeymoon from Congress, "But not me," after King's death; McPherson puts those words at a lunch with him and
  Califano in mid-March, before the withdrawal (427–28; new entry `cal.1968-03.elections-1968-2`).
- `cal.1967-06-05.middle-east-six-day-war` (new): McPherson dates his landing at Tel Aviv 3 a.m., June 6, and the war
  three hours later (414); FRUS XIX, doc. 158's note has telegram 3937 of June 5 on his meeting with Amit and Barbour.
- `cal.1968-10-31.vietnam`, `cal.1968-04-15.japan-and-korea`, etc.: no disagreement.
- Not in the calendar but noted: McPherson says he was sworn in as Assistant Secretary of State on his thirty-fifth
  birthday, the night the convention learned Humphrey was the choice (228: Aug. 26, 1964); the Executive roster has
  him from Aug. 23, 1964. And he joined the White House staff in March 1965 (235–36, 389); the roster's
  `wh.special-assistants` begins 1965-08. Leads below.
- The tariffs (291–92): McPherson has Johnson "roll back" the window glass and watch tariffs in 1967; the Public Papers'
  statement of Oct. 12, 1967 (1967 Pub. Papers 923) extends the sheet glass escape-clause duty to 1970. The rollback
  McPherson means is probably January 1967's (not read): a lead, not entered.
