# Wofford, *Of Kennedys and Kings* (1980): the calendar read against the book

Harris Wofford, *Of Kennedys and Kings: Making Sense of the Sixties* (1980), card `w.wofford-of-kennedys-and-kings`. Read Nov. 5–6, 2026, from the owner's Drive copy (file id 1lbcfunlMXDxQel1H7kSiJVkU6XVVWMZd, fetched through Drive's public download address, sharing unchanged): the Internet Archive's scan (ofkennedyskingsm0000woff_e1f6), Farrar, Straus and Giroux, first printing, 520 PDF pages with a text layer (read with `pdftotext -layout`, no OCR needed). Offset: printed page = PDF page − 12, checked at pp. 8, 38, 88, 188, 288, 388 and 468 (the folios printed at the foot of each page); constant through the book (no plates). The copy was deleted when the reading ended.

The book has no source notes: a few footnotes, nearly all asides, cite Miroff, Brauer, Bowles's *Promises to Keep* and Robert Kennedy's oral history. Its account rests on Wofford's own part (the 1960 campaign's Civil Rights Section, the talent search, the White House, 1961–62; the Peace Corps, 1961–66; Selma and Chicago, 1965 and 1968), on what Shriver, Bowles, Louis Martin, Bernhard, Moyers and others told him, and on the papers it quotes, chiefly his own memorandums and letters to the President. Witnesses: the papers it quotes for the new entries' claims, with the passages that quote them, are in `inbox/witnesses/2026-11-05-b4-wofford-of.yaml` (Wofford's memorandum of December 1960, his memorandum to Robert Kennedy on the FBI of January 1961, Shriver's report of Feb. 28, 1961, Wofford's memorandum on Bowles of July 15, 1961, his letters of January and March 1962); no by-work file, the book having no notes to key edges to. The passage witnesses of Wofford's pages registered from Branch's notes (`x.1980.wofford-of-kennedys-and-king-p…`) are left as they stand: they were cited as a work, not as a record read.

## How it was read

Pp. 11–242 (Parts I and II: the King call, the 1960 campaign, the talent search, civil rights in the White House, Selma, the FBI and King) were read in full, page by page. Pp. 243–284 (the Peace Corps' founding) were read in full; pp. 285–468 (the War on Poverty and national service, Vietnam and Bowles, Robert Kennedy, Chicago 1968, the epilogue, the appendix on the Civil Rights Commission) by their dated passages: every sentence holding a date, a month or a season, or Wofford's own part, found by search of the text and read in its context of a page or more. Pp. 1–10 (the prologue) and the appendix (pp. 461–484, the Commission of 1957–60) are before the span but for the passages noted under Passed over. Each pinpoint below was read against the page as printed; quotations were copied from the text layer and held to the page image where the OCR was in doubt.
