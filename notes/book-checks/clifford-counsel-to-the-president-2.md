What the book is: Clifford's memoir, written with Holbrooke: the Symington campaign and Kennedy's transition (1960),
Kennedy's lawyer and the Foreign Intelligence Advisory Board (1961–63, its chairman from April 1963), Johnson's
informal adviser (1963–67), Secretary of Defense (Mar. 1, 1968–Jan. 20, 1969). Clifford's own account throughout; every
entry from it says so where it is his alone ("by Clifford's account"). The notes cite, for 1968, Tom Johnson's notes of
the White House meetings (most printed in FRUS 1964–68, VI–VII), the 8:30 Group's notes by Elsey and Pursley (private),
and the back-channel cables in Westmoreland's papers. FRUS is cited first wherever it prints the meeting or the paper;
where Clifford differs from it, a Check.

The owner's ruling on the papers of government (brief-batch3 rule 5) was applied: the staff papers, cables and
intelligence letters the book treats are entries of their own, dated by the paper (McCone's letter of Apr. 28, 1965;
Raborn's of May 8; Clifford's *Liberty* report, July 18, 1967; Hoopes's letter, Feb. 13, 1968; MAC 1901; CJCS 02721;
Wheeler's CM-3098-68 and JCS 2767; Nitze's letter of Mar. 16; the Warnke–Pursley memorandum; Abrams's 7404 and MACV 7600;
Clifford's memorandum to Wheeler of June 8; Saigon 40788). Nothing in this patch was passed over as "below the level".
