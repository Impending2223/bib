# A report and the words it reports: the frame ruling (Nov. 13–14, 2026)

The owner, Nov. 13, 2026, on a Soviet record of a partner's words: "Strictly speaking, 'Alekseyev to Moscow: ' is Soviet-only, but 'Guevara says . . .' is Soviet-and-Cuban. (I don't mind having different levels of visibility in a single entry. I like it!)" And: "Don't make it a rule to split entries mixing a private Soviet act with its public form." The visibility layer (`tools/bib/vis.py`, STYLE 8) now takes three rulings:
- `vis:only:ussr+cuba` (as before): at the time these polities knew, and only these. With `@` and unit letters it governs those units alone (`vis:only:ussr@A`, `vis:only:ussr+cuba@B-D`, `vis:only:ussr@A.C`); a unit's ruling wins over the entry's.
- `vis:frame:ussr`: the first unit opens with a report's frame up to a colon ("Alekseyev to Moscow:", "The KGB in Havana to the Center:"); the frame is the report's own, known to the named polities alone; the rest of the entry is what was reported, ruled by the entry's (or the units') `vis:only:`.
- `vis:from:us:1962-02-10`: a polity learned later, from that day (not needed in this pass unless a row plainly calls for it: say so instead).

## Your rows
A JSON list (the lead names the file): the 92 calendar entries that carry a `vis:only:` ruling and whose `c` opens with words up to a colon (`id`, the ruling, the frame, the start of the rest). Most are not reports: "Castro to Alekseyev:" is a talk both knew; "The Presidium:" a session; "Khrushchev to Castro, by letter:" a letter its addressee read. Read each entry whole (`./bib show ID`, a few at a time; its `c`, `n` and the visibility lines) and rule:

1. **A report and its matter** (a Soviet official's or service's report, cable, letter or dispatch to Moscow, the Center, the Presidium or a minister, whose matter is what someone outside the Soviet government said, did or wanted): add `vis:frame:ussr` (the reporter's polity; `vis:frame:gdr` for an East German report, and so on). Then rule the matter: the polities whose people said or did it, plus the USSR (which had it from them or from its source). "Alekseyev to Moscow: Guevara says ..." is `vis:only:ussr+cuba` with `vis:frame:ussr`. "The KGB in Washington to the Center, quoting three congressional staff members: ..." is `vis:only:ussr+us` with `vis:frame:ussr` (the staff knew their own words). Where the matter is the Soviet officials' own assessment, a forecast, or a source's secret not said by any partner ("an invasion of Cuba imminent"), the entry stays `vis:only:ussr` and takes no frame: say why.
2. **Units of different matter.** Where one unit is the partner's words and another the Soviet reporter's own comment or a Soviet decision, rule the units: `vis:only:ussr@C` beside the entry's `vis:only:ussr+cuba`, or the like. Units are `c` split at sentence ends and semicolons (`./bib show` prints them A, B, C ...).
3. **Not a report** (a talk, a session, a letter delivered, a meeting): no change.
4. **A ruling now wrong**: an entry ruled `vis:only:ussr` whose matter is a partner's words (`cal.1962-10-29.cuba`, Alekseyev on seeing Castro: the audit made it `ussr`; under the owner's ruling it is a frame `ussr` and the matter `ussr+cuba`): remove the old tag and add the new.

Write each change with the `tag` op: `- tag: {id: cal.1960-02-10.cuba, add: ['vis:frame:ussr']}`; `- tag: {id: cal.1962-10-29.cuba, add: ['vis:frame:ussr', 'vis:only:ussr+cuba'], remove: ['vis:only:ussr']}`. Quote every tag that holds a colon or `@`. Never change `c`, `n`, the thread or other tags.
