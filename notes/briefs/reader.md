# The reader's brief

**Draft of Oct. 30, 2026 (second draft, after the comparison in `reader-comparison.md`), for the owner to read. Not
yet in force.** Until the owner approves it, the rules in force are CLAUDE.md's and the batch briefs', the latter kept
in `notes/briefs/superseded/` (superseded on approval). Once approved, this is the one text every agent that reads a
source against the calendar, or drafts calendar entries, works from; a batch brief adds only its assignment (the
sources and their copies, the entries, the patch's name, who else is at work). CLAUDE.md keeps the series' conventions
(the files, the forms, the build) and points here for the reader's rules; the owner's standing rules on reading go
here, not into CLAUDE.md.

Each rule is numbered (R1, R2 ...). Where each comes from is the table at the foot, "The rulings": the owner's ruling,
dated, with the owner's words, or "house rule" where the rule is the series' own practice. The owner's messages are in
`notes/owner-brief.md`. To amend a rule: change its text here and add a row to the table. Never append a ruling to a
rule's text.

Where a rule says "CLAUDE.md, X", the convention is there, under that heading, and is not repeated here.

## A. The work

**R1. What the calendar is.** A calendar of executive and legislative events, and of any other events that left a
trace, or should have left a trace, on the political and policy histories of the Kennedy and Johnson Administrations,
their Congresses, or those of their members; at least month by month. Its span: Jan. 6, 1960, to Jan. 20, 1969, and
the primary seasons of 1972 and 1976. Each entry is a memory aid and a bibliographic record, not a narrative: a brief
statement and its pointers, names and events. Each event carries a bibliography, or points to the entry that does.

**R2. The threads.** Every thread is its matter as Washington and its counterparts met it; "should have left a trace"
takes in what Washington did not see. The threads, their scopes and the subthreads: `lists/cal/th.yaml` and
`notes/threads.md`; the conventions (subthreads, ghosts, no name beginning "The"): CLAUDE.md, "Entries". A reader
proposes a new thread or subthread by patch; never writes the thread index.

**R3. A reading run.** For each source, in order:
1. The cites the calendar already makes to it (`./bib works show <card>`): hold each up (R33).
2. The whole source against the calendar over its span (Part B): each event the calendar holds that the source bears
   on takes its cite (R29); each event it lacks is a new entry.
3. What the source rests on (Part E).
4. What you pass over (Part F).
5. The patch, the book-check file (`notes/book-checks/<card without w.>.md`), the report (Part G).
A run may be stopped at any time: finish each source's patch before the next source.

**R4. Read it; nothing from memory.** Every date, figure, name and quotation from a page you have read. What you
recall and have not verified is a lead (R50), never an entry. A copy's page you have not checked against the image
is not yet read (R54).

## B. What is an entry

**R5. The test.** A dated event within the span that a thread follows, or should, is an entry. Reading a work, take
what the work dates and treats as an event: its notice is the test, whether or not the calendar holds the event yet.
R6 to R16 name the kinds of event readers have passed over; each is an entry.

**R6. The public record.** Messages, addresses, press conferences, orders, proclamations, signings, statutes, votes,
hearings, reports, decisions, elections, nominations and appointments.

**R7. The informal record.** For a person with a public role (executive, legislative, policy, foreign relations, the
administration of justice, the campaigns and the parties' communications): informal meetings, calls, conversations
and letters on public business or politics; lobbying; agreements and bargains; the planning of policy, personnel,
operations and communications; strategy sessions; appointments settled; in the West Wing, the members' offices, the
campaign headquarters. Their record is mostly memoirs, biographies, oral histories, recordings and diaries: date each
by its source, cite it by page, and name whose account it is where it is one participant's (R24).

**R8. The papers of government.** A staff or planning paper, a White House staff memorandum, an ambassador's,
commander's or consulate's cable, an intelligence memorandum or estimate: each an entry, dated by the paper, the copy
FRUS prints cited first where it prints one.

**R9. The parties and the members.** Never "minor": the position-taking, messaging, organization, communication,
policy formation and campaigning of either party out of the White House and of any member of Congress, leader or not:
a party's statements, papers, declarations and platforms; its committees' and leaderships' meetings and decisions; the
founding and doings of its factions and allied groups (the Ripon Society, the Free Society Association, Young Americans
for Freedom, the American Conservative Union, the Democratic Study Group); and the dated public word (a speech,
statement, press conference, interview, article or column under the person's own name) of a member, a governor, a
party officer or a party figure out of office (Eisenhower, Nixon, Goldwater, Rockefeller, Romney, Scranton, Percy,
Reagan) on policy or politics. Threads: Republican Party, its subthread In Congress, Democratic Party, Conservative
movement.

**R10. The counterparts.** Every authority, movement or armed group that Washington or its counterparts dealt with,
or later would, as a matter of foreign affairs, ally or adversary, recognized or not:
- the sovereigns: Hanoi, Peking, Moscow, Saigon, Bonn, London, Havana, and every other;
- the authorities without recognition: a secessionist state, a de facto regime, a government in exile (Biafra,
  Katanga, Rhodesia);
- the parties, movements, factions, officers' circles, insurgents, guerrillas, militias and bands, in power or out of
  it, in arms or not (the NLF, the Ba'ath, Fatah and the PLO, the Muslim Brotherhood, the Kurds, the communist parties:
  the PCI, the PCF, the JCP, the PKI).
Their decisions, meetings, papers, orders, envoys and public word on a matter a thread follows, or later followed,
each dated by its own record, with "Made public:" (R27) where the record was private (a Politburo resolution, a
plenum, Mao with Hồ at Changsha). Read them in their own languages where the source is in one; the entry is in the
calendar's English.

*Before the radar.* Such a body, and its leaders, are followed from their beginnings, not from the day Washington took
notice: the founding, the meetings, the plans, the recruiting, the contacts, the money and the arms, the splits and the
purges; the officers before the coup, the leaders of an insurrection before the rising, the rebels before the
rebellion, the party before its rule. The test is what the body became, then or later (a coup, a rising, a war, a
government, a matter of foreign affairs at any date, after the span too), where the work being read dates the act and
says what came of it: what came of it is the work's, never the reader's memory (R4). The first notice Washington took
(the first cable, report or estimate naming the body, its leaders or its plot) is an entry of its own, tagged
`notice:us`; a counterpart's first notice, where its record shows it, `notice:<the counterpart>` (`notice:uk`,
`notice:ussr`).

**R11. The organized interests.** The unions and their officers (Meany, Reuther, Hoffa, Carey, Dubinsky, Randolph,
Chavez); the business, farm and professional bodies (the Chamber of Commerce, the NAM, the Business Council, the Farm
Bureau, the AMA, the ABA, the NEA); the lobbies and associations; the churches' bodies; the civil-rights and peace
organizations; the foundations and study groups (the Ford Foundation's Gray Areas grants, Brookings, RAND, the CED);
and their leaders. Entries: their dated public word on policy or politics (a statement, testimony, a resolution, an
endorsement, a convention, a campaign); their dealings with officials and members; their acts that start, fund or
shape a program, a bill or a campaign the calendar follows (a grant, a study taken up, a strike, a contribution); the
investigations and prosecutions of them. Threads: Labor for the unions' politics; Labor disputes for strikes; the
event's own otherwise.

**R12. The parties to an event.** A defendant, a witness or a plaintiff in an event the calendar records, on that
event (Hoffa's interviews on his trials).

**R13. The columnists.** A named columnist is one with an entry in Part III's press sections (K–J Cong. III.J, Opp.
III.J, 1968 III.O, Cong. III.M: Lippmann, Reston, Krock, the Alsops, Pearson, Anderson, Evans and Novak, McGrory,
Buckley, Rovere, Wicker, Sidey, Broder). A named columnist's column on policy or politics is an entry where the work
being read dates and treats it; no run of the papers is read. Another journalist's column is an entry only where it
carried an act (a leak, a trial balloon) or made news. A columnist the work treats as a voice who has no Part III
entry: propose the columnist in your leads (Kraft, Childs, David Lawrence, Drummond, Kilpatrick, Fleeson, Kempton),
for the owner to rule.

**R14. Military matters.** A military operation, action or incident the work dates and treats, in every theater
(Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia, Thailand, the Korean DMZ, the Dominican Republic, Cuba, Berlin's convoys, the Congo airlift,
the *Liberty*, the *Pueblo*, Palomares, Thule, SAC's alerts): a named operation, a battle, an engagement; a deployment,
a unit's arrival or withdrawal, a base opened; a logistical decision or act (a port, an airlift, the munitions, the
construction program); an order, a directive, the rules of engagement; an incident at sea or in the air; an alert, a
nuclear accident. Its sorties, losses and strength go in `n` with their pages, whether or not the war table carries
the series. Threads: Vietnam and its subthreads, Air war, Laos, Defense, the event's own.

**R15. Trips and stretches.** A trip, tour, campaign swing or visit, its places named. An event the source dates only
to a month, a season or a stretch of days: dated so (`date: '1961-03'`, or the first day with "Check: the day"), never
"undated"; only what no source dates even to the month is undated, and it is a lead.

**R16. The operations of an event.** The days, stops, actions and incidents of an event the calendar records (the
Bay of Pigs' landing and its days, the Freedom Rides' stops, a campaign's rallies).

**R17. One event, one entry; an entry is an event-day.** Each act its own entry, dated by its own record: an
announcement and the act it announces, hearings and the vote, a note and its reply; the first keeps the event's
bibliography, the rest point back. Spans of two or three days are good, under a week tolerable, anything longer
suspect. A meeting, document or address whose matter falls in several threads is one entry, filed under its chief
thread (`thread:`), listed in the others (`also:`). Before adding an entry, compare it with every entry on its day in
every thread (`./bib find -l cal --since D --until D`) and with the pending patches' adds (`grep -l "date: 'D'"
inbox/*.yaml`, then read them): where one records the event, edit it; where a pending patch adds it, R49. File each
new entry under the thread its matter belongs to, even where that thread has an entry that day; never change an id or
a thread to get around the merge's duplicate flag.

**R18. What is not an entry.** Only:
- *Out of the brief*: outside the span (before Jan. 6, 1960, or after Jan. 20, 1969, the primary seasons aside); or
  matter no thread follows nor should (another country's domestic politics with no bearing, then or later, on its
  dealings with Washington or its counterparts, a change of its head of government excepted; a domestic campaign in China that does not touch the war).
- *Minor*: a repetition of a position already entered, with nothing new; private or social trivia (family and social
  matters with no bearing on public business or politics); a unit's routine the work does not treat as an event (a
  patrol without contact, a supply run).
- *The passed-over papers*: a covering note or transmittal, a draft not sent, a meeting's preparation, a repetition,
  private remarks.
"Below the level" means only a unit's routine, as above. No class of event is passed over in a blanket (R48).

## C. Writing the entry

**R19. The prose.** Compact, plain, compressed. Record what happened, not what it meant. No praise, blame, irony or
color: no "for Moscow's benefit", "a good midterm", "the movement reads it as a defeat". Short declaratives and
fragments; drop articles and verbs where the line still reads. Match the section you add to: read a few of its
entries first. CLAUDE.md, "The brief", gives a before and after.

**R20. The fields.** `id` from `./bib new-id cal <date> <thread>`; `when`, `date`, `thread`, `also`; no `s:`.
- `c`: what happened, in one to three clipped sentences: who, what, the number. A memoir's account is said so in `c`
  where it matters ("Johnson writes that ...").
- `n`: the pointers (Part D).
- `tags`, hidden: `name:<key>` for every person the entry names, or whose act it records, who has a name entry; the
  President where the entry is his message, address, press conference, signature, order, proclamation, NSAM, meeting,
  or a poll of his approval. Not a namesake, not a name in a case's title. `state:` and `city:` for the States and the
  cities (CLAUDE.md, "The brief", and `notes/threads.md`); `place:` and `province:` for Indochina (CLAUDE.md, "Maps of
  Indochina").
- The forms: CLAUDE.md, "House style": the places ("Places in the calendar's entries"), the Vietnamese names
  ("Vietnamese names and places"), the election results ("Election results in `c`"), a law in `c`; "Markup inside
  text" for the links, cards and YAML quoting.

**R21. Names.** `n` ends with "Names:" and every person in `c` who has a name entry, by surname, commas between
("Names: Heller, Tobin."). Where the tagged persons share a surname, the given name ("Martin Luther King", "Frank
Johnson"); a son or father with his suffix ("Harry F. Byrd Jr."); a name in its own order as it stands ("Ngô Đình
Diệm", "Satō", "Haile Selassie"). A justice is named only where the entry quotes his opinion or is about him, not for
writing or joining one. Nothing after the names but a sentence of its own ("Check: …"); no list or section.

**R22. Corrections.** Change `c` only where a record settles it beyond doubt, and say so in the note ("The Papers date
it Aug. 26."; "the book's version said plainly"); propose the fix in the patch, saying what the entry said. Else a
Check. Never rewrite an entry beyond what the source needs.

**R23. Check.** "Check:" marks a detail to verify ("Check: the day"; "Check: Sorensen gives …"), a sentence of its
own in `n`.

**R24. Accounts.** A memoir is its author's account ("by Rusk's account", "Ball writes"), never plain fact against
another record. Where the record (FRUS, the Public Papers, a tape, the Daily Diary) gives the same event, the record
first. Where accounts differ, say so: "Check: Sorensen gives …". A journalist's narrative is a secondary source: date
its events by the record where you can find one.

**R25. The raw material.** Code nothing; keep what the later passes will code:
- a position or an argument in the source's own words, briefly, with its page, in `n` ("Ball: 'a long protracted war'
  ([[w.x]], 412)"), not only the clipped `c`;
- the time and the sequence the source gives: `time:` (UTC, a full ISO instant), `time_approx: true` ("that evening",
  "about 7"), `follows: [id]` ("afterward", "then"), from Nov. 2, 2026; until then, the words in `n` ("at about 7
  p.m., p. 412");
- every figure the source gives, with its page: votes, whip and head counts, dollars, troops, polls, tons, sorties,
  losses, strength.

## D. Citing

**R26. The order of `n`.** The primary record, read and linked; the works read, each with its pinpoint and, where it
stands for part, its parenthetical; "Made public:"; "To check:"; "See [[id]]"; "Names:".

**R27. Made public.** An event done in private and documented in records nobody saw at the time (a recording, a
memorandum, an FBI file, a Politburo resolution) is dated by the record, and the note says where and when it became
public: "Made public: Drew Pearson's column, May 24, 1968." ("Made public: [[w.department-of-state-bulletin]], Nov. 19,
1973, 640–645.")

**R28. The primary record.** Cite it first, linked, in the series' forms:
- the statute, "[77 Stat. 56](…)"; the session laws; a treaty by U.S.T. and U.N.T.S.;
- the Public Papers, "Title, [1961 Pub. Papers 1](…) (Jan. 20, 1961)"; APP, linked, only where the Papers do not
  print the document (orders, proclamations, platforms, the campaign of 1960), never read;
- the Federal Register; the Congressional Record by chamber and day; a hearing (govinfo; the Daily Digest's rows,
  `daybook/committees/`, for its day and page);
- FRUS, with its volume's title and the document ("FRUS 1961–63, XXIV: Laos Crisis, doc. 4"; the text in
  `sources/frus-text/`);
- the case: the U.S. Reports; the lower courts on case.law's open PDFs;
- the Attorney General's and OLC's opinions (`daybook/opinions.yaml`); the *Bulletin* (`daybook/bulletin/`,
  `sources/dsb-pages.json`);
- a recording: the Miller Center's free page (`daybook/recordings/millercenter.yaml`), PRDE's only where it has none
  ("(PRDE only)"); never PRDE's transcripts;
- Johnson's Daily Diary (`daybook/diary.yaml`, 50 days), "[President's Daily Diary, Aug. 4, 1964](…), images 3, 5
  (LBJ Library; National Archives Catalog)";
- the presidential library's file; a State's canvass or session laws (`sources/states.yaml`, `sources/state-law.yaml`).
Where the record is found and the event's first entry lacks it, add it there too. Many events have no record of their
own (a march, a strike, a riot, a speech the Papers do not print): leave them, and say so in your notes. A record found
and not readable joins "To check:" with where it is. Where no record is reachable, the work is the bibliography and a
detail it alone gives carries "Check".

**R29. The bibliography.** An event is not a thread: the Bay of Pigs and Mongoose are two events in Cuba. The event's
first entry carries its full bibliography; later entries of the event point back, `See [[id]]`; an entry that opens a
new event carries its own. A work goes to the entries whose claims it bears out, with its pinpoint; where it treats
the event as a whole, to the event's first entry, at the end of the run (the build orders the run). A work is its card,
`[[w.id]]`, with its pinpoint after it (`[[w.gibbons-u-s-government-and]], II, 84–85`); no list or section. A work with
no card: make the card first (R53).

**R30. A pinpoint names a number.** A page, a volume and page, a document, a chapter or note by number, or a link to
the place read, in Bluebook's form ("123–25"; "45, 211"). The pinpoint is the page that bears out the claims, not the
page about the event: read it sentence by sentence against the entry's units. A pointer in words ("on Rusk", "for the
arithmetic", a chapter's title) is not one: the work stands behind "To check:", the words its parenthetical. `check`
fails on a new one.

**R31. Hard and soft.** The sources read come first; every work not read stands behind "To check:", a sentence of its
own before "See" and "Names:" ("To check: [[w.a]]; [[w.b]]."), the event's bibliography among them. Reading a work, move
it out of "To check:" with its pinpoint (and its parenthetical); an emptied "To check:" goes. A source found and not
read joins it.

**R32. What each source stands for.** A source cited under an entry stands for the whole of `c`, its dates, numbers,
names, quotations and facts, unless the note says otherwise. Where it does not, the entry and the note say which claim
each source is for: a parenthetical after the cite ("[[w.x]], 123 (the vote)"), a label before it ("Votes: ..."), or
the cite in the sentence of `c` it supports. Omission implies, determinately: under ABCD, "X; Y; Z (C)" says X and Y
stand for A, B and D, and Z for C and C's tie to the rest, not for A, B and D. A source read that does not bear out a
claim is no cite for it: the claim takes "Check:" or goes.

**R33. Holding up the cites already made.** For each entry citing your source:
- the page bears it out: pin it, out of "To check:";
- it bears out part: pin it with the parenthetical (and the letters, R34);
- the page is off: the right page;
- the source gives another date, figure, name or account: settle it against a third record where one exists (R22),
  else "Check: <Surname> gives …";
- the source does not treat the matter: the card may belong to another entry (move it), or was cited for the thread,
  not the event; drop it only where the source plainly does not bear on the entry, and the claim it stood for takes a
  Check.
Every case not a plain "holds" is a row in your book-check file: entry id, what the entry says, what the source says
(page), any third record, your ruling.

**R34. The claim map.** Each entry's `c` is lettered into units, split at its sentences' ends and its semicolons, never
inside a quotation, parentheses or brackets (`./bib claims show ID`). For each entry you cite or edit, write its map by
patch: set `src` (start from `./bib claims seed ID`; its `c:` the hash it prints, made again if your patch changes
`c`), a row a source at a page with the letters it grounds: `'w.card 123: A, C-D'`, `'<the link's address>: all'`
(a link covers all only where the record bears out the whole entry), and the rows of Part E. Every row names an item
the note or `c` prints; `check` holds the two together. Keep the rows other runs wrote; re-letter only where `c`
changes. Read the footnotes `./bib claims show ID --inline` makes of your map, in a scratch copy with your patch merged.

## E. What the sources rest on

Two forms. **R35 is in force now.** R36–R43 replace it once the registry of witnesses is built (queued for Nov. 2,
2026; `notes/extensions/memos/witnesses.md`); until then they are not in force.

**R35. The records the notes cite (now).** For each page you cite for an entry, each record the work's note gives for
that passage (a document, recording, interview, file) is described once in `sources/cited-records/<card without
w.>.yaml` (schema and check: `tools/bib/cited_records.py`, [cited-records]): `record`, `kind`, `date`, `where`,
`cited_by` (page and note), `found` (a copy the series holds), `read`. It is tied to the units it grounds by a `via`
row of the entry's map (`via w.card 70 n.36: B, E`); several records under one note for one passage, each its row, the
same letters. A record the series holds (the daybook's FRUS, the Public Papers, the recordings, the Diary): read it;
if it bears out its units, cite it first in `n` as the primary record, with its own row, `read: true`. An unread one
joins "To check:" with the work it comes from ("To check: Bundy to Johnson, Feb. 7, 1965, NSF, LBJ Library (cited by
[[w.dallek-flawed-giant]], 251 n.14)."). Only what the note gives: no record inferred from the text. A work with no
notes: say so in the book-check file. Studies, memoirs and documentary collections the note cites are logged too (the
rule of R37), as records of kind `other` with their pages, until the registry takes them.

**R36. Witnesses (from the registry).** A witness is anything that bears on a claim: a document, a recording, a
transcript; a print, edition or translation of any of them; a statement, a press account, a hearing; a study, a
memoir, an oral history, an interview; a person's testimony. Works are witnesses (their cards); a documentary
collection (FRUS, JMZW, *Current Documents*) is the print of each document it prints. The registry:
`sources/witnesses/` (its schema and check, when built: the memo, §4).

**R37. Edges and roots.** Each witness is tied to what it derives from, and how: copy, print, edition, transcription,
translation, quotation, citation, recollection, report. A root has nothing identifiable and recoverable upstream, for
the claim; a claim often has several. An open upstream is a source named and not recovered: name it exactly and say
why the branch ends (closed archive, not found, not held); it is a lead. Independence is counted by distinct roots and
distinct agents, never by works. The tree is drawn for each unit of `c`, not for the entry; each unit's rows reach the
witnesses by `via`.

**R38. Register everything.** Every witness you meet that bears on a claim, however well attested the claim is. A
copy of a witness already registered (the same text, nothing added) is entered as its edge to that witness, one line:
never counted as support, never dropped; the witness upstream is the one credited. A source the text names (a letter
quoted, an interview held) is a witness as the text gives it; an event the text tells without naming a source makes
none.

**R39. Follow each branch.** As far as the series can: a card you hold, the daybook's FRUS, the Public Papers, a
recording, the Diary, an open scan. Where you cannot, name the upstream and mark the branch open. A witness the series
holds and you read, bearing out its units, is cited first in `n` as the primary record (R28); an unread one joins "To
check:" with the witness it comes from (R35's form).

**R40. Describe each witness.** As the witness gives it, nothing graded: whose account it is (`agent`); when it was
made (`made`: contemporaneous, or later and when); its form (`form`); for each claim it bears on, whether its
information is firsthand (`knows`).

**R41. Variants.** Where witnesses differ, each reading against its unit, in the witness's words, with its page.
"Check: … gives …" stays in the note for the page's reader.

**R42. The record that is the act.** Where the series cites the record that constitutes the act (the statute or
session law, the order or proclamation, the regulation, the reported decision, the certified canvass, the release of a
figure as first reported, the journal of a confirmation), the units it constitutes close on it and need no tree: the
act, its text, its date, its number, the figure. Its circumstances keep their trees. A compilation, or a print of a
text that is not the act (the Clerk's *Statistics*, CQ, the *Directory*, FRUS, the Record's text of a speech), closes
nothing. Witnesses that agree with the record are not sought; one that disagrees is a variant. Where the record itself
is in question (a contested election, a document dated other than the act, a disputed validity), the unit is
`disputed` and takes a tree.

**R43. Already in.** A witness to an entry already in the calendar goes into that entry's map; it is never a reason to
pass the witness over.

## F. What you pass over, and leads

**R44. Passed over.** Your book-check file has a section "## Passed over": every dated event within the span that the
source treats and you did not enter, a row each: page | date | event, a few words | why (one of R18's: out of the
brief, minor, a passed-over paper; or a lead). Say in the file how the list was made. An event already in the calendar
is not passed over: it takes its cite (R29) and its witnesses (R43). The owner rules on the list.

**R45–R47.** (Kept free.)

**R48. No blanket exclusions.** Never a row or sentence passing over a class of event ("minor throughout", "not listed
event by event"); `check` warns on one [passed-over].

**R49. Pending patches.** An event another pending patch adds goes under "For entries in pending patches" in your
book-check file (`entry id | card, page (parenthetical) | units`), not into your patch; within your own group of
sources, a later source's patch builds on an earlier's the same way. The merger adds them.

**R50. Leads.** What you recall and could not place or verify (an event without a date, a figure without a source
read, a source not reached, a host refused, a columnist to propose): at the end of your book-check file under
"Leads", by the headings of `notes/leads.md` (States and cities, National, Foreign, the Executive roster), with the
entry it concerns and where to look. The merger copies them into `notes/leads.md`. Never into the calendar unchecked.

## G. The mechanics

**R51. By patch.** All calendar changes (and `th.yaml`'s) in one patch a source, `inbox/<date>-<name>.yaml` (CLAUDE.md,
"Changing things"): every `edit` and `delete` with a `base` read by `./bib show` just before you write it; keep every
cite, sentence and map row other runs wrote. `./bib merge <patch> --dry-run` clean. Never edit `lists/cal/*.yaml`,
never merge, never run git.

**R52. Finish clean.** `./bib check`: 0 errors from your work (report others' errors without fixing them); `./bib
audit-cal` for calendar entries.

**R53. The files.** Never read `build/`, nor a whole list: `./bib find`, `./bib show`, `grep -n`, and only the lines
you need. Edit only your own book-check and records files; a shared file (a works letter, `notes/leads.md`) by small
exact edits, re-read just before. A new card: CLAUDE.md, "The works catalogue" (author, title, year, the edition where
the copy is not the first; `./bib works adopt`, or the lock script the batch brief names), its note by "Bibliography
notes" (CLAUDE.md, "The brief": no reviews, no awards, no side labels).

**R54. The copies.** Into your own new directory under the scratchpad, never the repo; check it is what it says
(`file`); read its text layer (`pdftotext -layout`), or OCR a scan (`ocrmypdf --sidecar`, or `pdftoppm` and
`tesseract`) and say so; delete the PDF at once (and the page images), one at a time. Find the offset between PDF
pages and printed folios on three pages, again where it may change (the front matter, the plates, a second volume),
and check a few pinpoints against the page image (`pdftoppm -f N -l N -r 100 -png`); cite the printed page. Never
change a Drive file's sharing.

**R55. Copyright.** The books are the owner's copies, for this research: nothing of their text into the repo beyond a
short quotation an entry needs, marked and cited by page; never a paraphrase at length.

**R56. The network.** Through the proxy; never disable TLS; never get around a bot check (a refused host is a lead);
never read presidency.ucsb.edu, discoverlbj.org or PRDE's transcripts. Not readable here: the newspapers
(TimesMachine is a link by date, not a text), history.defense.gov, jcs.mil, HathiTrust's pages. Python with `-I` on
downloaded data. Never write a key anywhere.

**R57. Pronouns.** No gendered pronoun for an author in a note: "the note", the surname.

**R58. The report.** Under 400 words, a source at a time: the copy and its offset; cites checked, by ruling (held,
pinned in part, moved, cut, disputed), and the disagreements a line each; new entries (the count, the chief ones);
records or witnesses (found and read, found and not read, open); Passed over, by reason; leads; what you checked and
how; the patch written.
