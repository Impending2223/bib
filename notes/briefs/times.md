# Times of day and the day's order (Nov. 5, 2026)

The owner, Nov. 5, 2026: "Getting timestamps and 'follows' tags is a higher priority than reading the new books for now." The rules: `tools/bib/dayorder.py`, its STYLE 1-4 (CLAUDE.md, "The day's order"): `time:` a UTC instant, the local time turned by the place's IANA zone, '1961-10-17T00:30Z'; `time_approx: true` where the source gives the time loosely ("that evening", "about 7"); `follows: [<id>]` where the entry follows others of the same day, its basis in the note; never a made-up hour, never a time to force an order.

## Reviewers (a span of years each)

1. Read `CLAUDE.md`, `tools/bib/dayorder.py` (STYLE), `tools/times/propose.py` (its docstring: the tiers, the sources, the zones). Never read `build/` or a whole list file.
2. Your proposals: `notes/times-proposals.yaml` (regenerate first: `python3 -I tools/times/propose.py`), the entries of your years. For each entry: read it (`./bib show ID`) and the source the proposal names (the recording's catalogue time in daybook/recordings/; the FRUS document's own heading or stamp, in the daybook or `sources/frus-text/`; the Public Papers item's NOTE in `sources/ppp-text/`; the entry's own words). Take a time only where the source's time is the time of the entry's event: a cable's sending is not the meeting it reports; a memorandum's date-line is not a meeting's hour unless it says so. Settle the zone (Washington's, the Ranch's, Saigon's +8 in the 1960s; zoneinfo's history for daylight time). Several proposals: the best-grounded. "about", "shortly after", "evening": `time_approx: true`.
3. Write `time` (and `time_approx`) by `edit` with a fresh base, and the time's source in the note where the note does not already cite it (the recording's page, the FRUS doc, the Papers item: they are usually there already). Where the record gives a time the entry's `c` contradicts, a Check.
4. `follows`: on each day of your years with two or more entries, where the record shows one event came after another the same day (a meeting and the statement it decided; a call and the order it led to; a vote and the signing), and their times do not already say so, `follows: [<id>]` on the later, the basis in the note in a few words ("After the meeting: FRUS ..., doc. 42"). Not for events merely on the same day; not by guess. Find the days: `./bib ledger find DATE --until DATE` or a short script over `lists/cal/<YYYYMM>.yaml` listing days with several dated entries; read only those entries.
5. One patch a year, `inbox/2026-11-05-times-<YYYY>.yaml`, `edit` ops with fresh bases (time, time_approx, follows, and the note's added source). `./bib patch-check` until no errors; `./bib check`'s [day-order] rules are in it. Do not merge or commit.
6. Last message: per year, the times written (exact, approximate), the proposals refused and why (a sample), the follows written, and anything doubtful.

## Round 3: ranges (Nov. 6, 2026)

The owner, Nov. 5, 2026: "Yes, add the ranges and sort by time first." The rules: `tools/bib/dayorder.py` STYLE 5-6 (`time_range`, `time_band`, `time_zone`, `range_basis`, BANDS, KINDS_PLACE, band_range). A day's entries now sort by their place in the day; an entry with neither `time` nor `time_range` sorts after the placed ones. A reviewer a span of years places every entry of its years dated to a day that has none:

1. The proposals first: `notes/times-ranges.yaml` (`tools/times/propose_ranges.py`, its docstring). Take a row where its basis bears it out (read the entry, the Digest page or the source's words): `time_range`, with `time_band`/`time_zone` where the row has them, `range_basis: record` (or leave it out: record is the default), and the basis in the note in a few words where the note does not already cite it ("The Senate sat from noon to 5:34 p.m.: [Daily Digest, D435](…)."). A row with `check` (the Digest's standing order): take it unless the day's Digest shows another hour. A row that has an exact time too (`has_time`): take neither if the entry is already timed.
2. Then the event's kind, for every entry still unplaced: where the entry's own words or its source place it in a part of the day without an hour, a range from the record (as 1). Where nothing does, its kind (KINDS_PLACE): an office act (a meeting, a call, a signing, a swearing-in, a briefing, a news conference with no hour) or a paper written in an office (a memorandum, letter, cable, report, order, proclamation), the workday daytime of its place; a hearing, the same; a chamber's floor act with no Digest hours, the workday afternoon; a breakfast, a lunch, a dinner (a reception, a toast), their bands. `range_basis: kind`, the band and the zone, nothing in the note. The zone is the place of the act (a cable from Saigon: Saigon; a Washington meeting on Vietnam: Washington); Texas on standard time before 1967; a place of unsettled 1960s daylight time, the range widened by the hour (both readings).
3. Leave unplaced, rather than guess: an event whose kind gives no part of the day (a battle, a riot, a demonstration, a strike, a vote count, an election, a poll's release, a publication, a newspaper story, a death, a natural event) unless its record does; a span of several days; an entry whose place is unknown.
4. Patches: `inbox/2026-11-06-ranges-<YYYY>[-N].yaml`, edit ops with fresh bases (`set: {time_range: [...], time_band: ..., time_zone: ..., range_basis: ...}`, and `n` only where a record's basis is added), some 400 ops a file. Script the mechanical part (list the unplaced entries of a year with their `c`; classify by kind words; then read every classification you write, not a sample). `./bib patch-check` until clean. Do not merge or commit. Keep a script that rebuilds the patches on fresh bases.
5. Last message: per year, ranges from proposals, from records found in reading, by kind (by kind of kind), entries left unplaced and why (counts and a sample), anything doubtful.
