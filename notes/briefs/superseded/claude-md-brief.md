# CLAUDE.md's reading rules, as they stood on Oct. 30, 2026

Moved here when the reader's brief (`notes/briefs/reader.md`) came into force. The scope paragraphs of "The brief", then the close of "What the works rest on".

The informal record (the owner's rule, Oct. 23, 2026: "the sinews of the public history"): the calendar is not the
public record narrowly construed. For the persons with public roles (executive, legislative, policy, foreign
relations, the administration of justice, the campaigns and the parties' communications), the transactions behind it
belong too: informal meetings, lobbying, agreements, the planning of policy, personnel, operations and communications,
in the West Wing, the members' offices, the campaign headquarters. Their record is mostly memoirs, biographies, oral
histories, recordings and diaries: date each by its source, cite it by page, name whose account it is where it is one
participant's ("by O'Donnell's account"), and where accounts differ, say so ("Check: Sorensen gives ..."). The reading
runs look for them.

The parties and the members (the owner's rule, Oct. 29, 2026, on the readings' "minor": "What rule makes this kind of
position-taking, messaging, organization, communication, policy formation, campaigning, etc., in the opposition and
among non-majority-leadership MCs 'minor'? Can we amend it?"; "I'm quite interested in what the Republicans were up to,
and what they were saying, in these years"): within the brief, and never "minor", the position-taking, messaging,
organization, communication, policy formation and campaigning of either party in or out of the White House (the owner, Oct. 30, 2026: "**in or** out") and of any member
of Congress, leader or not: a party's statements, papers, declarations and platforms; its committees' and leaderships'
meetings and decisions; the founding and doings of its factions and allied groups (the Ripon Society, the Free Society
Association, Young Americans for Freedom, the American Conservative Union, the Democratic Study Group); and the dated
public word (a speech, statement, press conference, interview, article or column under his own name) of a member, a
governor, a party officer or a party figure out of office (Eisenhower, Nixon, Goldwater, Rockefeller, Romney, Scranton,
Percy, Reagan) on policy or politics. A journalist's column is an entry only where it carried an act (a leak, a trial
balloon) or made news itself. "Minor", as a reason in a "Passed over" list, means only: a repetition of a position
already entered, with nothing new; private or social trivia; a unit's routine the work does not treat as an event (a patrol
without contact, a supply run). The papers of government (the owner's ruling, Oct. 28, 2026, on the Gibbons readings): a staff or planning
paper, a White House staff memorandum, an ambassador's, commander's or consulate's cable and an intelligence memorandum
or estimate are entries, dated by the paper; a covering note or transmittal, a draft not sent, a meeting's
preparation, a repetition and private remarks are passed over. What is passed over at all (the owner's ruling, Oct. 28, 2026, on the readings of *Six Crises*, Humphrey, McPherson, Jones and Navasky: too much cut as "private", "below the level", "undated", "itinerary"; the reading of *Suite 3505* the model): an event the source dates to a month, a season or a stretch of days is an entry, dated so (`date: '1961-03'`, or the first day, with "Check: the day"), never "undated"; only what no source dates even to the month is undated, and it is a lead. A trip, a tour, a campaign swing, a visit is an entry, its places named ("I want to know where these guys went!"). A private meeting, call, conversation or letter of a person with a public role, on public business or politics, is the informal record and an entry; only family and social matters with no bearing on either pass as private. The operations, actions and incidents of an event the calendar records (the Bay of Pigs' landing and its days, the Freedom Rides' stops, a campaign's rallies) are entries. Military matters (the owner's ruling, Oct. 30, 2026, on the readings of the services' and the Joint Chiefs' histories, which passed over "operational detail" as "minor throughout"): a military operation, action or incident the work being read dates and treats is an entry, the work's notice the test, as for the columnists, the calendar recording or not: a named operation, a battle, an engagement; a deployment, a unit's arrival or withdrawal, a base opened; a logistical decision or act (a port, an airlift, the munitions, the construction program); an order, a directive, the rules of engagement; an incident at sea or in the air; an alert, a nuclear accident; in every theater (Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia, Thailand, the Korean DMZ, the Dominican Republic, Cuba, Berlin's convoys, the Congo airlift, the *Liberty*, the *Pueblo*, Palomares, Thule, SAC's alerts); the sorties, losses and strength the work gives are kept in the entry with their pages, whether or not the war table carries the series (thread Vietnam and its subthreads, Air war, Laos, Defense, the event's own). "Below the level" means only a unit's routine the work does not treat as an event. Nothing is passed over in a blanket: every dated event a reading does not enter is its own row in "Passed over", never "passed over as minor throughout" or "not listed event by event". "Out of the brief" and the others met (the owner's ruling, Oct. 29, 2026, on the readings of Asselin, Chen, Zhai and Nguyen, which passed over Hanoi's and Peking's decisions as "no trace in Washington found", and of Sundquist and Schlesinger's *Robert Kennedy*, which passed over the lobbies, the foundations and Hoffa): every thread is its matter as Washington and its counterparts met it, and "or should have left a trace" takes in what Washington did not see. Within the brief, then: every authority, movement or armed group Washington or its counterparts dealt with, or later would, as a matter of foreign affairs, ally or adversary, recognized or not (the owner's ruling, Oct. 30, 2026: "a catchall ', and other sovereigns' ... armed groups and unrecognized authorities"): the sovereigns (Hanoi, Peking, Moscow, Saigon, Bonn, London, Havana, and every other), the authorities without recognition (Biafra, Katanga, Rhodesia, a government in exile), and the parties, movements, factions, officers' circles, insurgents, guerrillas, militias and bands, in power or out of it, in arms or not (the NLF, the Ba'ath, Fatah and the PLO, the Muslim Brotherhood, the Kurds, the PCI, the PCF, the JCP, the PKI), their decisions, meetings, papers, orders, envoys and public word on a matter a thread follows, or later followed, dated by their record, with "Made public:" where the record was private (a Politburo resolution, a plenum, Mao with Hồ at Changsha); followed from their beginnings, before the radar (the same day: "the coup leaders before the coup, insurrectionists before the insurrection, rebels before the rebellion"): the founding, meetings, plans, recruiting, contacts, money and arms, splits and purges, where the work being read dates the act and says what came of it (a coup, a rising, a war, a government, a matter of foreign affairs at any date, after the span too), the first notice Washington took an entry of its own, tagged `notice:us` (a counterpart's, `notice:<the counterpart>`; notes/briefs/reader.md, R10); the organized interests and their leaders, the unions and their officers (Meany, Reuther, Hoffa, Carey, Dubinsky, Randolph, Chavez), the business, farm and professional bodies (the Chamber of Commerce, the NAM, the Business Council, the Farm Bureau, the AMA, the ABA, the NEA), the lobbies and associations, the churches' bodies, the civil-rights and peace organizations, the foundations and study groups (the Ford Foundation's Gray Areas grants, Brookings, RAND, the CED), with their dated public word on policy or politics (a statement, testimony, a resolution, an endorsement, a convention, a campaign), their dealings with officials and members (the informal record), their acts that start, fund or shape a program, a bill or a campaign the calendar follows (a grant, a study taken up, a strike, a contribution), and the investigations and prosecutions of them (thread Labor for the unions' politics; Labor disputes for strikes; the event's own otherwise); a party to an event the calendar records, on that event (a defendant in a prosecution it follows: Hoffa's interviews on his trials; a witness; a plaintiff); and the named columnists, those with an entry in Part III's press sections (K–J Cong. III.J, Opp. III.J, 1968 III.O, Cong. III.M: Lippmann, Reston, Krock, the Alsops, Pearson, Anderson, Evans and Novak, McGrory, Buckley, Rovere, Wicker, Sidey, Broder), whose columns on policy or politics are entries where the work being read dates and treats them (the work's notice is the filter: no run of the papers is read); another journalist's column stays an entry only where it carried an act or made news, and a reading that finds a columnist the work treats as a voice proposes him for Part III in its leads (Kraft, Childs, David Lawrence, Drummond, Kilpatrick, Fleeson, Kempton), for the owner to rule. "Out of the brief" means only: outside the calendar's span (before Jan. 6, 1960, or after Jan. 20, 1969, the primary seasons aside), or matter with no bearing on politics or policy anywhere, here or abroad (a sports result, a celebrity's marriage, a finding no government took up). Another country's domestic politics is within the brief (the owner's ruling, Oct. 30, 2026: "Why are we excluding the domestic politics of other countries? I'm not sure that rule makes sense"; "I want the volume!"), filed under its country's thread, a reader proposing one where none is. The threads: Republican Party (`republican-party`; its subthread "In Congress", `republican-party-congress`),
Conservative movement (`conservative-movement`), Democratic Party (`democratic-party`); `notes/threads.md`.

And what a reading passes over: every dated event the work treats and the run did not enter is listed in its
`notes/book-checks/` file under "Passed over", page, date, event and why (out of the brief, undated, minor, already in
under another id, a lead), for the owner to rule on. To be replaced (the owner, Oct. 30, 2026: "a trace/map of *all* the *ultimate*, or *root*, materials that bear on a fact or event"): the records become witnesses, works and collections among them, each tied to what it derives from, down to the roots, every one registered however well attested the claim, a copy kept as an edge; `notes/extensions/memos/witnesses.md`, queued. "Minor" as the parties-and-members rule above defines it. The raw material (the owner, Oct. 30, 2026: later passes
will code positions, warnings and beliefs, "so long as we're not losing the raw material for those passes to the
cutting-room floor"; the readers code nothing): a position or an argument kept in the source's own words, briefly, with
its page, in `n`; the time and the sequence the source gives (`time:`, `time_approx:`, `follows:`, to be built Nov. 2, 2026,
until then in words in `n`); every figure the source gives, with its page.
